# Why the six films failed their screening

The commissioning viewer watched the six AutoMovie 0.3.0 films on 19 November 2026 and rejected all six. The four Sword directors and the kickboxing director had signed their respective pictures. The basketball director withheld the fourth cut. The commission returns to screenplay for every film.

## What counted as a pass

The productions checked file duration and decoding, inspected picture samples in chronological order, and compared selected rendered frames against their encoded counterparts. Reviewers identified narrative events such as a shared loaf, a hand on a sleeve or a basket. The directors treated those findings as sufficient to accept five films whose rhythm, acting and physical interactions remained unresolved. The commissioning agent reported those approvals as completed films. Its own spot checks also looked for the planned event and matched the file against the director's hash.

The existing [delivery review](../../.agents/skills/review/delivery-review.md#video) asks for a separate verdict on the depicted practice and a viewer report for what only playback shows. The [direct-film criteria](../../.agents/skills/video/direct-film.md#what-to-look-for) expressly include intersecting surfaces. The reports below retain the difference between what the reviewers saw, what a test measured and what the recipient saw at playback speed.

## Where the time went

The sampling rate determined which frames a reviewer received. It did not set the movie's playback clock. For example, the basketball film has 4,320 pictures at 24 fps. The review sheets take frames 0, 5, 10, 14 and 19 of each second, and label each cell with its actual frame number divided by 24. Frame 432 is 18.000 seconds. The seven-second replay occupies seven seconds of broadcast time. There is no half-speed multiplier. A reader can scan an entire four-second sheet in a glance or spend minutes on it. Even a correctly labelled sheet does not impose the pace the audience experiences.

Across the six movies all 15,480 video packet presentation times start at zero for their respective film and have the expected constant interval, 1/24 or 1/30 second. Every final chronology used elapsed-time labels. Bread Cloths, The Better Seat and Two Hands printed frame numbers as well. Bread Cloths and The Last Carry included the last frame in addition to the 300 regular samples. Adjacent samples are four or five output frames apart at 24 fps and six frames apart at 30 fps. All 30 decoded spot checks (five cells per film) match their printed times within the displayed precision. A contact or a cut shorter than that interval can fall between cells. ## What the research covered

The [basketball research index](../../movies/shorts/slam-dunk/docs/research/index.md) includes 1994 Japanese game rules, the clock, court markings, a passing and footwork reference, Denver's baseline cut and Ray Allen preparing his feet at the corner. Those sources informed the sequences and chosen positions. The production record does not show a timed reference possession carried through to foot plants, acceleration, defensive recovery or off-ball spacing in the rendered cast. The live game selected run, guard or stand from a route-speed threshold and used fixed cycle rates. The SDK gestures implemented for the bench clarified a different part of the film. The original evidence demonstrates a research-to-performance gap. It cannot establish a worker's private reasoning. [Game clock and routes](../../movies/shorts/slam-dunk/src/composition/scenes/two-hands/010-game/game.ts) · [Rendered players](../../movies/shorts/slam-dunk/src/entities/cast/basketball/players.ts)

The [Sword study](../../movies/shorts/sword/docs/research/history/road-and-households.md) documents a bread delivery, the cost of walking in armour, historical objects, mounted figures and the layout of a funeral procession. It expressly uses the illustrated manuscript as a visual comparison. It gives a historical basis for the court, props and circumstances. The inspected study and reviews do not establish the performed durations, coordinated motion of the cast or clearance of the rigged props at their filmed locations. The screenplay and production allocated seconds by story beat, then triggered many poses, prop positions, reactions and sound cues separately. Source-to-encode agreement faithfully reproduced these choices.

The basketball team also recorded a concrete SDK limit. Its probe admitted a stationary dribble and refused certain combined travelling or airborne ball actions because both plans claimed the same body channels. The production implemented live court travel and feet locally. The installed bench gestures did reach the rendered players. These findings locate one composition problem. They do not establish a universal defect in the SDK's running motion or explain why nine other players often had so little to do. The rendered-rig check sampled ten players on all 4,320 frames. Blue #8's body centre stayed fixed during the final screen, but the left sole, about one millimetre above court level, moved a net 39.2 cm in one second (frames 4185–4209). The shoe of the dribbler moved 22.1 cm in a third of a second in its low phase. Screen-space measurements alone would have been misleading: the broadcast camera shifted one stationary player's court position by 120 screen pixels during an earlier screen. The discrepancy is visible in the film and measurable in court coordinates.

The kickboxing study had competition rules, an activity/pause study abstract, a broadcast still and a camera interview. It explicitly distinguished those last two sources from observing actual fight footage. The production specified an athlete's pose and timed the other performer's response and foley separately. At 5.95 seconds the replayed scene still has over 100 screen pixels of clear space between the relevant displayed forms while the other athlete reacts. A genuine final-stage camera overlap elsewhere does not cancel this earlier clear gap. The focused review requests explain why a fresh reader did not correct the directors' drift. The Better Seat's final brief asked about the witnessed decision, offered hand and closing laugh. The Last Carry's director was assigned staging and props only. Answer Me's detail images were taken at 51.375, 53.792, 57.958 and 59.958 seconds. The Round's director acknowledged reviewing the last targeted repair and inferring earlier interactions from a readable result. Basketball's unprompted reader had to provide observations by four-second group; its dense reader received preselected events. These procedures could produce valid local findings. None could turn a missing whole-performance judgment into a pass.

## Proposed scaffold changes

Keep the director and independent reviewers. Use the existing skill owners and records. These are proposed changes to behavior; the installed and shipped instructions have not been edited.

| Owner | Existing instruction and observed gap | Proposed decision rule |

| [Research](../../.agents/skills/story/planning/research.md#performance-study) and its existing [screenplay consumer](../../.agents/skills/story/layers/screenplay.md) | The skill already asks for timing, action density, distribution, primary sources and a frame comparison. A source about a rule or an object could nevertheless satisfy a beat whose central claim concerned motion. | For the practice that sells the scene, identify a reference passage, its actual duration, who acts or waits and what changes because of the other participants. Carry that observation into the relevant beat and the motion or staging case that must exhibit it. Keep period or costume research credited to the question it actually answers. |
| [Motion judgment](../../.agents/skills/motion/judgment.md#before-widening) | A representative exchange is already required. A working guard pose, walk in isolation, prop crossing or hand marker could pass while the staged interaction failed. | Choose the exchange that could disprove the commission. Inspect the actual cast, prop and costume surfaces, partners and affected bystanders in the delivery view from approach through response. Use the actual clock, a second view or source geometry to resolve ambiguous overlap, float or skating. Identify when an installed action is refused, overridden or applied to a different rendered body. Test each materially different interaction where the first case gives no coverage. |
| [Segment review](../../.agents/skills/motion/segment-review.md) | The instructions already prescribe ordered samples, adjacent frames, use-scale views and joins. The selected details clustered around the last repaired shot. | For a retake, verify the changed shot and retain an independent reading of the whole practice. Sample representative intervals of live play or travel, include secondary actors, and trace each central reaction to a visible preceding event. Select consecutive frames at actual interactions even when a cue or test says they already happened. |
| [Delivery review](../../.agents/skills/review/delivery-review.md#acceptance-record) and [director judgment](../../.agents/skills/production/director.md#judgment-and-revision) | The record already separates technical completion from a verdict on the depicted practice, and requests observations that require playback. Five directors nonetheless signed sampled pictures as finished films. | Bind the verdict to the cut, viewing mode, witness and scope. An independent story read establishes what was understood. For a commission whose success depends on pace, weight, ensemble rhythm or sound, a judgment on those aspects remains open until the actual master has a normal-speed viewing by a witness who can observe them. The director must carry a material dissent or missing observation into the release decision. The coordinator reports that same scope. |

Keep the capture scale explicit. Chronology sheets already call for 5–10 samples per second, with the higher rate for fast actions and consecutive frames around events. Full-size stills show spatial detail and a complete time interval can expose a planted foot or a missing response. A denser contact sheet still has no enforced playback speed. Deliberate pauses, legal stationary screens, safe performance gaps and actual slow-motion replays can belong in a good film; the judgment turns on their intended and observed effect. [Capture times and sheet labels](../../.agents/skills/visual-debugging/capture.md#times)

Use the existing acceptance record as the authority for promoting a revision. The Round's promotion script matched a director approval phrase, the hash, the decoded count and the captions. Its inputs allowed promotion despite recorded uncertainty about the depicted practice. A handoff or automation should carry the scoped verdict for that revision and any unresolved material observation. Preserve historical receipts after a withdrawal. A folder named `accepted` or a restored MP4 cannot grant current acceptance. This belongs in [delivery review](../../.agents/skills/review/delivery-review.md#acceptance-record) with a short cross-reference from [media records](../../.agents/skills/production/media-records.md#pipeline-files).

The director can ask one open question in the independent brief: “Which part of the requested experience is least convincing, where, and what in the delivered film makes you say so?” Give the reviewer the full picture and the information needed to interpret the clock. Obtain the unprompted response before supplying the writer's intended result, the previous verdict or a list of repaired shots. Then compare that account against the commission, the practice evidence and any contrary findings. A focused retake verdict remains scoped to the retake.

## How to test a future wording change

Give independent reviewers the same unlabelled-as-to-outcome cuts, with accurate source and film times, using the same model, reasoning effort and media capability. Run the current wording and the proposed wording separately. Before providing prior verdicts, check whether each reviewer identifies a specific interval and correctly states which quality claims remain open. Test the mounted stretch, the early live basketball possession and the early remote reaction as adverse cases. Include a genuine intentional hold, a correctly labelled replay and a legal stationary screen as controls. A rule that simply rejects slow scenes or requires perpetual player motion fails the control. This comparison is proposed and has not been run.
