# Direct 3D film observations

The commissioning viewer rejected all six films made with AutoMovie v0.3.0 at `958d78ffa`. The [review report](0.3.0-film-review-return.md) records the playback findings and the return to screenplay. The following observations describe the historical production and director reviews.

## Review method

Reviewers first saw chronological samples from the encoded film without a screenplay or production notes. At five samples per second, a one-minute film yields 300 pictures and a three-minute film yields 900. Full-size source and decoded images at the same times and consecutive frames around cuts and contacts helped locate what the first reader had missed. Speech, rhythm and lip sync still need uninterrupted playback and listening. [Capture](../../.agents/skills/visual-debugging/capture.md#times) · [Delivery review](../../.agents/skills/review/delivery-review.md#video)

The commission authorized the director to reopen the complete screenplay when an assembled film exposed a story problem. Writers revised the text where timing or causality failed. When the text already specified the missing action, the director could order a retake. Decisions and outcomes for each film follow. ## What changed in the films

**Answer Me.** Two picture reviewers read the sleeve restraint as a handshake or truce, even after the arms were separated. The director reopened the screenplay. A short command and a continuous shot now show Luc touching Rémy's upper arm, releasing him and opening the gate. The next reviewer followed the release and reunion, though the silent touch could still read as an appeal or goodbye. The accepted cut also held Rémy's planted right ankle steady over frames 1408–1410 as his left foot rose. [Acceptance](../../movies/shorts/sword/docs/acceptance/answer-me.md)

**Bread Cloths.** The first reader recognized the shared bread and missed Alix's rescue. The screenplay asked the viewer to follow the slip on the upper path while another group moved below. The writer gave Alix 38–44.5 seconds uninterrupted. Later staging took the loose strap out of her silhouette and kept a cloth tail in each woman's hand. The final reader described Perrin dropping the sack to save Alix and counted one whole loaf and half a loaf in each cloth. The precise grip remained hard to see. The last two repairs preserved 1,100 decoded frames pixel for pixel. [Acceptance](../../movies/shorts/sword/docs/acceptance/bread-cloths.md)

**The Better Seat.** The first reader saw Martin go to one knee and assumed he had lost. The screenplay gives the decision to the boot crossing the boundary. Showing the boot alone still left the witnesses' reactions disconnected. The director kept the text and cut from the print to the witnesses, then isolated the offered hand before the saddle changed hands. The final reader saw a possible ring-out, the deliberate handover and the fingers moving through the tear. The exact wager and joke still depend on dialogue. [Acceptance](../../movies/shorts/sword/docs/acceptance/the-better-seat.md)

**The Last Carry.** From 22 to 30 seconds the bearers' faces, sleeves and rail obscured the change of support. Another face covered the returning shoe at 53.4–53.8 seconds. Lowering the camera for the transfer separated the bearers' profiles from the timber. The final reader followed the widow taking the load, the two men remaining outside the burial ground and the removal of the splinter. The exact support and pinch remained partly hidden. The director kept the wide shot for its view of the widow carrying on. [Acceptance](../../movies/shorts/sword/docs/acceptance/the-last-carry.md)

**The Round.** The first complete reading followed the change in control and the winner. Repeated guards flattened the middle. The count and the two spectators' different final reactions were hard to follow. All 36 screenplay beats went back to the writer. A full-size frame from the fourth cut then showed the downed fighter apparently floating. The bottom of his pelvis mesh was about three centimetres above the mat, his head nine centimetres above it and part of the supporting forearm sixteen centimetres below it. Matching source and encoded frames preserved the same staging error. The director returned the affected shots and retained the revised screenplay. [Acceptance](../../movies/shorts/kickboxing/docs/acceptance/the-round.md)

**두 번째 손.** A blind reviewer followed the tie, the go-ahead three and the final rebound. At frame 1237 the bank shot placed the ball center behind the backboard. Sampling the four shots and repeated dunk at 480 Hz also found about eight centimetres of rim penetration on the dunk and seven on the last shot. In the corrected source the bank touches the board at 51.53 seconds, the clean three clears the ring by seven millimetres and the last ball touches the rim at 178 seconds.

Two subsequent basketball readings followed the bench gestures but missed why the scorer redirected the captain's congratulations to the teammate who set the screen. A reviewer using the Korean captions recognized the screen and still missed the connection. The writer found that the gesture and its explanation occurred about ten seconds apart. The director reopened the script to replace one analyst cue with two short lines from the players themselves. The score, replay and programme clock stayed fixed.

## What the SDK contributed

The first four Savoy films, boxing cut and basketball broadcast used some combination of native geometry, body measurements and gait tables. All six initially posed their human performers through local code. A separate source review traced each decisive action from its registered clock to the displayed cast. The subsequent productions put installed actions on the visible figures. Film-specific constraints remained, including four changing shoulders under one bier and a running player's live ball. The horse used native bind and gait data with local leg articulation. [Motion composition](../../.agents/skills/motion/compose/composition.md#actions)

The eighth cut of *The Last Carry* used a local Three.js renderer with shadows and sent JPEGs to the encoder. Its SSIM of 0.993 measured agreement with those JPEGs. The subsequent accepted cut used AutoMovie's standard beauty draw and lossless source transport. The difference matters when interpreting benchmark claims. One question for the next campaign is whether the handoff alone lets a director trace a visible action to its source and renderer. ## Export and recovery

- **Color range.** The boxing exporter produced full-range pixels, while the H.264 header signaled limited range. On frame 61, correcting the signal in the same picture stream reduced mean red, green and blue errors from 10.25, 9.29 and 8.18 to 1.37, 0.63 and 1.62 on the 0–255 scale. Both files had passed frame count and duration.
- **Interrupted render.** The first basketball renderer exited after frame 2107 without a completion report. All 2,108 saved PNGs passed dimension, decode and checksum checks. Drawing five of those frames again reproduced their original RGB values. Rendering resumed at frame 2108 and yielded a complete 4,320-frame cut. The cause of the process exit remains unknown.
- **Accepted frames.** The second basketball cut's sheets were briefly copied to the accepted directory before the director's verdict arrived. The director returned the cut. The producer removed the duplicate after verifying all 200 original files by hash and confirming that no manifest or acceptance page pointed to it. The film's working review remained intact.
- **Project-wide Evidence.** Sword passed individual picture reviews, then its project-wide Evidence run reported 20 errors. A decimal fraction immediately after a ternary question mark confused the pinned parser in three files. Several diagnostics followed from that one problem. Tests also lacked answers, and the populated entity and composition folders needed their checks enabled. After the owners corrected the syntax, source layout and answers, the standard run covered all 51 units across nine obligations with zero errors or warnings. The four approved film hashes stayed the same.
- **Audio review.** Two Sol producer sessions returned HTTP 400 on an audio attachment. Replacement producers used images, captions, speech recognition where available and measurements of the decoded audio. These verified cue timing and levels. The delivery records identify which listening judgments remain open.
