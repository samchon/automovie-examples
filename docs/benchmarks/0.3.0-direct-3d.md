# Direct 3D film observations

Six films use the local AutoMovie v0.3.0 checkout at `958d78ffa`. Pilot observations below identify frames to revisit. Each film's acceptance record owns its final verdict.

## What the current skills cover

The [capture guide](../../.agents/skills/visual-debugging/capture.md#times) calls for five to ten stills per second in chronological order, with the higher rate for fast action and consecutive frames around cuts and contacts. [Delivery review](../../.agents/skills/review/delivery-review.md#video) asks the reviewer to record an audience response to the delivered picture before reading the screenplay or code. It covers every scene and join. Still images establish what is visible at the sampled times. Listening and playback have their own evidence.

The [director](../../.agents/skills/production/director.md#judgment-and-revision) may return a technically passing film. When an authored decision fails on the assembled timeline, [assembly](../../.agents/skills/production/assembly.md#film-assembly) sends it back to the [screenplay's author](../../.agents/skills/story/correction/repair.md) and calls for a rebuild. For these six films the client authorized reopening the whole screenplay when the picture exposes a dramatic failure. The resulting film is the subject of the next reading.

## First complete viewing

For *The Last Carry*, the Sol producer inspected 300 frames from a 60-second encoded cut at five frames per second, then compared 32 full-size source samples. The procession and the shoe's return were legible. From 22 to 30 seconds the bearers' silhouettes obscured the transfer of weight to the widow. At 53.4–53.8 seconds another woman's face briefly covered the shoe. At 56.8–57.6 seconds the reaching hand appeared to pass behind the splinter. The producer returned the cut for staging and camera repairs. An independent reading of the repaired film will test whether the screenplay itself needs to change.

For *The Round*, an independent Sol reviewer recorded a first response after viewing 900 chronological samples from the 180-second encoded file. The shift in ring control and the winner were clear. Repeated close guards flattened the middle, the count had to be inferred, and the reviewer missed the two spectators' different responses at the end. The file decoded all 5,400 frames and the final claps were present in the mix. The commissioning producer returned all 36 screenplay beats to a Sol writer for another pass alongside the staging repairs.

For *Answer Me*, two independent readings of consecutive 60-second cuts gave the sleeve restraint the opposite meaning. The first reviewer saw an agreement or handoff. After the director separated the arms, directed Rémy's weight away from Luc and cleared the foal from the gate swing, a fresh reviewer still saw a handshake or truce. The second cut decoded all 1,440 frames. The director returned the whole screenplay to a Sol writer to reconsider what the viewer knows before the grip and what releasing it changes.

For *Bread Cloths*, the first reader recognized the two tables and the divided loaf but missed Alix's rescue. The writer traced the competing focus to the screenplay. It asked viewers to follow a slip on the upper path while another group moved below. The director adopted a revision that holds on Alix from 38 to 44.5 seconds until her heels find firm ground. The next encoded cut will show whether the rescue reads on its own.

## What the SDK contributed

The director accepted the eighth cut of *The Last Carry* after the procession and the widow's ending read on screen. A separate source inspection found native Human hand surfaces on a locally constructed body. The production supplied its own gait and arm solver. Its renderer enabled shadows and sent JPEGs to the encoder. The reported SSIM of 0.993 compared those JPEGs with the decoded film. Comparison against AutoMovie's standard draw remained open. [R8 acceptance](../../movies/shorts/sword/docs/acceptance/the-last-carry.md#technical-verification)

The initial cuts of *The Better Seat* and *Bread Cloths* went through AutoMovie's draw path. Their figures used meshes from the installed Human package, while a shared production helper posed the limbs independently. *Answer Me* used the same helper for riders and used the installed horse's bind pose and gait data in a local leg solver. The boxing production used Human body measurements and locally interpolated poses. The installed actions and operators intended for these performances are described in the [motion composition guide](../../.agents/skills/motion/compose/composition.md#actions).

The first basketball render used the installed Human mesh, basketball model and a running gait table. Local code posed the players and built the hoop. The SDK offers a hoop with rim and board contact points, as well as pass, catch and leap actions. A later cut can test their contribution against the original game clock, ball path and broadcast angles.
