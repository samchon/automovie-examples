## Causes in the performance

Each floor track prescribes positions and times ahead of the play. `floorAt` eases into and out of each segment over 13% of its duration. Intermediate marks return instantaneous floor velocity to zero even when the next mark lies on the same continuing run. The value called `speed` is the whole segment’s displacement divided by its duration. It selects the same run animation even as the root brakes to a stop. A direction change switches the route vector at the mark. The pose has no corresponding planted turn or transfer of support. As a result, the airborne and planted parts of a recognizable step have no consistent relation to starting, slowing, turning, or covering court distance. [Floor routes and interpolation](../../../src/composition/scenes/two-hands/010-game/game.ts#L81)

The default action chooses `run` above 0.55 m/s segment average and otherwise `guard` or `stand`, with no `walk` selection. The fixed phase rates are 1.64 cycles/s for running, 1.4 for dribbling and 0.48 for guard, screen and box-out. A single wave sets each local foot’s fore-aft position and partial lift. The root then carries that target through space while the leg solver aims at it. There is no court-space support target or contact transfer. A catch, pass, shot or gesture overrides the legs while the floor track keeps moving. Defending while moving above the threshold uses the ordinary forward run. `guard` at lower speeds faces the fixed opponent or the ball, dependent on possession, and has only 13 mm of nominal foot lift for a 1.82 m actor. Each opponent responds to its own authored poses and times. Matchup facing and occasional ball-directed arms cannot resolve a brush, denial step, screen collision, forced reroute, or closeout on their own.
