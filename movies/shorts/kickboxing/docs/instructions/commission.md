## One round in 180 seconds {#one-round}

User requirement. "180 seconds", from the bout's opening exchanges through the knockout and its aftermath, ending at 180. The film is one scheduled three-minute round that the knockout ends early; nothing outside that round is shown.

## Two equal championship fighters {#fighters}

User requirement. "Two highly trained adult men, both 180 cm and 80 kg, fighting at championship level." The owner's A delivers the finish and B receives it; the brief gives them names, because a bare "A" reads as an article in screenplay action. Their equality of size and level is part of the premise: the bout is decided by a learned habit, not by a mismatch.

## Glove touch, then exchanges {#glove-touch}

User requirement. "They step into an actual glove touch and immediately exchange." The touch is a real contact of gloves that both men step into, and the first exchange follows it without a reset.

## The exchange vocabulary {#exchange-vocabulary}

User requirement. The fighters "exchange combinations, feints, checks with counters, slips, parries, rolls and blocks, with varied footwork." Every one of these appears, performed by both men, and a check is answered by a counter.

## The ruleset {#ruleset}

User requirement. "Under GLORY rules: a clinch only for one immediate knee and then release, no elbows, throws or sweeps." Also: "No downed attack." The contest is fictional and claims no accredited event; the rules study holds the rule text it follows.

## Contacts before the finish {#contacts-before-finish}

User requirement. "Body and glancing contacts interrupt breath and guard without early knockdowns." Landed blows before 150 seconds cost breath, guard position and balance, and nobody goes down before the finish.

## B's learned tell {#tell}

User requirement. "B has learned to drop his left arm whenever A's right knee rises; A exploits that tell with a feint and then with the finish." This production reads "has learned" as learned during this round, on screen: B acquires the habit from A's right body kicks, A proves it with a feint that lands, and only then throws the finish.

## The finish at 150 seconds {#finish}

User requirement. "At 150.000 A's right Brazilian kick lands: his instep and lower shin come down over B's lowered left arm onto the area behind B's left ear at the junction of jaw and neck." The second is an anchor the edit lands on. The kick starts as the body kick B has learned to expect.

User clarification, given on 2026-11-13. The landing point "behind B's left ear at the junction of jaw and neck" is the side of the head and neck below and just behind the ear lobe, struck by a kick aimed at the side of the head, which kickboxing rules treat as a legal target. It is not the back of the head.

## No injury mechanism {#no-injury-mechanism}

## The active referee {#referee}

User requirement. "The visible referee is an adult man." "The referee is always active: positioning, side view, clinch breaks, signals and the knockdown procedure." "The referee is a visible participant with readable signals, gaze, expression and support." Under the adopted representation a polygon man has no face, so his expression is read from the turn of his head and trunk, his posture and his voice, and his support is the physical care he gives B after the result.

## Fifteen-second phases of one scene {#phases}

User requirement. "Each H2 scene is the next fifteen-second phase of one continuous ring scene, headed `INT. KICKBOXING RING - NIGHT` and then `CONTINUOUS`, with no time jump; each H3 is an exchange beat with its beat record, short present-tense action and separate speaker and dialogue blocks." The screenplay therefore has twelve scenes, and each scene's fifteen seconds are a true anchor of the edit. After the finish there is no exchange between the fighters, so this production reads the last two phases' exchange beats as the bout's closing exchanges between the men and the officials: the fall, the count, the result, the celebration and the bell. A heading is an address, not a cut, so the finish landing at 150.000 opens the eleventh phase while the uncut kick and fall run across that boundary.

## Pressure changes across the round {#progression}

User requirement. "Change pressure, angle and initiative across the round; do not loop the first minute." Each phase up to the finish hands the next a changed situation: who leads, where the fight stands in the ring and what each man has learned. After the finish the change is the procedure's, from the count to the result.

## The camera relates the fighters {#camera}

User requirement. "The camera reacts to the action and relates the fighters." Also: "Keep feet, targets and heads readable together on the same screen axis." A framing that drops the feet, the struck target or either head during an exchange fails this requirement.

## The sound of the film {#sound}

User requirement. "Start and match-ending bells, English referee words, breaths, mat friction and distinct contacts carry the film." Also: "No replay, time compression or commentary." Every sound the screenplay names is one the scene could produce; no commentator, music score or replay voice is heard.

## The winner beside the fallen man {#ending}

User requirement. "The winner's raised gloves, supported victory steps and excited breath remain visible beside the downed opponent in the wide ending." The last image holds A's celebration and B on the canvas in one wide frame, with the referee attending B. This production reads "supported victory steps" as steps that carry A's weight on his own planted feet, each one landing and bearing him, not as steps in which anyone holds him up: no corner man enters the ring before 180, and the ruling describes the winner's own movement.

## Direction {#direction}

Delegated choice, decided by this production on 2026-11-13 within the rulings above, under [direction](../../../../../.agents/skills/story/planning/direction.md). The situation, the finish and the ending are fixed, so the directions compared differ in whom the audience follows and what it knows when.

- Following B from his side of the ring, so that the audience shares his ignorance of his own tell and the finish arrives as a surprise. Rejected: the feint the commission requires would then read as an ordinary miss, and a knockout from a habit the audience never saw forming would read as luck, which contradicts the premise that a learned habit, not a mismatch, decides the bout.
- Following the referee as the audience's eye, so that the round is read through the official who manages it. Rejected: it pulls attention from the two men the camera must relate, and the referee's care at the end would outweigh the fight it closes.
- Following both men from a ringside position, with the audience let in on A's plan before B is. Chosen.

The audience watches both men and learns A's plan before B does: it sees the right body kick land, sees B's left arm begin to drop at each rising right knee, and sees A test that drop with a feint, so the Brazilian kick arrives as a payoff it has watched being built rather than as a surprise. B is no victim: he leads much of the round with long combinations and low kicks, and A's plan is a slow answer to his pressure, which costs A breath and ground before it pays. Attention is sustained by the alternation of initiative and by the growing pattern, and after the finish by the count's real-time procedure. The finish, the count and the celebration close the bout completely, and nothing remains open. Within the user's [tone](#tone) the image stays with readable, whole bodies and the sound with the ring's own noises. This serves the commission because the owner's finish depends on a learned tell, which only reads as craft when the audience has seen it learned.
