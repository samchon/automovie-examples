## Sporting reference

The commission depicts the opening round of a fictional match under GLORY rules. The 180 seconds are programme time and include the aftermath of a stoppage before the scheduled three-minute round ends. Under the current GLORY rules a standard match has three three-minute rounds, with five possible, and a title match generally has five. WAKO's ring disciplines use three two-minute rounds and one-minute intervals, so that timing belongs to a different competition format. The short's spatial steps match GLORY's general down procedure. The referee sends the standing athlete to the farthest neutral corner, starts the count after sending him there and can count through ten to a knockout, followed by the timekeeper's end bell. The ordered cues are present in the film. Their fine gestures are harder to see in the wide than the separation is. The 37-second break depicts recognizably distinct separation and re-entry, but the athletes' near-static guard hold and Daniel's initial overlap against Blue weaken the visual evidence that stepping between them and withdrawing released the action. ## Common causes

**Independent events on a shared clock.** `performanceAt.ts` samples each root route and adds timed joint accents and response curves. `sound.mjs` schedules foley by authored times. The native overlays cover guard, selected officials' and witnesses' gestures and two limited post-knockdown walks. They leave the bout exchanges and fall on the authored pose system. Simultaneous clocks alone leave the interacting surfaces, the visible cause and the response independent. That produces the large remote reactions at 5.95 and 11.72, the smaller world gaps coincident with projection overlap at 21.95 and 92.47, and the weak handoff from the close arrival to the fall at 150. **Gait driven by root distance and speed.** Smooth travel between marks drives a sinusoidal step amplitude in the actor's current facing. The standing rig adjusts vertical pelvis height relative to the lowest foot. This leaves horizontal foot support free to drift while a body reacts, turns, brakes or translates. The numerical drift and the repeated upright resting pose explain why the athletes look less grounded than their scale, costumes and hand animation suggest. Pace changes exist at route level. The changes in support, recovery and speed that would give those routes athletic meaning are seldom equally clear. **Camera position tied to plot geography without enough control of silhouettes.** The fixed apron route shows every corner and makes the overall chase, count and ending understandable. The same continuous camera path repeatedly aligns the fighters toward the lens or leaves them behind ropes, particularly during the exchanges at 65.5, 82.33, 92.50 and 97.87. Whole bodies in frame can still hide the side of an interaction that makes it readable. The broadside early misses and the better angle at the finish show that framing and physical separation are distinct failure modes.
