# The bell

The timekeeper's bell, the only signal in the film that is not a person's voice or body.

## Form and use {#form-and-use}

### A struck metal bell at the officials' table {#bell}

The bell is a metal ring bell fixed at the officials' table beside the ring and struck with a hammer by the timekeeper, who sounds the start and end of rounds and the end of the match ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#round); [sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#bell)). It is heard far more than it is seen; when the table is in frame, the timekeeper's arm swinging to it is the only visible cause ([ringside](../crowds/ringside.md#timekeeper)).

## When it sounds {#signals}

### The opening bell {#opening-bell}

At the film's first second the bell sounds once, a single clean strike, to start the round ([sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#palette)).

### The match-ending bell {#ending-bell}

At 170.1 the bell sounds again as a rapid run of strikes, unmistakably different from the opening's single one, to end the match after the knockout ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#count); [bout rules](../world/bout.md#knockout)). It cuts through the crowd's noise without stopping it ([crowd](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#reactions)).

### No warning and no other bell {#no-warning}

No ten-second clapper, gavel or warning bell sounds, because GLORY names none inside a round and the match is over before the round's last ten seconds ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#round)). The bell does not sound at the knockdown or during the count. The single strike at 0 and the run at 170.1 are the bell's only sounds in the film.
