# The ringside

Everyone outside the ropes: the hall's audience, the officials and the two corners. Their duties follow the [referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#ringside).

## The audience {#audience}

### A dark, full hall {#dark-hall}

The hall is full and blacked out for the main event, as [broadcasts](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#broadcast) do, so its audience is never seen: beyond the ring's light there is only darkness. The audience is heard as one indistinct crowd voice with no word that can be made out. It murmurs through footwork, rises with shouts at heavy landed blows, roars at the knockdown and holds that roar through the count beneath the referee's voice, and surges again at the knockout signal and the bell.

## Officials and corners {#officials}

### The timekeeper and the bell {#timekeeper}

The timekeeper sits with the bell at the officials' table, outside the ropes beside the [table corner](../sets/ring.md#zones), in the dark. He strikes the bell once to start the round as the film opens, and on the match-ending bell's [anchored second](../world/work.md#anchors) strikes it in a rapid run to end the match. He is heard, not seen.

### The ring doctor {#doctor}

The ring doctor, an adult man in a dark blue short-sleeved shirt, grey trousers and blue medical gloves, sits in the dark below the steps of the [doctor's corner](../sets/ring.md#zones) for the whole round and enters the ring only when the referee calls him. Called after the knockout signal, he climbs through the ropes at once and reaches Vermeer within seconds, well before the film ends; the screenplay decides the beat. He kneels at Vermeer's head and checks him without moving him, and from then on Vermeer's care is his, with the referee still at Vermeer's side. He works in silence, as only the referee speaks, and nothing he does names a cause.

### Judges and the announcer {#judges}

Three judges sit at ringside and the ring announcer at the table; none is seen, and the announcer does not speak within the film, because the result is announced after it ends.

### The corners {#corners}

Each fighter's corner team, three men, sits in its seats below its corner for the whole round, in the dark beyond the light, and is never seen. They do not enter the ring, pound the apron or approach their man during the count, and their coaching is lost in the crowd's voice, never heard as words. After the knockout, Kessler's corner stays outside the ropes while Vermeer is down: the contest lets nobody come into the ring to a fallen fighter but the referee and the doctor, and the winner, who is already in the ring, keeps away from him.
