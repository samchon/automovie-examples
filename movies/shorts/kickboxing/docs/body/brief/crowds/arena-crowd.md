# The arena crowd

The paying crowd around the ring: never a subject of the story, always its sound and its dark surround.

## Makeup and arrangement {#makeup-facet}

### Who and where {#makeup}

A full house of a few thousand adult fight fans, seated from the first ringside rows a few steps from the apron up into tiers that disappear into the dark hall ([ring](../sets/ring.md#arena); [sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#crowd)). It is a mixed crowd with no home fighter, so its noise follows the action, not a man. In the picture it is the simplest form that reads as rows of people beyond the lit ring: the nearest rows catch some spill of the ring light and show shapes that sit, lean and stand; behind them the crowd is a dark mass ([work record](../world/work.md#what-it-cannot-show)). No one in it is singled out.

## What it does {#behaviour}

### Reactions {#reactions}

The crowd watches the whole round and reacts to it, by the [sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#crowd):

- through the round it is a constant bed of noise that swells with each landed or nearly landed strike and settles between exchanges;
- it rises just after the finish lands, not before it, and roars through the fall, the near rows coming to their feet;
- through the count it is held lower, in suspense, so that every number of the referee's carries over it;
- it surges again at the knockout signal at 167.6 and through Vos's celebration;
- the match-ending bell at 170.1 cuts through the noise, and the roar carries on to 180.

### Voice {#voice}

The crowd makes no intelligible words: no shouted names, no chant with words, no single voice that can be made out ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#language)). Its sound is roar, groan, rising noise and clapping. No music or announcer is heard over it ([work record](../world/work.md#left-out)).
