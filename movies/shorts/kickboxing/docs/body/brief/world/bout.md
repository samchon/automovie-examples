# The bout's rules

The GLORY ruleset as this bout keeps it. The rule text and its articles are in the [rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md); this record settles which of those rules the film uses and how they bind the people in it. The contest is fictional and claims no accredited event ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#ruleset)).

## The match {#the-match-facet}

### A non-title match of three rounds {#the-match}

The bout is a scheduled three-round GLORY middleweight match between two ranked contenders, not a title fight, because a GLORY title match runs five rounds ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#round)). Both men weigh 80 kg, inside the middleweight class ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#ring-and-equipment)). The film is its first round; the knockout ends the match inside it, so the second and third rounds never happen and nobody mentions them.

### The round's clock {#round-clock}

The first round is scheduled for three minutes and starts with the timekeeper's bell at the film's opening second. Its clock runs without a stop until the knockout: no foul, injury time-out or doctor's examination halts it before 150. The round never reaches its last ten seconds before the match ends, so no ten-second warning is heard ([bell](../props/bell.md#no-warning)).

## The opening {#opening}

### The glove touch {#glove-touch}

The referee's instructions and the corner rituals happen before the film begins. At the bell the referee calls "Fight!", and the two men come out of their corners to the middle of the ring and step into a touch of gloves, a courtesy GLORY's rules neither require nor forbid ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#glove-touch)). The touch is a real contact of gloves, made in good faith: neither man strikes during it. The first exchange begins from the distance the touch leaves, with no step back to reset, as the [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#glove-touch) rules. Which man leads that exchange is the screenplay's choice.

## Legal and illegal actions {#actions}

### The legal arsenal {#legal-arsenal}

Each man may punch with the padded glove (straights, hooks, uppercuts), kick with the foot or lower leg to the legs, body and head (round kicks low, middle and high, front and push kicks to the body, the Brazilian kick as a high kick), and knee to the body or leg, the knee only within the clinch rule below ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#legal-targets)). Feints, checks, blocks, parries, slips and rolls are the defences the [exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#defences) records at this level. The spinning back-fist, spinning and jumping kicks are legal but neither man throws them: the film's craft is the plain arsenal thrown well.

### What never happens {#fouls-kept-out}

No man throws an elbow, a head butt, a throw, a sweep or a push meant to off-balance; no man strikes the back of the head, the back or the groin, kicks the legs with a push kick, holds a caught leg, holds the ropes, clinches to stall or rest, or strikes after "Break" or "Stop"; and no man strikes an opponent who has any part but his feet on the canvas ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#fouls)). Apart from a grip slow to open after its knee ([one knee, then release](#one-knee-clinch)), no foul occurs, so no caution, warning or point deduction is ever given, and the round's clock is never stopped for one. Neither fighter speaks, which is also the rule's conduct ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#fouls)).

### The finish lands on a legal target {#finish-target}

The finish's contact lands on the side of Roux's head and neck, below and just behind his left ear lobe, at the junction of jaw and neck, from a kick aimed at the side of the head. GLORY makes only the back of the head a foul, so this is a legal high kick with its legal surfaces, the instep and lower shin ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#finish); [rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#legal-targets); [Brazilian kick study](../../../research/kickboxing/brazilian-kick.md#legality)). The contact stays on the side of the jaw and neck and never on the rear of the neck, which the stricter regulations count as the back of the head. In the picture it reads as the shin arriving across the side of the head from Roux's left, never from behind him.

## The clinch {#clinch-facet}

### One knee, then release {#one-knee-clinch}

A man may take hold of the other only to throw one immediate knee; after it he must let go at once, and holding beyond that is a foul ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#clinch)). A clean clinch is a grip, a single knee and a release, made by the fighters themselves without a command. When a grip is slow to open after its knee, the referee steps in with "Break!", both men step back, and they fight on without "Fight!" ([referee](../cast/referee.md#clinch)). That slowness is the round's only foul. GLORY gives the referee alone the power to impose a consequence for a foul ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#fouls)), and this brief decides that he answers it with the break alone and no caution, because the grip opens a moment late rather than to stall. At least one clinch in the round is broken this way, because the commission names clinch breaks among the referee's duties ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#referee)); which clinch, and whose grip, is the screenplay's choice.

## The knockdown and the count {#knockdown}

### What counts as down {#down}

A man is down when, from a legal strike, any part of him other than his feet touches the canvas ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown)). Before 150 nobody is down, nobody is held up by the ropes after a damaging blow, and the referee gives no standing count, because no blow before the finish takes either man past the cost of breath, guard and balance ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish)). The finish is the bout's only knockdown.

### The count {#count}

After the knockdown the referee calls "Down!", sends Vos to the neutral corner farthest from Roux, and starts the count only once Vos stands in that corner facing the centre of the ring. The count therefore starts at 157, with Vos in the corner and turned by then; the referee speaks "One" at 158 and each next number one second later, through "Ten" at 167 ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#count); [rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown)). The count runs without interruption: Vos does not leave the corner, no corner man approaches, and the referee does not pause at "Eight" to examine Roux, because a pause there would make the result a technical knockout instead of the knockout the commission names. Roux does not rise before "Ten".

### The knockout and the bell {#knockout}

"Ten" with Roux still down ends the match by knockout. The referee signals it at 167.6 by waving both arms crossed above his head, and the timekeeper's bell ends the match at 170.1 ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#count); [referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#knockdown-procedure)). From the signal on, the match is over: Vos may leave the corner, and his celebration from 168.1 breaks no rule ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#winner)).

## The result {#result-facet}

### No announcement before 180 {#result}

The referee's ten and his signal decide the result; the formal announcement by the ring announcer, and any raising of the winner's hand, come after the film ends ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#medical-and-result)). No corner team enters the ring before 180. The ring doctor enters at the referee's call to attend Roux, the only person besides the three already in the ring who does so ([ring doctor](../cast/ring-doctor.md)).
