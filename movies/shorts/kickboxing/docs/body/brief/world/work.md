# The work

The fixed conditions of this short: what it promises, what it shows and leaves out, what its people can show, and the form every scene shares. The [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md) holds the owner's rulings these decisions adopt.

## Promise {#promise}

### Audience and experience {#audience-and-experience}

The film is for adults who may know nothing of kickboxing's rules. They watch two equal men from ringside and are let in on the winner's plan before the loser is: they see the right body kick land, see the other man's left arm begin to drop each time the kicker's right knee rises, and see the kicker test that drop with a feint that lands. The finish then arrives as a payoff they watched being built, not as a surprise. The loser is no victim: he leads much of the round, and the winner's plan is a slow answer to his pressure. Attention holds through the alternation of initiative and the growing pattern, and the knockout, the count and the celebration close the bout completely, leaving nothing open.

### Tone {#tone}

Sober sports realism. The round is shown as the men fight it, with no slow motion, glamour lighting or heroic framing for either man, and the knockout is neither celebrated by the film nor dwelt on as horror: it is the result the audience saw coming, and the film watches the winner, the fallen man and the referee with the same attention.

### The film {#the-film}

One film, `kickboxing`, 180 seconds long in a landscape 16:9 frame, as the [delivery profile](../../../../PROJECT.md#delivery-profile) records. Its line: two equal middleweights fight one round, and the habit one of them learns from the other's body kicks becomes the opening for the knockout at 150 seconds.

## Scope {#scope}

### What the film shows {#what-the-film-shows}

One scheduled three-minute round, the bout's first, from the start bell to 180 seconds; the knockout ends the bout early, and the film stays in the ring through the count and the celebration. Nothing outside that round is shown: no walkout, introductions, interval, announcement of the result, replay or later scene.

### What the film leaves out {#what-the-film-leaves-out}

- No replay, time compression or commentary, and no music score, announcer's voice or replay voice.
- No line, caption or image says why the struck man cannot rise; the film shows where the kick lands and that he does not rise by ten.
- No knockdown before the finish.
- No speech but the referee's.

### Anchors {#anchors}

The film's clock is the round's clock: the start bell rings as the film opens, so the round would have reached its scheduled end at 180 seconds had the knockout not ended it. These seconds are anchors the picture and sound land on:

| Second | Event |
| --- | --- |
| Every 15 seconds | One phase of the round ends and the next begins, twelve in all |
| 150.000 | Kessler's right Brazilian kick lands |
| 157 | The referee begins the count over Vermeer |
| 158 to 167 | "One" to "Ten", one a second |
| 167.6 | The referee signals the knockout |
| 168.1 | Kessler's celebration begins |
| 170.1 | The bell ends the match |
| 180 | The film ends |

## Representation {#representation}

### Polygon men {#polygon-men}

Kessler, Vermeer and the referee are polygon men. A polygon man shows his posture, limbs and stance, the turn of his head and trunk, his contacts and his place in the ring, and the colours of his clothes and gloves. He has no face, eyes, mouth, hair, skin detail, sweat, blood, swelling or marks, so no beat may ask for an expression, a wound or a visible mouthguard. A man's attention reads from where his head and trunk turn, his effort from his posture and breath, and the referee's care from what his hands and body do.

### Coarse forms {#coarse-forms}

The ring, its ropes, posts and canvas, and the light rig above are coarse forms: they show shape, position and colour, not texture. The hall beyond the light is dark and shows no figures.

## Form {#form}

### One continuous ring scene {#one-ring-scene}

The screenplay is one continuous scene in the ring, written as twelve fifteen-second phases. The first is headed `INT. KICKBOXING RING - NIGHT` and each later one `CONTINUOUS`, with no time jump. Each phase is a run of exchange beats, each beat with its record, short present-tense action, and separate speaker and dialogue blocks for the referee's words.

### A round that changes {#round-changes}

No phase repeats an earlier one. Each hands the next a changed situation in who leads, where in the ring the fight stands and what each man has learned of the other, so that pressure, angle and initiative move across the round and the whole ring is used: its centre, its four sides along the ropes, its corners, the angles the men cut and the circles they turn.
