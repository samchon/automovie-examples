# The work

## Fixed conditions {#conditions}

### One film of 180 seconds {#film}

The work is one film, `kickboxing`, running exactly 180 seconds in a landscape 16:9 frame, as the [project's delivery profile](../../../../PROJECT.md#delivery-profile) sets it and the [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#one-round) requires. It shows the first round of a three-round match from the opening bell to the wide frame after the knockout, in real time and in one continuous scene, which its screenplay divides into twelve phases of fifteen seconds each, as the [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#phases) sets.

### Polygon men in a coarse ring {#representation}

Every person is a faceless polygon man and the ring a coarse form. The film can show where each body is, how it moves, what it touches and where each head and trunk turn; sound carries breath, voice and contact. It cannot show a face or its expression, fingers, sweat, blood, swelling, reddened skin or a mouthguard, so the film carries fatigue, pain and joy in posture, timing and breath, and the count in the referee's voice and arm.

### The film in one line {#one-line}

`kickboxing`: two equal kickboxers trade the lead through a round until one man's patient body kicks teach the other to drop his arm, and a Brazilian kick over that arm lands at 150 seconds and drops him for a count he does not beat.

## Audience and experience {#audience}

### Who it is for {#viewers}

Adult viewers who know combat sport from broadcasts and want to watch a fight they believe: they notice a combination that ends in a kick, a check answered at once, a fighter cut off at the ropes. A viewer who has never watched kickboxing still follows it, because the film keeps the two men, their feet and their targets readable and lets the decisive pattern repeat in plain sight.

### What they live through {#experience}

They watch a level fight in which the lead keeps changing hands, feel the round's pressure in breath, footwork and the ropes, and slowly see what one fighter is building before the other does. The finish lands as a payoff they watched being prepared, and the count holds them in real time on a man who does not get up while the winner waits in a corner, before his release breaks out.

## What the film leaves out {#scope}

### Outside the round {#left-out}

The film shows no walkout, introductions, interval, later round, announcement of the result, raising of the winner's hand or post-fight interview, and no replay, slow motion, time compression, commentary or music. Nobody is shown attacking the fallen man, and nothing in picture or sound states a medical cause for the knockout or names an injury. Cornermen, judges, the timekeeper and the audience stay outside the light, the [apron camera's operator](../cast/camera-operator.md#who) being the one member of the crew inside it; only their sound reaches the film, as the [arena crowd](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#unseen) and the [ring sound](../sound/ring-sound.md#palette) record.
