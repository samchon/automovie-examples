# The work

The short's fixed conditions: who it is for, what it must leave them with, the one film it consists of, the frame and representation it is written for, its tone, and what the commission leaves out. Every other brief record and the screenplay work inside these conditions.

## Audience and experience {#audience-and-experience}

### Audience {#audience}

The film is for adult viewers of fight films, including viewers who do not follow kickboxing. They hear no commentary, read no caption and never hear a fighter's name, so everything they learn comes from the picture, the ring's own sounds and the referee's few English words ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#sound), [language](../../../instructions/commission.md#language)). They tell the two equal men apart by colour alone: [Benlloch](../cast/benlloch.md#wardrobe) fights in white, [Visser](../cast/visser.md#wardrobe) in black. The film can therefore ask them to track two fighters, one referee and one habit, and no more. It never relies on a rule the picture does not show being kept, such as scoring.

Left to the screenplay: how and when each piece of information reaches the audience.

### Central experience {#central-experience}

From ringside, the audience watches an even, hard round between two equals and gradually sees what one of them is building. Benlloch's right body kick lands. Visser's left arm begins to drop each time Benlloch's right knee rises. A feint proves the drop. The finish then comes from the very start Visser has learned to answer. The knockout arrives as a payoff the audience has watched being made, not as a surprise. The count, the result and the celebration then close the bout completely, in real time. This is the commission's [direction](../../../instructions/commission.md#direction), and the brief's other records serve it.

The experience fails if the audience never sees the habit form, if the finish reads as luck, or if Visser reads as a victim rather than as a champion whose own good defence is turned against him.

## The film {#the-film}

### Kickboxing, one round {#film-kickboxing}

`kickboxing`: a challenger and a defending champion, equal in size and level, fight round one of a title bout under GLORY rules. A habit the champion learns during that round lets the challenger knock him out at 150 seconds. The film holds the count, the result and the winner beside the fallen man until 180.

### Running time {#running-time}

The film runs 180.000 seconds as one continuous scene, from the round's opening bell to the end of its aftermath, with nothing outside that round ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#one-round)). This agrees with the project's [delivery profile](../../../../PROJECT.md#delivery-profile). The commission's anchors are fixed seconds of this running time. Its twelve scenes each cover fifteen of them, so each scene's start is an anchor too ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#phases)).

The opening bell sounds at 0.0 seconds, so film time and round time are the same clock ([bout](bout.md#start)).

### The frame {#the-frame}

The picture is landscape 16:9 at 1280 × 720, the user's choice ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#frame)), recorded in the [delivery profile](../../../../PROJECT.md#delivery-profile). Two men standing at kicking distance fit it head to toe only when the camera looks across the line between them. That is why the [camera](../cinematography/camera.md#side-view) keeps that side view.

## Representation {#representation}

### Faceless polygon men {#faceless-polygon-men}

Every person is a polygon man: a body of simple shaded forms with no face, eyes, mouth, hair or skin detail ([project context](../../../../PROJECT.md#representation)). A hand reads as a hand, but a count of raised fingers is not relied on to read at ringside distance. What a person feels, attends to and intends is therefore written only as what such a body can show:

- attention: the turn of the head and trunk toward its object;
- effort and fatigue: breath heard, the rise and fall of the shoulders and chest, and gloves carried lower or higher;
- a hurt: a fold of the trunk, a clamped elbow, an unweighted or lifted leg, a step back;
- intent: stance, weight and the start of a movement;
- excitement: raised arms, quick steps and loud breath;
- care: kneeling, a hand laid on another body, a lowered head.

A screenplay line that needs a facial expression, a look of the eyes, a tear, sweat, blood or the shape of a mouth has nothing to show it with. It must be rewritten as one of the signs above.

### What the representation cannot show {#what-it-cannot-show}

Beyond faces, it cannot show the ear, the outline of the jaw or the muscles of the neck. The finish's landing point therefore reads from where the shin and instep meet the side of the head and neck and from how the head moves, not from anatomy ([bout](bout.md#finish-target)).

It cannot show a mouthguard, hand wraps, tape or any text, so no story point rests on them. It shows a glove and its colour, trunks and their colour, the shin and the bare foot.

The crowd and the officials at ringside are the simplest forms that read as seated and standing people ([crowd](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#makeup), [ringside](../crowds/ringside.md)).

## Scope {#scope}

### What the commission leaves out {#left-out}

The film shows nothing outside the one round: no walk-out, introductions, interval, other round or post-fight interview. Within the round, it has:

- no commentary, music score, announcer's voice, replay, slow motion or other time compression;
- no caption, graphic or on-screen clock;
- no word from a fighter, a corner team or the crowd;
- no scoring, because the knockout ends the bout before round one is scored ([bout](bout.md#round-one));
- no formal announcement of the result and no raising of the winner's hand before 180. These would come later, once the fallen man has been cared for ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#announcement));
- no medical explanation. The film shows where the kick lands and that Visser cannot rise by ten, and never says why in medical terms ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#no-injury-mechanism)).

No one bleeds.

## Tone {#tone}

### Sober and close {#sober-and-close}

The user's [tone](../../../instructions/commission.md#tone) is sober and close: two professionals doing hard, skilled work in front of a loud room, with respect at the touch and care at the end.

In this film that means:

- The violence is the sport's own, shown at real speed for its craft and its cost in breath and balance, never relished or decorated.
- The camera stays near the men and level with them, and holds them whole rather than chasing impacts ([camera](../cinematography/camera.md#look)).
- The sound is the ring's own rather than designed punches ([sound](../sound/ring-sound.md#palette)).
- The knockout is decisive but not cruel. The film stays with the fallen man and the referee's care as much as with the winner's joy.

It fails as highlight style: slow or repeated impacts, triumphant music, a camera that dives at the fallen man, or a winner who taunts.
