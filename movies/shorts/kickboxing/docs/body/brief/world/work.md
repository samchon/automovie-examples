# The work

The short's fixed conditions: who it is for, what it must leave them with, the one film it consists of, the representation it is written for, and what the commission leaves out. Every other brief record and the screenplay work inside these conditions.

## Audience and experience {#audience-and-experience}

### Audience {#audience}

The film is made for adult viewers of fight films, including viewers who do not follow kickboxing. They hear no commentary, read no caption and never hear a fighter's name, so everything they learn comes from the picture, the ring's sounds and the referee's few words. They tell the two men apart by corner colour alone: [Vos](../cast/vos.md) fights in white and [Roux](../cast/roux.md) in black, as the [gloves](../props/gloves.md#corner-colours) settle. The film can therefore ask them to track two men, one referee and one habit, and no more; it never asks them to know a rule the picture does not show being kept.

### Central experience {#central-experience}

From a ringside position the audience watches an even, hard round between two equals and gradually sees what one of them is building: Vos's right body kick lands, Roux's left arm begins to drop each time Vos's right knee rises, a feint proves the drop, and the finish comes from the very start Roux has learned to fear. The knockout arrives as a payoff the audience has watched being made, not as a surprise, and the count, the result and the celebration then close the bout completely in real time. This is the commission's [direction](../../../instructions/commission.md#direction), and the brief's other records serve it.

## The film {#the-film}

### Kickboxing, one round {#film-kickboxing}

`kickboxing`: two equal middleweights fight one round under GLORY rules; a habit the black-corner man learns during it lets the white-corner man knock him out at 150 seconds, and the film holds the count, the result and the winner beside the fallen man to 180.

### Running time {#running-time}

The film runs 180.000 seconds as one continuous scene, from the round's opening bell to the end of its aftermath, with no time outside that round ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#one-round)). This agrees with the project's [delivery profile](../../../../PROJECT.md#delivery-profile). The commission's anchors inside it are fixed seconds of this running time, and the screenplay's twelve phases each cover fifteen of them ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#phases)).

### The frame {#the-frame}

The picture is landscape 16:9, the [delivery profile](../../../../PROJECT.md#delivery-profile)'s frame. Two men standing side by side fit it only when the camera looks across the line between them, which is why the [camera](../cinematography/camera.md#side-view) keeps that side view.

## Representation {#representation}

### Faceless polygon men {#faceless-polygon-men}

Every person is a polygon man: a body of simple shaded forms with no face, eyes, mouth, fingers' detail or skin surface ([project context](../../../../PROJECT.md#representation)). What a person feels, attends to and intends is therefore written only as what such a body can show:

- attention, as the turn of the head and trunk toward its object;
- effort and fatigue, as breath heard and the rise and fall of shoulders and chest, and as gloves held lower or higher;
- pain or a hurt, as a flinch, a clamped elbow, a lifted or unweighted leg, a step back;
- intent, as stance, weight and the start of a movement;
- excitement, as raised arms, quick steps and loud breath;
- care, as kneeling, a hand laid on another body, a lowered head.

A screenplay line that needs a facial expression, a look of the eyes, a tear, sweat, blood or a mouth's shape has nothing to be shown with and must be rewritten as one of the signs above.

### What the representation cannot show {#what-it-cannot-show}

Beyond faces, it cannot show the ear, the jaw's outline or the neck's muscles in detail, so the finish's landing point reads from where the shin meets the side of the head and neck and from the head's movement, not from anatomy ([bout rules](bout.md#finish-target)). It cannot show a mouthguard, tape or text on clothing, so no story point rests on them. The crowd is the simplest form that reads as seated and standing people in the dark beyond the ring ([crowd](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#makeup)).

## Scope {#scope}

### What the commission leaves out {#left-out}

The film shows nothing outside the one round: no walk-out, no introductions, no interval, no other round and no post-fight interview. Within the round it has no commentary, no music score, no replay, no slow motion or other time compression, no caption or graphic, no announcer's voice, no word from a fighter, a corner team or the crowd, and no medical explanation: it shows where the kick lands and that Roux cannot rise by ten, and never says why in medical terms ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#no-injury-mechanism)). No one bleeds, and the result is not formally announced before 180 ([bout rules](bout.md#result)).

## Tone {#tone}

### Respect and hard work {#respect}

The tone is sober and close: two professionals doing hard, skilled work in front of a loud room, with respect at the touch and care at the end. The violence is the sport's own, shown for its craft and its cost in breath and balance, never relished, sped up or decorated. The knockout is decisive but not cruel: the film stays with the fallen man and the referee's care as much as with the winner's joy.
