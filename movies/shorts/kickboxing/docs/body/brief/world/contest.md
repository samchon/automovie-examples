# The contest

## Rules {#rules}

### A fictional bout under GLORY's rules {#ruleset}

The bout is a fictional professional kickboxing match, three rounds of three minutes, run under GLORY's Official Rules of 16 March 2026 as the [rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#format) records them. It claims no accredited event, title or ranking. Both men weigh in at 80 kg, inside Middleweight, and wear 10-ounce gloves in their corner's colour.

### What the fighters may do {#legal-actions}

Punches to the head and body, round kicks to the legs, body and head, the Brazilian kick among them, the teep to the body and one knee from a clinch are legal, under [legal actions](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#legal-actions). Elbows, throws, sweeps, pushes to unbalance, straight kicks at the legs, strikes to the back of the head, the back or the groin, and any blow after "Break" or "Stop" or at a man with more than his feet on the canvas are fouls, and neither fighter commits one.

Pulling up the front leg without throwing a technique is stalling, so neither fighter holds his lead leg up: he lifts it to throw a technique with it, such as the teep or a knee, or to check a low kick or what he reads as one, and puts it down again. Body kicks are blocked with the arm, and high kicks with the forearm beside the head. The rules would allow a caught leg to be held for one strike, but neither fighter catches a kick in this bout.

### The clinch {#clinch}

A fighter may tie up his opponent only to throw one knee at once, then lets go. When the pair stays tied after that knee, the referee calls "Break", both step back, and the fight resumes as he steps out, without another command, under [commands](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#commands).

## The round {#round}

### Round one of three {#round-one}

The bell opens round one. The referee stands beside the two fighters at the centre, not between them, and calls "Shake hands!"; they step in and touch gloves, and as the gloves part he calls "Fight!". Both men stand equally far from him at that moment, as GLORY's rule for the command asks, so the first exchange follows the touch at once, without a reset, under the referee's [commands](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#commands). The knockout ends the match inside that round, so no interval or second round exists in the film.

## Knockdown and result {#knockdown}

### Nobody down before the finish {#no-early-knockdown}

Every blow before the Brazilian kick, landed or glancing, costs breath, guard or balance and never puts a man on the canvas, as the [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish) rules and the [consequences](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#outcomes) of the outcome table give it. The Brazilian kick is the bout's only knockdown.

### The count {#count}

A fighter is down once any part of him other than his feet touches the canvas. The referee calls "Down!", sends the standing fighter to the neutral corner farther from the fallen one and begins the count only when that fighter stands there facing the centre. The count is the referee's own: one second after it begins he calls "One", then one number a second through "Ten", under [knockdown and count](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown). If the standing fighter left the corner, the count would stop until he returned.

### The knockout {#knockout}

A fighter who has not stood with a ready guard by eight is counted straight on to ten, and an uninterrupted ten is a knockout. The referee signals it by waving both arms crossed above his head, and the timekeeper's bell then ends the match, under [the result](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#result). Only the ring doctor may then enter the ring, and the referee keeps the fallen man lying still until she reaches him, under [the aftermath](../../../research/kickboxing/knockout.md#aftermath).

## Time and place {#time}

### A night card indoors {#night}

The bout is a night card in an indoor arena. Weather and season do not reach the ring.

## Tone {#tone}

### Played straight {#straight}

The film plays the fight as sport: two equals, real technique and real rules, inside the limits the [work record](work.md#left-out) sets. Its suspense comes from the fight itself, and its finish is decisive without being cruel: nobody gloats over the fallen man or strikes him.
