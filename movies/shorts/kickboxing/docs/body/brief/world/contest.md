# The contest

The bout the round belongs to and the rules it is fought under. The rule text is in the [rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md); this record settles how this fictional contest applies it.

## The bout {#bout}

### A fictional GLORY bout {#fictional-bout}

A middleweight bout under GLORY rules on a fictional promotion night; it claims no real event, title or promotion. Both men weigh 80 kg, inside GLORY's middleweight class. The round shown is the bout's first, so both men start it fresh and neither has seen the other's answers yet. The judges' scores never matter: the knockout ends the bout before any round is scored.

### Corners and colours {#corners}

Kessler fights from the black corner in black gloves, Vermeer from the white corner in white gloves, because GLORY's ring has a black-post and a white-post corner and its gloves match the athlete's corner. The other two corners are neutral.

## The rules in force {#rules}

### What each man may throw {#legal-techniques}

Both men may punch with the padded glove, kick with the foot or lower leg to the legs, body and head, and knee. The clinch is legal only for one immediate knee, after which both release at once. Elbows, throws, sweeps, pushes meant to unbalance, head butts, kicks that thrust straight into the legs, and strikes to the back of the head, the back or the groin are fouls. Nobody strikes a man who is down, or strikes after the referee's "break" or "stop".

### The finish's target {#finish-target}

The commission lands the finish on "the area behind B's left ear at the junction of jaw and neck", and the rules forbid targeting the back of the head. This contest reads the landing point as the side of the head and neck, the hollow below and just behind Vermeer's left ear lobe where the jaw meets the neck, struck by a kick aimed at the side of his head. The referee treats it as a legal side strike, and the film never shows it as a blow to the back of the head. The [landing-point reading](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#landing-point) stays open for the user to confirm.

### Downs and the result {#downs}

A man is down when a legal blow puts anything but his feet on the canvas, and the referee counts over him by the [knockdown procedure](../cast/referee.md#count) while the other man waits in a neutral corner. A man not standing ready to fight by ten has lost by knockout, and the timekeeper's bell ends the match. The round clock runs through the count, and the bell cannot save a man who is down. Before the finish no blow puts either man down: landed blows cost breath, guard and balance, never the canvas.
