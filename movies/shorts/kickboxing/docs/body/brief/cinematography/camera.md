# The camera

The film's look: where the camera stands, how it frames and moves, when it may cut, and how the picture is lit and graded. The shot-by-shot camera belongs to each beat of the screenplay. These are the rules every beat keeps, under the commission's [camera](../../../instructions/commission.md#camera) and the user's [tone](../../../instructions/commission.md#tone).

## Look {#look-facet}

### A reacting camera at ringside {#look}

The camera is one observer at ringside who reacts to the action and relates the two men, the way sober broadcast and fiction coverage do. It is not a highlight reel ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#sober-coverage)).

It works from the apron, just outside the ropes, as broadcast apron cameras do. It moves round all four sides as the fight needs ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#apron-cameras)). Its lens sits at about the height of the men's heads, looking slightly down. From there it sees the men's feet over the top rope when they stand at the middle of the ring or beyond it, but not when they come nearer its own ropes ([rope geometry](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#main-cameras)). So when the fight comes toward the camera's side, it moves along the apron to another side. Where it cannot, the top rope crosses the men's legs, and their feet stay in view behind it. It never enters the ring.

It never goes to the canvas, overhead or onto a fighter's body. It never takes a fighter's point of view.

## Framing {#framing-facet}

### The side view {#side-view}

The base frame is the head-to-toe two-shot from the side, with the line between the men running across the screen. Both men, their feet, the struck target and both heads are readable together on that one screen axis ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#camera), [coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#feet-in-frame)).

The frame keeps room:

- above the heads for a raised kicking leg;
- below the planted feet for the canvas.

Both men stay in focus.

A frame that drops either man's feet, the struck target or either head during an exchange is wrong, however close or dramatic.

### Closer and wider {#sizes}

**Closer.** Between exchanges, after the referee's break or at a reset with the men out of range, the camera may come closer: to the two men from the knees up, or to one man's guard or one man's breath. It goes back to the base frame before the next exchange starts. A clinch's knee is a strike, so the clinch keeps the base frame.

**Wider.** The camera widens to the whole ring in three places:

- for the count, which holds the neutral corner and the fallen man together ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#count));
- for the last image, which holds the winner, the fallen man and the referee together ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#ending));
- when the men cross the ring.

## Movement {#movement-facet}

### It moves because they move {#movement}

The camera moves only in answer to the fighters, smoothly and at their pace:

- it tracks along the ropes as they circle;
- it eases in as they close;
- it eases out as they part ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#motion)).

It keeps the side view as their line turns. When their line swings round, it moves round the ring with them rather than letting them line up toward the lens.

When the [referee](../cast/referee.md#side-view) blocks the view, the camera moves round him rather than cutting ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#referee-occlusion)). It works from the side of the men away from the referee where it can.

It never shakes for effect, whips, spins or flies.

## Cutting {#cutting-facet}

### Cuts only at resets {#cuts}

Long takes carry the round. A cut is allowed only at a reset: after the referee's break, or at a moment when the men stand apart out of range. It may go from one side of the ring to the other, as broadcasts do ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cutting), [screen direction](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#screen-direction)). The audience keeps the men apart by white and black, not by which side of the screen each is on ([work](../world/work.md#audience)).

The camera never cuts during an exchange. It also never cuts:

- from the start of the finishing kick's chamber to the end of Visser's fall ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#fall));
- inside the count, which plays in real time ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#count)).

No cut skips time or repeats it ([work](../world/work.md#left-out)).

## Light and grade {#light-facet}

### Real light, one grade {#grade}

The picture keeps the arena's real light ([arena](../sets/arena.md#lighting)): a bright, even ring and a dim, warm ringside falling into darkness. It is graded once for the whole film, near neutral, with no colour shift between phases ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#colour-grade)).

Every frame runs at real speed, as the [work record](../world/work.md#left-out) sets ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#no-slow-motion)).
