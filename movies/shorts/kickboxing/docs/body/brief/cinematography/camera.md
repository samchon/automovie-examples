# The camera

How the film is seen: one ringside camera that reacts to the action and relates the two men ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#camera)). The laws, their sources and their measurable targets are in the [coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#laws); this record settles the ones the film keeps.

## Grammar {#grammar}

### One take in real time {#one-take}

The whole film is one uncut shot from 0 to 180 at normal speed ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl1)). Being one ringside camera, it never switches to an overhead, a mat camera or a point-of-view shot ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl11)); the work's other exclusions hold as the [work record](../world/work.md#left-out) states them. What broadcast does with cuts, this camera does by moving. The screenplay's twelve scene headings are addresses in this one shot, not cuts ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#phases)).

### Ringside, outside the ropes {#ringside}

The camera is a ringside presence: it travels the whole perimeter of the ring outside the ropes, never enters them, and looks over the top rope from a little above a standing man's head, as the audience at ringside would ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl2); [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#direction)). It moves no faster than a real ringside rig could ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#targets)).

### The side view {#side-view}

In every exchange the camera looks across the line between the two men, close to perpendicular to it, so they stand side by side across the frame with both men's feet, both heads and the struck target in the picture together ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl3); [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#camera)). It frames them head to foot, close enough that each body's turns and the guard's height read, never so close that a foot, a head or the target leaves the frame. When the line between them turns, the camera travels round the ring to keep the side view; when they move across the ring, it follows ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl5)).

### The referee beyond the fighters {#referee-beyond}

The referee keeps his side view from the far side of the fighters' line, so the camera holds the near side and he reads between and behind the two men ([referee](../cast/referee.md#positioning)). When he crosses the view, at a break or at the knockdown, the camera arcs round him rather than let him hide a head, a foot or the target ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl4)).

### Changing sides only in the open {#changing-sides}

The camera crosses to the other side of the fighters' line only while the men reset, circle apart or are separated, never during an exchange, so the viewer always knows who is on which side ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl6)).

### The ropes and the corners {#ropes}

When a man is driven to the ropes or into a corner, the camera moves to the neighbouring side of the ring and looks along the ropes, keeping the side view of the pair and leaving the ropes out of the middle of the picture ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl7)).

## The key moments {#moments}

### The tell from the kick side {#the-tell}

The exchanges in which Roux's habit forms and is proved are seen from Vos's right side, which is Roux's left, so Vos's rising right knee and Roux's dropping left arm face the camera ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl8); [Roux](../cast/roux.md#the-tell)). Exchanges that do not build the tell may be seen from either side.

### The finish {#the-finish}

The finish at 150.000 is seen from the same side: the instep and lower shin landing over Roux's lowered left arm on the side of his head face the camera. As the kick comes, the camera rises and looks down onto the canvas beyond Roux, because he falls on his right side, away from it ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl8)). It holds both men head to foot through the kick and holds Roux through the whole fall, from the loss of support to the body at rest, with no cut and no reframing that loses him ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#fall); [Roux](../cast/roux.md#the-fall)).

### The count in one frame {#the-count}

Between the finish and 157 the camera travels while the referee calls "Down!" and sends Vos to the neutral corner, to a place beyond Roux looking from him toward Vos's corner, raised and looking down. From 157 to the knockout signal it holds Roux on the canvas in the foreground, the kneeling referee counting on his fingers beside him, and Vos standing in the neutral corner in the depth of the picture, all in one frame, in real time ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl9); [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#count)).

### The wide ending {#wide-ending}

From 168.1 the camera widens and slows to hold, in one wide frame to 180, Vos celebrating with raised gloves and quick steps, Roux on the canvas and the referee attending him ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl10); [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#ending)). The last image holds all three.

## Look {#look}

### Observational realism {#realism}

The look is observational and real: a searching, reactive camera that follows the fight as a ringside watcher would and never stylises it ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#look)). Both fighters and the referee stay in focus wherever they are in the ring ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl12)).

### Light {#light}

The ring is lit evenly from above, so no man drops into shadow anywhere on the canvas, and the hall beyond the first rows falls to darkness ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl12); [ring](../sets/ring.md#arena)). The light does not change during the film.
