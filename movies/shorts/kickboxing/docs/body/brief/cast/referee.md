# The referee

The visible referee: an adult man who is always active, through positioning, the side view, clinch breaks, signals and the knockdown procedure, and whose signals, attention and care can be read ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#referee)). His conduct follows the [referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md).

## Identity {#identity-facet}

### Who he is {#identity}

Graham Tull is a British referee in his early fifties, of international grade, with many title bouts behind him ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#attire)). He is neither fighter's countryman. That is the brief's choice, so that the official reads as neutral to both corners. He speaks English, his first language, and every word he says in the film is a short English command or a count ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#language)).

In the screenplay he is REFEREE. His name is never heard or shown.

### His body and dress {#dress}

He is an ordinary adult man of middle height, shorter and heavier than the fighters. He wears a white short-sleeved shirt, a black bow tie, black trousers, black shoes and pale medical gloves ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#attire)). His white shirt and dark trousers read apart from both fighters, the man in white trunks and the man in black.

## How he works the round {#conduct}

### The side view {#side-view}

He stands off the line between the fighters, near the perpendicular through its middle. That way he sees both men in profile and keeps an open space between them in view. He is never behind either man ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#side-view)).

When they circle he moves on a wider circle around the same centre, smoothly, without bouncing or mirroring their footwork ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#movement)). He uses the edge of the ring and keeps clear of a round kick's reach.

### Distance {#distance}

In open exchanges he stays at a prudent distance, outside a kick's reach. He closes in when:

- the men reach the ropes or a corner;
- they tie up;
- a stoppage may be near;
- the crowd is loud enough to bury his voice.

He never steps in early or stands in the way of a close exchange ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#distance)).

### Clinch breaks {#clinch-break}

At a lawful clinch he closes in and watches the knee and the release, and says nothing ([bout](../world/bout.md#clinch)).

At a tie-up, the break follows GLORY's order ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#clinch-break)):

1. He closes in and calls "Break!"
2. Both men step back, and he steps in between them at an angle, never from behind.
3. If they have not parted at the word, he parts them both, with an open hand on each man's chest or near shoulder at full arm's length.
4. He takes one full step straight back, and never walks between them afterwards.
5. The men fight on as he steps back, with no further command.

### His words {#words}

He uses only the words this round needs, each short and plain, and nothing else ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#commands)):

- "Fight!" to start the round;
- "Break!" to part a tie-up;
- "Down!" at the knockdown;
- "Neutral corner!" to send Benlloch away;
- "One" to "Ten" for the count;
- "Doctor!" to call the doctor;
- "Stay down." to Visser after the result.

He does not encourage, comment or speak to the crowd. His count is loud and clear, one word a second.

### His signals {#signals}

His hand signals are the ones the study gives ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#signals)):

- a forward arm and open hand for "Fight";
- a point to the canvas for "Down";
- an arm pointed at the far neutral corner while he stands between the men;
- for the count, his raised arm brought down in one stroke toward Visser's head on each number, with the hand open. The study's raised fingers are not relied on, because the hands may not read at ringside distance ([work](../world/work.md#faceless-polygon-men)). The count reads from his voice and that stroke together;
- the knockout signal: both arms swept to cross above his head, then waved apart;
- a raised, beckoning arm toward the doctor at the neutral steps.

### What his attention shows {#attention}

He has no face, so his attention reads from the turn of his head and trunk ([work](../world/work.md#faceless-polygon-men)):

- in exchanges, toward the space between the men;
- at a clinch, toward the hold;
- during the count, toward Visser, with turns of the head up to Benlloch in the corner;
- after the result, between Visser, Benlloch and the doctor's steps: down to Visser, up to Benlloch with his raised palm, and across to the doctor as he beckons.

His posture tells his reading of the moment. It is upright and easy at range, crouched and close on the ropes, and bent or kneeling at the fallen man. It is never theatrical ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#level)).

## The knockdown {#knockdown-facet}

### His procedure {#knockdown}

From the fall to the bell he works in the [study's order](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#knockdown-procedure), which the commission's anchors fix ([bout](../world/bout.md#knockdown-and-result)):

1. He calls "Down!" and points to the canvas as Visser falls.
2. He steps between the two men, with his body shielding the fallen man, and sends Benlloch to the far neutral corner with "Neutral corner!" and a pointing arm. He does not rush him.
3. He watches Benlloch go, moving to Visser as he does. He does not count until Benlloch stands in the corner facing the centre.
4. At 157 he places himself at Visser's head, bent or kneeling, where Visser can see and hear him. He stands on the side away from Benlloch's corner, so that Benlloch stays beyond Visser in his view.
5. He counts from "One" at 158 to "Ten" at 167, aloud and with the [count stroke](#signals) of his arm, with his head turned to Visser and turning up to Benlloch. He does not touch or help Visser during the count. He does not stop at "Eight", because Visser is still down.
6. At 167.6 he gives the knockout signal.
7. At once he calls "Doctor!" with a beckoning arm, and moves round Visser's head to kneel on Benlloch's side of him. Through the count Visser lay between him and Benlloch; now his own body is between them as Benlloch is released ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#knockdown-procedure)).

### His care {#care}

After the result he gives Visser the care the rules let a referee give, and that is the commission's "support" ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#care)):

- He kneels close at Visser's head, on Benlloch's side of him ([knockdown](#knockdown), step 7).
- He rests a hand lightly on Visser's upper arm or shoulder to keep him lying still, and says "Stay down."
- He does not turn him, sit him up or take out his mouthguard, which is the doctor's work.
- With his body between them and a raised palm toward Benlloch, he keeps Benlloch's celebration away from Visser.
- He stays kneeling until the doctor is with them. The doctor's entry is in [ringside](../crowds/ringside.md#doctor).
