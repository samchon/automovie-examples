# The referee

## Identity {#identity}

### A senior professional official {#who}

An adult man in his late forties, a professional referee with long experience of championship bouts. The film never names him and he has no history with either fighter.

## Body and recognition {#body}

### Build and dress {#build-and-dress}

Of ordinary athletic build, a little shorter and lighter than the fighters. He wears a long-sleeved light grey shirt, black trousers and black shoes, so from any side he reads as the one clothed man among two bare-chested ones. His hands are bare.

## Movement style {#movement}

### Calm and economical {#calm}

He walks, never bounces, runs or waves his arms, and his steps are short and planted. Between commands his hands hang ready in front of his waist and his head and trunk turn to whichever man is working, so the audience reads his attention from where he faces.

## Repertoire {#repertoire}

### Positioning {#positioning}

He works the perimeter of the action, across the fighters' line from the point between them, so he sees both men side-on and never stands between them or behind either one, as the [referee mechanics](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#positioning) describe. When they circle he walks the other way around them; when his side runs out of room he goes around behind the action to the other side.

### Closing in {#closing-in}

He comes closer when a fighter is on the ropes, when the pair clinch and when a man is hurt, approaching from the side at an angle.

### The clinch break {#clinch-break}

When a clinch holds after its one knee, he reaches an open arm between the two men at arm's length and calls "Break!", steps in as they step back, then steps straight back out, under [breaking a clinch](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#clinch-break).

### Knockdown and count {#knockdown}

He calls "Down!" pointing at the canvas, then points the standing fighter to the farther neutral corner, calling "Neutral corner!". He takes a place a step from the fallen man that keeps the corner in front of him, waits until the standing fighter faces the centre, and counts aloud, one number a second, each number marked by his counting hand dropping toward the fallen man's eyes and both hands from six, under the [knockdown mechanics](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#knockdown). He keeps the count going only while the corner holds.

### The wave-off {#wave-off}

At ten he waves both arms crossed above his head, then kneels at the fallen fighter's side, by his chest, keeps him lying still with a hand on his shoulder and stays with him until the ring doctor arrives and after, as [after the knockout](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#after-knockout) records. He does not take out the fallen man's mouthguard or turn him.

## Speech and voice {#voice}

### Few words, all English {#english}

He speaks only the ring's commands in English, "Shake hands!", "Fight!", "Break!", "Stop!", "Down!", "Neutral corner!", and the count, as the [commands](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#commands) record them, in a firm middle voice that carries over the crowd without shouting. The count is even and unhurried whatever the crowd does.

## Values and motives {#values}

### Safety first, then fairness {#safety}

He wants a fair fight and nobody hurt beyond the sport's risk. In every phase his objective is to see both men's hands and feet without blocking either, and at a knockdown his attention goes to the fallen man before anything else.

## Relationships {#relationships}

### His authority {#authority}

Both fighters obey him at once and without argument; he treats them identically.

## Knowledge {#knowledge}

### What he knows {#rules}

He knows the [contest's rules](../world/contest.md#rules) exactly and makes no medical judgment beyond whether a man can go on.

## State changes {#state}

### After the result {#after-result}

From the wave-off he stays kneeling at the fallen man's side, one hand on his shoulder, watching him, and no longer looks at the winner. When the [ring doctor](ring-doctor.md#attending) arrives she kneels at the fallen man's head, and he stays where he is, kneeling at his side by his chest.
