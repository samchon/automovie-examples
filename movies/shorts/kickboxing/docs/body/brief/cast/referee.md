# The referee

The one official inside the ropes. His conduct follows the [referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md) and the [contest's rules](../world/contest.md#rules).

## Identity {#identity}

### Who he is {#who}

An adult man, an experienced referee of professional kickboxing, unnamed: nobody calls him anything. The screenplay calls him REFEREE.

### Appearance {#appearance}

A white short-sleeved shirt, black trousers, black shoes and blue medical gloves, the ring referee's dress of the WAKO rules without its bow tie. His covered torso and long trousers set him apart from both bare-chested fighters, his white shirt from Vermeer's white trunks.

## Conduct during the exchanges {#conduct}

### Position and attention {#position}

He is never still while the men fight. He works from the side of their line, a step or two outside their reach, where he sees both men's feet, hands and heads, and moves as they circle so he stays side-on to them. He closes in when they reach the ropes or a corner and when they clinch, and he never stands where he would block a blow or an escape. His attention reads from his head and trunk, which turn to follow every exchange, and his readiness from his bent knees and raised hands.

### Breaking and stopping {#breaking}

When the men hold without an immediate knee, or stay locked after the knee, he steps between them with "Break!"; both step back and the fight resumes as he steps out. He uses "Stop!" only to halt the fight for a foul. He starts the round with "Fight!" once both men stand clear of him.

## The knockdown and the result {#knockdown}

### The count {#count}

When Vermeer goes down, he calls "Down!" with a sweep of his arm and sends Kessler to the farthest neutral corner, pointing to it with "Neutral corner!", and checks over his shoulder that Kessler stays there. On the count's [anchored seconds](../world/work.md#anchors) he takes his place beside Vermeer, facing him, and counts from "One" to "Ten", one number a second, showing each to Vermeer with his fingers.

### The result and his care {#result}

On the knockout's anchored second he crosses his forearms above his head, the knockout signal. Then he kneels beside Vermeer and keeps him lying still with a hand on his shoulder, neither turning him nor taking out his mouthguard, because that care belongs to the doctor, and he raises his other arm to the ringside with "Doctor!". He stays at Vermeer's side to the end of the film, his head turning between Vermeer and the ringside where the doctor sits, his body between Vermeer and the rest of the ring.

## Voice {#voice}

### His words {#words}

He speaks English and only these words: "Fight!", "Break!", "Stop!", "Down!", "Neutral corner!", the count from "One" to "Ten", and "Doctor!". His voice is clipped and loud enough to carry over the hall, and its urgency, more than anything he can show, is his expression.
