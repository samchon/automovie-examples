# Bastien Roux

The commission's fighter B: the black-corner man, who learns the habit that loses him the bout and receives the finish ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#fighters)).

## Identity {#identity}

### Name {#name}

Bastien Roux, a French professional middleweight. The screenplay calls him Roux. His name is never spoken in the film; the audience knows him as the man in black ([work record](../world/work.md#audience)).

### Body {#body}

An adult man, 180 cm tall and 80 kg, with a full-time kickboxer's lean, muscled build, the same height and weight as Vos ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#fighters)). He is not smaller, slower or weaker than Vos; he loses to a habit, not to a mismatch.

### Wardrobe {#wardrobe}

Black trunks that stop above the knee, black gloves, bare feet and bare upper body ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#ring-and-equipment); [gloves](../props/gloves.md#corner-colours)). His wardrobe does not change.

## How he fights {#fighting}

### Level and stance {#level}

He fights at the same GLORY championship level as Vos, with the same markers but one: his chains run longer than Vos's ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#level-markers); [repertoire](#repertoire)). He stands orthodox, left foot forward, so his left side, ribs and arm face Vos's right kick ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#tell)).

### Repertoire {#repertoire}

He has the whole exchange vocabulary the commission names and uses each of it during the round ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#exchange-vocabulary)):

- long combinations, his trademark: a few bursts in the round of four to six strikes, punches finished with a right low kick to Vos's lead leg in the Dutch pattern, among the two- and three-strike chains that make up most of his work ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#chaining); [targets](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#targets));
- feints of the jab and the shoulder to bring Vos's guard up before the low kick ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#feints));
- checks of Vos's low kicks, answered from the replanted leg ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#check-counter));
- slips, parries and rolls against Vos's punches, and the high guard and arm blocks against everything ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#defences));
- footwork that walks Vos down and cuts off the ring, moving parallel to him rather than following ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#ring)).

## Mind {#mind}

### Personality and values {#personality}

Confident, forward and proud of his work rate; he believes the man who throws more and walks forward takes the round. He is honest in the ring: he means the touch, takes his punishment without complaint and keeps coming. His flaw is the strength of his adjustments: once something hurts him, he guards it hard.

### Game plan {#game-plan}

He means to take the round with volume: walk Vos down, cut off the ring, put him on the ropes and chop his lead leg with low kicks at the end of long combinations. He leads much of the round, and his pressure is real: it takes Vos to the ropes more than once and costs him on his lead leg ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#direction); [Vos](vos.md#cost-of-blows)). When Vos's body kicks start to hurt, Roux keeps pressing but guards his left side.

### Knowledge {#knowledge}

At the bell he knows Vos from footage: a patient counter-fighter with dangerous kicks, whose right body kick is his signature. He has never seen Vos throw a Brazilian kick, because Vos has not thrown one in a recorded fight ([Vos](vos.md#repertoire)). He respects the right body kick, which is why it can condition him ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#feints)). He never knows that his left arm has started dropping at Vos's knee: the drop is a reflex he has not decided on. When Vos's feint lands a blow over it, he feels the blow, not the pattern, so he does not correct it.

### Relationship with Vos {#relationship}

They have never fought before. Roux respects Vos as an equal and wants to beat him in the open, by work; he feels no grudge. Vos's side of it is in [his record](vos.md#relationship).

## Habits and tells {#habits}

### The tell {#the-tell}

His habit is learned during this round, on screen ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#tell)):

- Baseline at the bell: when Vos's right knee rises, Roux keeps his left glove at his head and brings his left elbow down over his ribs only as the kick comes, in time to block it on the arm.
- What changes it: Vos's right body kicks reaching his left side, landed or blocked on the arm, at least three times, with the cost in breath each one carries ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#tell)).
- The habit: from then on, whenever Vos's right knee rises, Roux's left arm drops to his ribs at once, before the kick's line is decided, whatever the knee lift turns into. It leaves the left side of his head open for that moment.
- How it shows: the audience sees the drop at least twice before Vos tests it, so the pattern is readable without commentary ([work record](../world/work.md#central-experience)).
- The movement: the drop is the faulty form of an elbow block, the elbow down against the ribs and the glove lowered from the jaw, held for about the time the expected kick would take and then returned, by the [arm-drop law](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#arm-drop).
- The proof: once before the finish, Vos raises the right knee without kicking, the arm drops, and Vos's right hand, the strike that reaches the side the drop opens, lands over it on the left side of Roux's head, glancing, costing him balance but not putting him down ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#tell)).
- The habit fires one last time as Vos's right knee rises for the finish, and the Brazilian kick comes down over the lowered arm at 150.000 ([Brazilian kick study](../../../research/kickboxing/brazilian-kick.md#why-it-works)).

Roux shows no other tell. Exactly which exchanges carry these steps, and when, is the screenplay's.

## Voice {#voice}

### Breath only {#breath}

He never speaks ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#language)). His breath is heavier than Vos's from the middle of the round on, because his output is higher and the body kicks take it, and it is forced out of him with each body kick that lands ([sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#breath)). On the canvas his breath is heard, ragged, which is part of how he reads as conscious ([down](#down)).

## States over the round {#states}

### The cost of blows {#cost-of-blows}

Before 150 Vos's blows cost him breath, guard and balance and never more: each right body kick forces breath out and makes his left elbow clamp lower for the next exchanges, a check rebounds his low kick, and the proof's blow over the dropped arm turns his head and shakes his balance; but his legs stay sturdy and his hands come back up ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#non-finishing-contacts); [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish)). He is not down before the finish.

### The fall {#the-fall}

The finish drops him in one articulated, uncut fall onto his right side, in the phases the [Brazilian kick study](../../../research/kickboxing/brazilian-kick.md#fall) records ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#fall)):

1. the shin lands and his head and neck turn and drop toward his right, his left arm still low;
2. both knees give at once and his guard goes slack, with no step to catch himself;
3. his trunk tips to his right as his knees fold, and neither arm reaches out to break the fall;
4. his right hip and shoulder meet the canvas, then the right side of his head;
5. he comes to rest on his right side.

The fall's end is the side-lying rest the commission rules, not a fall onto his back. It runs across the 150-second boundary without a cut ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#phases)).

### Down {#down}

Through the count he is conscious but cannot get up: he stirs, lifts his head, pushes at the canvas with a glove, draws a knee up, and sinks back, and he never gets his feet under him before "Ten" ([bout rules](../world/bout.md#count)). He never lies limp and still while the count runs, because a referee would stop a count for a man who looked to need immediate help ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown)). Nothing in the film explains why he cannot rise ([work record](../world/work.md#left-out)). After the knockout he stops trying and lies on his side under the referee's hand, still breathing, until 180 ([referee](referee.md#care)).
