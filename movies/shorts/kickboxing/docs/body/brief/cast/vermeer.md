# Ruud Vermeer

The commission's fighter B, who receives the finish. Technique laws are in the [technique study](../../../research/kickboxing/techniques.md) and round patterns in the [bout-patterns study](../../../research/kickboxing/bout-patterns.md).

## Identity {#identity}

### Who he is {#who}

A fictional professional middleweight at championship level from a Dutch kickboxing gym, whose style is the punch-and-low-kick pressure of K-1 and GLORY. The film never says his name, and the audience knows him as the man in white. The screenplay calls him VERMEER.

### Body {#body}

An adult man, 180 cm and 80 kg, the same height and weight as Kessler.

## Appearance {#appearance}

### Fight kit {#kit}

White trunks ending above the knee, white gloves, bare torso and feet, with hand wraps under the gloves and a mouthguard that the representation does not show.

## Fighting style {#style}

### Stance and pressure {#stance}

He fights orthodox, left foot forward, and walks forward behind a high guard, cutting the ring off to drive Kessler towards the ropes and corners. He leads much of the round, and his pressure is the problem Kessler's plan answers.

### Repertoire {#repertoire}

He opens with punches and ends with a kick, in the [Dutch pattern](../../../research/kickboxing/bout-patterns.md#styles): long combinations of jab, cross, hooks and body shots that finish with the right low kick to Kessler's lead thigh, the kick landing as he leaves punching range. He feints with his hands and shoulders to draw Kessler's counters. He defends with a high guard, parries, catches, blocks and rolls under hooks, slips the straight punches, and checks Kessler's low kicks with his lead shin and counters off the check. In a clinch he lands one knee and releases. Every one of these is at championship level.

## Habit and knowledge {#habit}

### The arm drop {#arm-drop}

At the bell he has no tell: he covers a body kick with his left elbow only once he reads it as a body kick. Once Kessler's right body kicks have landed on his left ribs and cost him breath, he starts dropping his left elbow to his ribs the instant Kessler's right knee rises, before the kick's height shows. From then on he does it every time, through the finish, and each drop takes his left glove off his left cheek and jaw.

### What he knows {#knows}

He knows Kessler's body kick hurts and he means to stop paying for it. He does not know that his answer has become a habit Kessler can read, and he never sees the Brazilian kick coming, because it rises from the same knee lift his arm has learned to answer.

### How he reads the proof {#reads-the-proof}

When Kessler's feint draws his arm down and the follow-up lands over it, he takes the blow as a fast hand he failed to see behind a feint, not as a sign of a pattern in himself. He answers as his style does: he walks forward harder to take Kessler's room and keeps his eyes on Kessler's hands. The habit survives because it lives below his choices. The drop answers the rising knee in the instant of the chamber, before he could see where the kick goes ([tells](../../../research/kickboxing/bout-patterns.md#tells)), and Kessler's real body kicks still come from that knee, so the drop still saves his ribs every time one does.

## Relationships {#relationships}

### Kessler {#opponent}

He has never fought Kessler and holds no grudge; he respects him as an equal and steps in to touch gloves at the start, then fights from the touch. He means to break Kessler down with pressure and low kicks, and takes Kessler's lateral movement as a sign he is winning the centre.

### The referee {#obeys-referee}

He obeys the referee at once: he breaks when told and stops on "stop".

## Voice {#voice}

### Breath and effort {#breath}

He never speaks. His sound is breath: hard exhales behind his combinations, and a breath that catches each time a body kick lands. After the finish he makes no sound but slow breathing.

## State through the round {#state}

### What the round costs him {#costs}

Kessler's body kicks cost him breath and pull his left elbow tighter to his ribs; checked low kicks cost his shin; glancing punches cost him guard and balance. None puts him down before the finish.

### The fall {#fall}

The [finish](../world/contest.md#finish-target) takes his muscle tone at once. He does not step or reach to save himself: his legs give, and he falls away from the kick onto his right side in an unbroken drop, by the [knockout fall law](../../../research/kickboxing/techniques.md#knockout-fall), and is on the canvas within about a second. His fall passes through its phases in view: the support going from his legs, his body turning to his right as it drops, his right side meeting the canvas, and his body settling at rest.

### Down through the count {#down}

He lies on his right side. During the count he makes only small movements that come to nothing, a hand or a knee shifting on the canvas, and he never gathers himself to rise. He is still down at ten and stays down through 180. Nothing in the film says why.
