# Teo Kessler

The commission's fighter A, who delivers the finish. Technique laws are in the [technique study](../../../research/kickboxing/techniques.md) and round patterns in the [bout-patterns study](../../../research/kickboxing/bout-patterns.md).

## Identity {#identity}

### Who he is {#who}

A fictional professional middleweight at championship level who came to kickboxing from karate, which is why the snapping Brazilian kick is in his game at all. The film never says his name: no one speaks it, and the audience knows him as the man in black. The screenplay calls him KESSLER.

### Body {#body}

An adult man, 180 cm and 80 kg, the same height and weight as Vermeer. The equality is the premise: the bout is decided by a learned habit, not by size or reach.

## Appearance {#appearance}

### Fight kit {#kit}

Black trunks ending above the knee, black gloves, bare torso and feet, with hand wraps under the gloves and a mouthguard that the representation does not show. Black against Vermeer's white tells the two men apart from any side.

## Fighting style {#style}

### Stance and movement {#stance}

He fights orthodox, left foot forward, on the balls of his feet. He prefers long range in the open ring and keeps it with lateral steps, pivots off the line of attack, circles and the teep. Pressed towards the ropes, he circles out or pivots and changes places rather than covering up.

### Repertoire {#repertoire}

His main weapon is the right roundhouse kick to the body, thrown off his rear leg onto Vermeer's left side. Around it he throws the jab, the cross, hooks and uppercuts in combinations, the right low kick, the teep and, when a clinch comes, one knee before releasing. He feints with his hands, his lead knee and above all his rising right knee. He defends with slips, rolls, parries, catches and blocks, and checks Vermeer's low kicks with his lead shin, firing the cross or an inside low kick straight off the check. Every one of these is at championship level, with none of the beginner faults the [level markers](../../../research/kickboxing/techniques.md#level-markers) list.

### The Brazilian kick {#brazilian-kick}

His one rare weapon is the right [Brazilian kick](../../../research/kickboxing/techniques.md#brazilian-kick). It starts from exactly the chamber of his right body kick; at the top of the knee lift his support foot pivots further, his hip turns over, and his instep and lower shin come down over a dropped guard onto the side of the head. Thrown into a high guard it is blocked and leaves him exposed in recoil, so he throws it only into a guard he has seen fall.

## Plan and knowledge {#plan}

### What he learns {#what-he-learns}

He enters the round knowing nothing of Vermeer's answers to his weapons; he learns them by watching. He sees that his right body kick costs Vermeer breath, then sees Vermeer's left arm start dropping to the ribs each time his right knee rises. He does not trust a pattern he has only seen: he proves it with a feint of the rising knee, which draws the arm down, and a follow-up that lands over it.

### His objective {#objective}

He means to win by a clean finish rather than on points, and he is patient enough to absorb Vermeer's pressure while he builds one. His plan in this round is to make the body kick hurt, let the habit form, prove it, and only then throw the Brazilian kick from the same chamber. He does not throw the Brazilian kick before the habit is proven, so the audience sees it for the first time as the finish.

## Voice {#voice}

### Breath and effort {#breath}

He never speaks. His sound is breath: a short exhale with each strike, breathing that deepens as the round goes on, and after the result an excited, open breath and wordless sound, never a word.

## State through the round {#state}

### What the round costs him {#costs}

Vermeer's low kicks that beat his check cost him a moment off his lead leg; body punches cost him breath; glancing head blows cost him guard and balance. None puts him down. His breathing grows heavier through the round.

### After the finish {#after-finish}

On the referee's order he goes to the farthest neutral corner and stays there through the count. After the knockout signal, from the celebration's [anchored second](../world/work.md#anchors), his gloves go up and he takes quick victory steps, each one landing his full weight on the canvas. He keeps clear of Vermeer and the officials attending him, so that one wide frame holds his celebration beside the fallen man.

## Relationships {#relationships}

### Vermeer {#opponent}

They have never fought each other and hold no grudge: each knows the other as an equal at the top of the division. Kessler steps in to touch gloves at the start as a mark of that respect and fights from the touch without resetting. He takes no liberty with a beaten man: he stops on the referee's word and celebrates away from him.

### The referee {#obeys-referee}

He obeys the referee at once: he breaks when told, goes to the neutral corner when sent and stays until the result.
