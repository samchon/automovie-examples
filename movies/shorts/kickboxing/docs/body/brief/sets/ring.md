# The ring

The square of canvas where the whole film happens. Its rule dimensions are in the [rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#the-ring).

## Shape and landmarks {#shape}

### The square and its corners {#square}

A raised square ring of the larger GLORY size, so the men have room to circle, angle and cut each other off. Four ropes close its sides. Kessler's black corner and Vermeer's white corner face each other diagonally across the ring; the two corners between them are neutral. The fighters' stools are taken out at the bell, so the corners are bare during the round.

### Zones {#zones}

The screenplay places every exchange in one of the ring's zones: the open centre, one of the four sides along the ropes, one of the four corners, or the space between them that a man crosses when he cuts an angle. Each side and corner is named by its corner posts, such as "the ropes between the white corner and the far neutral corner", so a reader can follow where the fight moves.

## Dressing and light {#dressing}

### Canvas, ropes and posts {#canvas}

The canvas is mid-grey and bare of logos, so black and white gloves, bare feet and shadows read against it. The ropes are white. The corner pads are black in Kessler's corner, white in Vermeer's, and grey in the two neutral corners.

### Light {#light}

A square rig hangs over the ring and lights the canvas evenly and hard from above, with no coloured or moving stage light. The light ends at the apron: the hall beyond is dark.

## How the space is used {#use}

### Inside and outside the ropes {#inside-outside}

Inside the ropes are only the two fighters and the referee until the doctor is called. A man driven to the ropes feels them at his back and must step or pivot out; nobody holds them, because that is a foul.
