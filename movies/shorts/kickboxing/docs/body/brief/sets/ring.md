# The ring

## Shape {#shape}

### A large GLORY ring {#large-ring}

A square raised ring of GLORY's large size, a pale matte canvas enclosed by four ropes, as the [contest format](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#format) gives it. Padded posts stand at the four corners. The canvas runs on past the ropes as a narrow apron on all four sides, wide enough for a camera operator to stand on, and beyond the apron the ring drops to the dark arena floor. A short flight of steps leads up to the apron at each of the two neutral corners, used only by officials.

## Zones and landmarks {#zones}

### The centre {#centre}

The middle of the canvas. Whoever holds it makes the other man move around him, so taking it back is an act of its own.

### The open ring {#open-ring}

The canvas between the centre and the ropes, where the fighters circle, angle off and trade.

### The ropes {#ropes}

The band of canvas just inside each side of ropes, about two steps deep. A fighter backed into it has two steps left before his back touches the ropes, and his ways out run along them, to one side or, after a feint that way, to the other, as [the ropes](../../../research/kickboxing/ring-craft.md#ropes) describes.

### The fighters' corners {#corners}

Arjan's corner has a black post and Bastien's corner, diagonally across, a white one, each matching its fighter's gloves under the rules. The fight never returns to them inside the film.

### The neutral corners {#neutral-corners}

The two remaining corners have grey posts. After a knockdown the standing fighter is sent to whichever of them lies farther from the fallen man, and he waits there facing the centre.

### The apron {#apron}

The strip of canvas outside the ropes, where the [apron camera](../cinematography/coverage.md#apron-camera) works. Nobody else stands on it during the round.

## Orientation {#orientation}

### Where the corners lie {#corner-layout}

As the [hard camera](../cinematography/coverage.md#hard-camera) sees the ring, the black corner stands at the left end of its side and the near neutral corner, which holds the [corner camera](../cinematography/coverage.md#corner-camera), at the right end. Across the ring, the far neutral corner stands opposite the black corner and the white corner diagonally across from it, at the far right. The fighters walk out from these corners at the opening bell in the screenplay's [first scene](../../screenplay/kickboxing.md#s01-b01), and every later scene keeps them.

### The four sides {#sides}

The ring's sides are named as the hard camera sees them: the near side, between the black corner and the near neutral corner; the far side, between the far neutral corner and the white corner; the left side, between the black corner and the far neutral corner; and the right side, between the near neutral corner and the white corner. Each side has its ropes and its apron, so a scene places a fighter on the right-side ropes, or the operator at the far end of the right-side apron, without naming a corner. The screenplay first uses the names in its [opening beat](../../screenplay/kickboxing.md#s01-b01), which places the black corner on the left of the near side, and every later scene keeps them.

## Use {#use}

### Everyone stays inside the ropes {#inside}

The fighters and the referee move on the one level of the canvas inside the ropes, and nobody enters or leaves the ring until the bell ends the match. Then only the [ring doctor](../cast/ring-doctor.md#entrance) climbs in, through the ropes at a neutral corner.

## Light {#light}

### A lit square in the dark {#lit-square}

Hard, even light from overhead lights the canvas, the ropes and the three men inside them, with soft fill from the front so gloves, shins and feet stand clear of their own shadows. Beyond the apron the arena falls away into darkness. The light never changes during the film.
