# Ring sound

What the film is heard as: the ring's own sounds over the crowd, with no commentary, music or designed effects ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#sound)). Each beat's sound belongs to the screenplay. These are the sounds the scene can produce, and how they sit together.

## Palette {#palette-facet}

### Only what the ring makes {#palette}

The film's sounds are the commission's, as ring microphones hear them ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#what-is-heard)):

- **The bells:** the [bell](../props/bell.md#strikes), at the start and at the end of the match.
- **The referee:** his English words, the only speech ([referee](../cast/referee.md#words)).
- **Breath:**
  - the fighters' exhales on each strike, through closed teeth;
  - their breathing between exchanges, louder as the round goes on;
  - a forced breath when a body kick lands ([Benlloch](../cast/benlloch.md#voice), [Visser](../cast/visser.md#voice)).
- **Mat friction:**
  - the bare feet's scuff and squeak on the canvas, and the scrape of a support foot as it pivots;
  - the officials' shoes, the referee's and the doctor's, on the canvas and on the steps, quieter and harder than the bare feet;
  - the thud of a body on the canvas at the fall;
  - the slide of a glove on the canvas after it.
- **The ropes:** their creak and give when a man leans or is driven onto them, and when the doctor climbs through them from the steps.
- **The contacts:** each its own sound, as the [contacts](#contacts) below set out.
- **The crowd:** wordless, underneath everything ([crowd](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#in-sound)).

Nothing outside this palette is heard; what is left out is listed once in the [work record](../world/work.md#left-out).

## Contacts {#contacts-facet}

### Each contact its own sound {#contacts}

Every contact sounds of what it is, so the ear can tell what landed and on what ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#sound), [coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#translation)):

| Contact | How it sounds |
| --- | --- |
| Gloves touching | Soft leather on leather, light |
| A punch on a glove, forearm or shoulder | A flat, padded smack |
| A punch landing on the body | A heavier, duller thud with the breath forced out |
| A punch glancing on the head | A short, glancing slap |
| Shin meeting shin in a check | A hard, bony knock |
| A low kick on the thigh | A deep, heavy slap |
| A body kick landing on the ribs | A loud, cracking slap, with the breath forced out |
| A body kick taken on a covering arm or elbow | A heavy, flatter smack, duller than the landed kick, with no forced breath |
| A knee to the body | A short, deep thud |
| The finishing kick on the side of the head and neck | The loudest single contact of the film, sharp and heavy |

A contact is heard exactly where it lands, never before. A blocked strike sounds blocked, not landed. No contact is sweetened, slowed or followed by silence for effect ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#knockout-reaction)).

## Mix {#mix-facet}

### Ring over crowd {#mix}

The ring's sounds always sit over the crowd. The crowd's swells rise between and around the contacts, never over a contact or a word ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#mix)). Each breath has its own small space just after the contact it belongs to.

The crowd's own course through the round, including the count, is the [crowd's record](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#in-sound). Whatever its level, the referee's every number, the bell and Benlloch's breath stay above it.

The sound is heard from where the camera is, at ringside. Near the ropes the contacts and breath are louder and closer; across the ring they are a little further off.
