# The ring's sound

What the film's sound is made of and how it is balanced. Contacts follow the techniques of the [technique study](../../../research/kickboxing/techniques.md); the bell and the referee's words follow the [rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#the-round-and-the-match).

## Palette {#palette}

### Sources {#sources}

Every sound comes from the ring and the hall: the bell, the referee's voice, the fighters' breath, their feet on the canvas, their contacts, and the crowd. Nothing else is heard, as [what the film leaves out](../world/work.md#what-the-film-leaves-out) records.

### Contacts {#contacts}

Each kind of contact has its own sound, so the audience can hear what landed without seeing it: glove on glove in a parry or a catch, glove on forearm in a block, glove on bare ribs or belly, glove glancing off the head, shin on thigh in a low kick, shin on shin in a check, shin on forearm and ribs in a body kick, and the knee into the body in a clinch. The finish, instep and shin on the side of the head, sounds unlike any contact before it, and the fall ends in the dull weight of a body on canvas.

### Breath and feet {#breath-and-feet}

Each man's breath marks his effort: exhales with strikes, a breath caught when a body blow lands, breathing that grows heavier through the round. Bare feet squeak on pivots and hush on steps across the canvas.

## Balance {#balance}

### What the ear follows {#priorities}

The listener stands at ringside with the camera. In exchanges the contacts and breath are in front and the crowd behind them. The referee's words are always clear above the crowd, including through the roar of the count. The bell cuts through everything.
