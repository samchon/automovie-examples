# Ring sound

## Palette {#palette}

### A broadcast mix without commentary {#mix}

The film sounds like a live broadcast with only the ring's own sound left in it, as the [fight sound](../../../research/sound/fight-sound.md#law) law sets it and within what the [work record](../world/work.md#left-out) leaves out.

### The bell {#bell}

The timekeeper's bell, heard and never seen: a clear ring that opens the round, and the same ring, struck again, that ends the match after the knockout, under [the knockout](../world/contest.md#knockout).

### The referee's voice {#referee-voice}

The [referee](../cast/referee.md#english) is heard close and clear above everything else, as if miked, so each command and each number of the count lands.

### Contact {#contact}

Each kind of contact has its own sound, heard closer than ringside and louder in close framings:

- glove on glove or forearm, a parry or a cover: a flat slap;
- glove landing on the head: a sharp snap, softer when it glances;
- glove landing on the body: a deep thud with an emptying breath behind it;
- shin landing on the ribs, the body kick that teaches the habit: a heavy, hollow whack with a grunt behind it;
- shin blocked on the forearm and elbow: a hard double knock, arm then ribs, duller than the landed kick, so the audience hears the arm take it;
- shin on shin, a check: a hard knock of bone through skin;
- shin on thigh, a landed low kick: a heavy smack;
- knee driven into the stomach: a short, deep thump and a forced breath;
- sole on forearm or stomach, the teep: a flat push of sound;
- the finishing kick: a single heavy crack, unlike anything before it;
- a body on the canvas: a dull thump.

### Feet and breath {#feet-and-breath}

Bare feet scuff and squeak on the canvas through all the footwork. Both fighters breathe audibly, with a sharp exhale on each strike and a grunt when hit in the body; their breathing grows heavier as the round goes on.

### The crowd and the corners {#crowd-bed}

The [arena crowd](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#knowing) is a bed under everything, swelling and falling with the action, with the corners' shouts carrying over it.
