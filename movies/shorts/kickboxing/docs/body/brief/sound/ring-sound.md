# The ring's sound

What the film is heard as: the ring's own sounds and its crowd, with nothing added ([commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#sound)). The palette's sources and targets are in the [sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#palette). Voices belong to their people: the [referee's words](../cast/referee.md#commands), the fighters' breath in [Vos's](../cast/vos.md#breath) and [Roux's](../cast/roux.md#breath) records, and the [crowd's voice](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#voice).

## Perspective {#perspective}

### Heard from the camera {#from-the-camera}

The film is heard from where the camera is at each moment: what is near it is louder and what is far is softer, so a contact on the far side of the ring is quieter than one beside the ropes in front of it ([sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#palette)). Every sound comes from something in the ring or the hall; nothing is heard that no one there could make.

### What is never heard {#excluded}

The work's exclusions of commentary, music and every voice but the referee's hold as the [work record](../world/work.md#left-out) states them. Beyond them the sound adds no stylised effect, such as the animal sounds some fight films lay over blows, because every sound must be one the scene could produce ([sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#palette)).

## The palette {#palette}

### The bell {#bell}

The bell's single strike opens the round and its rapid run ends the match at 170.1; nothing else rings ([bell](../props/bell.md)).

### Contacts {#contacts}

Each contact belongs to one of these classes, and no two classes share one sound, so the audience can hear what met what ([sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#contacts)):

- glove on glove, the touch, a parry or a punch blocked on the gloves: short, light and paired, the lightest contact;
- glove on head, a punch landing on the head, glancing or clean: sharper and shorter than glove on body;
- glove on body: duller and heavier, often with the struck man's breath forced out at the same instant;
- shin on body, a round kick landing on the ribs: a thick, heavy slap, heavier than any glove contact, with the struck man's breath forced out;
- shin on arm, a kick blocked on the arm, and shin on shin, a check: hard and sharp, bone through skin;
- shin on leg, a low kick landing on the thigh: a heavy slap, lower than shin on arm;
- knee to the body in the clinch: heavy and short, harder than glove on body;
- instep and shin on the side of the head and neck, the finish: the heaviest single contact of the film, with no glove in it, followed at once by the fall and not by a voice.

A feint makes no contact sound; a missed strike is heard only as the striker's exhale and his feet.

### Canvas and ropes {#canvas}

Bare feet scuff, step and pivot on the canvas through every exchange, with the weight changes in them; this is the commission's mat friction ([sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#ring)). A man driven onto the ropes makes them strain, soft and low, because they are padded. Roux's fall is heard as its own sequence of canvas contacts from his hip and shoulder to his head, the heaviest a low thump with the give of the ring floor in it ([Roux](../cast/roux.md#the-fall)).

### The room {#room}

Under everything runs the crowd's wordless bed, rising and settling with the action and held below the referee through the count, so that every word he says is heard clearly ([crowd](../crowds/arena-crowd.md#reactions)).
