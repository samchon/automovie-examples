# docs/research/officiating/referee.md

How the referee of a professional kickboxing bout conducts it, as the film's always-active adult male referee must: his positioning, clinch breaks, words and signals, gaze and posture, the knockdown procedure, the knockout signal, his care for the fallen man and his conduct toward the winner ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#referee)).

GLORY's own rules govern wherever they speak. Where they are silent, the study uses official officiating documents:

- the ABC boxing referee manual;
- the ABC professional kickboxing rules;
- Ontario's professional kickboxing regulation;
- the WAKO referee rules and WAKO rules.

Each translation from boxing, or from amateur kickboxing, is stated. The rules themselves are in [kickboxing/rules.md](../kickboxing/rules.md).

Each observation carries its kind:

- **[rule]**: reported rule or manual text, with its locator.
- **[obs]**: a direct observation of a retained original, such as a rendered page.
- **[meas]**: a reported measurement from a study.
- **[inf]**: this study's inference, with the reasoning stated.
- **[choice]**: the production's own choice, made or proposed here.

**The bridge from boxing to kickboxing.**

- **[rule]** The ABC's kickboxing rules use the boxing knockdown procedure: "the referee shall send the standing fighter to a neutral corner and administer a Mandatory Eight (8) Count" (K 5). Their knockdown, the downed-fighter definition and the "no saving by the bell" rule match the boxing manual (K 5, 8, 11.F; A pp. 5–7).
- **[inf]** The ABC manual's break, positioning and kneeling-count mechanics transfer to kickboxing, with these exceptions where GLORY differs: the ABC referee picks the count up from a knockdown timekeeper (A p. 5), while in GLORY the referee alone counts and starts only once the opponent is in the neutral corner (G 5.05.B.2, Appendix 3.D; see the [rules study](../kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown)); the ABC kickboxing rules have no standing eight count (K 7), while GLORY has one (G 5.05.B.1.b). The manual's "BOX" command becomes GLORY's "fight" (G 5.05.A.1). Its "low blow" beltline becomes the groin foul (G 5.07.A.3). The longer reach of kicks argues for a longer open-phase distance than in boxing, which is entered under the [targets](#targets) as a production choice.

## The officials present {#officials}

- **[rule]** "Matches are regulated by the Referee who stays inside the Ring for the duration of the Match … The Referee is exclusively responsible for making decisions during the Match" (G 5.01.A, p. 8).
- **[rule]** Also present (G 5.01, pp. 8–9):
  - three to five Judges;
  - Ring Inspectors with each corner;
  - the Judge Supervisor;
  - a Ring Doctor, who "must always be present at the Ring";
  - the Timekeeper, with the gong;
  - the Scorekeeper;
  - the Ring Announcer.
- **[rule]** Ontario seats the ringside medical practitioner "in close proximity of the neutral steps", with a paramedic beside him, and the timekeeper on a side free of judges (O 3.1.2–3.1.5). "No persons other than the contestants, the referee and the ringside medical practitioner … may be in the ring during the progress of a round" (O 4.1).
- **[rule]** Cornermen remain seated and may not enter the Ring during the Match (G 5.03.B, p. 10). They may not approach their Athlete during the Down count (G 5.05.B.2).
- **[unavailable]** Crowd conduct is not covered by these sources.

## Attire {#attire}

- **[unavailable]** GLORY's document does not describe referee attire, and no footage was sampled.
- **[rule]** The ABC prescribes a grey or blue shirt, a black or blue bow tie, dark blue or black trousers, boxer-type shoes and optional plastic gloves (A pp. 1–2). WAKO prescribes a navy-blue jacket, a white short-sleeved shirt, a WAKO bow tie for central referees, black trousers and black laceless gym shoes, and bars a central referee from wearing glasses (R Art. 14, p. 22). A WAKO ring referee "must use medical gloves" (R Art. 3.4.2, p. 8).
- **[obs]** The WAKO plates show the referee in a white short-sleeved shirt, a black bow tie, black trousers and black shoes, wearing white medical gloves (R pp. 47–48).
- **[choice]** The production picks within these.

## Positioning and movement {#positioning}

- **[rule]** "Be positioned in the CENTER of the ring before the bell rings to commence each round and maintain control to keep boxers in their corners until the bell sounds" (A p. 4).
- **[rule]** "The Referee begins or resumes the Match with the command 'fight' only once the Athletes are separated an equal distance from the Referee" (G 5.05.A.1).
- **[rule]** "To best observe the boxers', referees should position themselves at appropriate distances, while maintaining a side view, perpendicular to the boxers" (A p. 4).
- **[rule]** "The referee should move around the ring in a smooth and graceful manner. In general, the referee should maintain a distance that allows for a clear view of the action and the ability to move quickly if needed" (A p. 4).
- **[rule]** The referee shortens the distance when (A pp. 4–5):
  - the fighters move toward or fight on the ropes;
  - "the fighting ability of one of the boxers is in doubt and a stoppage may be imminent";
  - they are "mainly resorting to infighting, holding, or clinching";
  - noise drowns commands;
  - the ten-second warning sounds.
- **[rule]** "In order not to disturb a close fight, the Central referee should not stand in the way or step in too early" (R Art. 3.4.4, p. 9).
- **[inf]** For the film's round:
  - In open exchanges at kicking range, he stands off the fighters' line on the side away from the camera's axis, at a side-on angle to both.
  - As the fight goes to the ropes or a corner, he closes in and stays on the open side, so that neither he nor the ropes trap the fighters.
  - In a clinch he closes to arm's reach.
  - Through the last phases before 150 s he tightens his distance, as A's pattern builds and B tires: "a stoppage may be imminent" (A p. 4).

## The clinch break {#clinch-break}

- **[rule]** GLORY's break: "The command 'break' is used by the Referee to interrupt an action of the Athletes and to separate the Athletes after which they must take a step backwards. The Referee steps in between both Athletes. As soon as he steps back the Athletes resume the fight without the instruction 'fight.'" (G 5.05.A.2, p. 12)
- **[rule]** ABC: "When physically breaking the boxers, it is recommended to use the word 'Stop' as your first command, followed by 'Break' … The referee should never walk through the boxers after a break. Be sure to break at a full arm's length, addressing both boxers, and step straight back after the break to resume your normal distance." (A p. 5) "Never walk between boxers after breaking a clinch." (A p. 15)
- **[rule]** WAKO: "BREAK - this command is used by the Central referee to break a body to body position, after which each kickboxer must make one step back" (R Art. 3.4.4, p. 8). With a deaf fighter, "the Central referee can touch the shoulders or arm of the fighter with their hand to 'stop' or 'break'" (R p. 9).
- **[rule]** The clinch itself is legal only "to immediately attack with a single legal knee strike; after the legal knee strike the Athlete must disengage the clinch immediately" (G 5.07.A.11, p. 17).

**[inf]** The break has five steps:

1. He steps in, saying "Break!" (or "Stop! Break!").
2. He places his open hands on both men at full arm's length, on the near shoulder or upper chest of each. This is how a break is made "physically" (A p. 5).
3. Both men step back.
4. The referee steps straight back, out from between them, never walking through.
5. The action resumes without "Fight".

A clean GLORY clinch, with a grip, one knee and a release, needs no command. The referee's readable act is to close in and hover a hand ready.

## Gaze and posture {#gaze-and-posture}

- **[rule]** "Remember, it's all about Concentration and Positioning" (A p. 4).
- **[rule]** When a fighter is hurt, the referee watches: "Are the boxer's hands up? Is their head snapping back from the punches? How are their legs (wobbly-sturdy)?" (A p. 7).
- **[rule]** During the count he must "keep an eye on the standing boxer to assure they stay in the neutral corner" (A p. 5). He "should ensure that the fallen boxer can clearly see and hear their count … when necessary, should kneel to the fallen boxer so he can hear and see the referee" (A pp. 5–6).

**[inf]** The production's representation gives the referee no face ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#referee)), so his attention reads from the turn of his head and trunk:

- **Live action:** his head tracks the midpoint of the two men, and his chest stays side-on to their line.
- **The knockdown:** head and trunk snap to B and then to A as he sends A away.
- **The count:** his head is down to B for each number, with glances up to A in the corner.
- **After the knockout:** his trunk and head lower over B, and stay turned away from A's celebration.
- **Posture:** stance light and knees flexed while he moves; low over B during the count.

**Care of the fallen man.**

- **[rule]** "If the kickboxer is unconscious or semi-conscious after the knock down on the floor, the Central referee, after calling the doctor, will keep the kickboxer lying on the floor and will not allow him to get up until the arrival of the official doctor. The Central referee will not otherwise intervene (remove the tooth guard or rotate the unconscious kickboxer to the lateral position). All interventions on the kickboxer will be carried out by an official doctor." (W Ch. 7 Art. 9.1, pp. 135–136)
- **[rule]** "If a Kickboxer remains unconscious, only the Central referee and the doctor in charge will be allowed to stay in the ring" (W p. 135).
- **[inf]** WAKO's rule is written for an unconscious or semi-conscious fighter. B is conscious but unable to rise ([rules study](../kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown)). The film applies the same conduct to him: after the knockout he has lost, and keeping a man who could not rise within ten lying still until the doctor sees him is the cautious reading of the rule's purpose, while rolling or lifting him would still be the doctor's work. This translation is the production's.
- **[rule]** During the count, "The referee should not assist the downed fighter in any way" (A p. 6).
- **[rule]** GLORY lets "Only the Ring Doctor" enter during an examination (G 5.01.E.4) and authorises him to act "during, and after Matches" (G 5.01.E.2).
- **[inf]** The ABC's mouthpiece mechanics (A p. 8) concern a fighter who rises and continues. Neither the ABC nor Ontario has the referee remove a knocked-out fighter's mouthpiece. **The sources do not support the referee removing B's mouthpiece. WAKO forbids it.**
- **[choice]** His support is visible care instead:
  - kneeling at B's head and shoulder, facing him;
  - one hand resting on B's shoulder or upper arm to keep him still;
  - his voice low to B ("Stay down." is a production choice, not rule text);
  - turning to guide the doctor in.

**The doctor's route.** **[inf]** The doctor sits by the neutral steps (O 3.1.2), so he enters through a neutral corner.

- **[choice]** The doctor enters after the bell, between 170.1 and 180 s. The commission's final wide holds "the referee attending B" ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#ending)).

## The winner {#winner}

- **[rule]** Raising the winner's hand appears in none of the sources:
  - GLORY names the Judge Supervisor and Ring Announcer as those who declare the result (G 5.01.D, 5.01.H).
  - The ABC referee should "Help the ring announcer declare the fight result accurately" (A p. 15).
  - The WAKO referee "announce[s] the winner … following command of Chief referee" (R Art. 3.4.3, p. 8).
- **[unavailable]** No source gives the referee a part in the winner's celebration. Raising the hand is not covered by the retained sources.
- **[rule]** The opponent waits in the corner "until the Referee gives him permission to leave this corner and resume the Match" (G 5.05.B.2).
- **[inf]** That permission concerns resuming the Match. Once the knockout signal has ended the Match at 167.6, A's leaving the corner to celebrate at 168.1 breaks no rule.
- **[choice]** The referee does not attend A before 180 s. His duty to the hurt man comes first (A p. 7), and the announcement falls after the film.

## Markers of a professional referee {#level-markers}

**[rule]** The ABC lists what separates competent mechanics (A pp. 3–7, 14–15). A professional referee:

- is in the centre before each bell;
- keeps a side view;
- adjusts his distance to the situation;
- breaks at full arm's length and steps straight back;
- never walks through the boxers;
- counts "in a clear and straightforward manner" with fingers;
- keeps an eye on the standing fighter;
- kneels so the fallen fighter sees and hears him;
- does not assist the downed fighter;
- stays with a hurt fighter until the doctor arrives;
- does not coach;
- uses no attention-seeking phrases.

**[inf]** A referee who stands square between the fighters, stays still while they circle, walks through after a break, counts without fingers or with his back to the standing fighter, or lifts the fallen man reads as a beginner.

**Actions the referee must not take.**

- **[rule]** Walking through the fighters (A p. 5).
- **[rule]** Assisting the downed fighter during the count (A p. 6).
- **[rule]** Stepping in too early (R p. 9).
- **[rule]** Removing an unconscious fighter's mouthguard or rolling him (W Art. 9.1).
- **[rule]** Coaching (A p. 15).
- **[rule]** Letting cornermen approach during the count (G 5.05.B.2).
