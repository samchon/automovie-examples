# The referee and the officials of a kickboxing ring

The conduct of the referee and of the officials and corners around the ring, for a brief and screenplay that need a referee who is always active and readable and a ringside that behaves correctly while it is seen or heard. Rules are in the [rules study](../kickboxing/rules.md); sources and locators are in the [research index](../index.md).

Observation kinds: *rule*, *cross-check*, *inference*, *choice*, as in the rules study.

## The referee's place and movement {#position}

- The referee stays inside the ring for the whole match (GLORY 5.01 A). *rule*
- His first responsibility is the fighters' safety; he must not stand in the way or step in too early in a close fight, and signals violations with gestures (WAKO Referee Rules 3.4.2 and 3.4.4). *cross-check*
- He starts or resumes the fight only when the fighters stand an equal distance from him, and on "break" he steps between them and steps back to let them resume (GLORY 5.05 A1 and A2). *rule*
- To see both fighters, their feet and their hands without blocking the fight, he works from the side of their line, moving as they move, closing in when they reach the ropes or clinch. *inference* from the rules above; no retained source measures referee distance.

## Words {#words}

- WAKO requires referees at international events to speak English and to give all commands in English (WAKO Referee Rules article 1 and 3.4.2). *cross-check*
- GLORY's commands are "fight", "break" and "stop"; a down is announced by a "down" call with a gesture (GLORY 5.05 A and B2). WAKO adds "shake hands" for the glove touch, "stop time" and "time" (WAKO Referee Rules 3.4.4). *rule*, *cross-check*
- In a down count he counts one to ten aloud at one-second intervals (WAKO Rules chapter 7, article 9). *cross-check*

## Signals {#signals}

WAKO's ring hand signals, as photographed (WAKO Referee Rules appendix 11.4, pages 47 and 48): *cross-check*

| Signal | Form |
| --- | --- |
| Fight / stop | One arm extended forward and lowered between the fighters |
| Stop time | Hands formed into a T |
| Official warning | One fist raised before the chest (GLORY's own: right arm up, pointer finger extended, 5.08 A2) |
| No holding | Both fists held together at the chest |
| No pushing | Both open palms pushed forward |
| No attack to the back of the head | Hand placed on the back of the own head |
| Knockout or technical knockout | Both forearms crossed above the head |
| Count | Each second shown with the fingers, held towards the fallen fighter (WAKO Rules chapter 7, article 9) |

## The knockdown procedure {#knockdown}

1. Call "down" with a gesture (GLORY 5.05 B2). *rule*
2. Send the opponent to the farthest neutral corner, pointing to it; the opponent stays there until released, and the count stops if he leaves (GLORY 5.05 B2; WAKO Rules chapter 7, article 9). *rule*
3. Count from one, a number a second, showing each with the fingers to the downed fighter (WAKO Rules chapter 7, article 9). *cross-check*
4. Count at least to eight, then judge whether the fighter, standing in fighting position with raised hands, stable and clear-eyed, can go on; if so, "fight" (GLORY 5.05 B3; WAKO chapter 7, article 9). *rule*
5. If he is not ready by ten, the fight ends by knockout, signalled with the crossed forearms, and the timekeeper sounds the bell (GLORY 5.05 B5; WAKO appendix 11.4). *rule*

## After a knockout {#after-knockout}

- If the fighter is unconscious or semi-conscious, the referee calls the doctor and keeps the fighter lying on the floor, not letting him get up until the doctor arrives; he does not remove the mouthguard or turn the fighter onto his side, because all intervention on the fighter belongs to the doctor (WAKO Rules chapter 7, article 9.1). *cross-check*
- While the fighter remains unconscious, only the referee and the doctor stay in the ring unless the doctor needs help (WAKO Rules chapter 7, article 9.1). *cross-check*
- A fighter knocked out by a head strike is examined by a doctor immediately (WAKO chapter 7, article 9.1); GLORY requires a clinical assessment after any knockout (GLORY 2.03 D). *rule*

## The referee's appearance {#appearance}

- WAKO's central referee wears a white short-sleeved shirt with a dark bow tie, black trousers, black shoes and a belt (WAKO Referee Rules appendix 7.1, photographs page 40), and in ring sports medical gloves (3.4.2). *cross-check*

## The other officials and the corners {#ringside}

- The ring doctor is present at the ring throughout and is the only person who may enter during an examination (GLORY 5.01 E1 and E4). *rule*
- The timekeeper sounds the gong or bell for the start and end of each round and for the end of the match after a knockout (GLORY 5.01 F; 5.05 B5). *rule*
- Judges sit at ringside and score; the ring announcer announces the match, warnings and scores (GLORY 5.01 B and H). *rule*
- Cornermen sit in their designated seats during the match, may not enter the ring or pound the apron, may not approach their athlete during a count and coach without excess (GLORY 5.03 B; 5.05 B2). *rule*

## What stays unavailable

- GLORY publishes no referee manual with positioning, signals or count procedure; those above come from WAKO's ring rules and are adopted as this fictional contest's practice. *choice*
- No retained source measures how far from the fighters a referee stands or how fast he moves.
