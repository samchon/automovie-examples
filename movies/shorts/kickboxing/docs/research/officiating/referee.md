# Referee mechanics

This study records how a professional referee positions himself, closes in, breaks a clinch, signals and runs a knockdown, so the screenplay and code never leave him idle or invent his conduct. GLORY's rules (`GLORY`) give the commands and the count; the boxing referee manuals of the Association of Boxing Commissions (`ABC-2022`, `ABC-2025`, `ABC-2011`) and the World Boxing Association (`WBA`) give the mechanics GLORY leaves out. Each observation carries its kind as the [index](../index.md#observation-kinds) defines it. Where boxing practice is carried into this kickboxing bout, the study says so.

## Positioning {#positioning}

- [reported] Referees "maintain their position as far away from the boxers as possible", "utilize the perimeter of the ring and continue to move in a graceful manner", and "should not bounce or give the appearance by their actions that they are also boxing" (`ABC-2022` pp. 1–2).
- [reported] "Try to maintain the pace of the circling boxers, maintaining a side view of both boxers" (`ABC-2022` p. 2); the current manual: "maintaining a side view, perpendicular to the boxers", at "a distance that allows for a clear view of the action and the ability to move quickly if needed" (`ABC-2025` p. 4).
- [reported] "Keep a window between boxers. Position is important. Never get caught behind the boxers or the action. Try to close the gap when a boxer is on the ropes" (`ABC-2022` p. 5). "Basically, the Referee should move in a direction opposite to that of the boxers. It is said that the greater distance affords a better view of the boxers. However, the Referee should stay at a reasonable distance to the boxers so that he will be at hand should a dangerous situation arise" (`WBA`, Movement and Positioning); "The principal requirement, however, is that the Referee should be facing the inner or dominant side of the boxers and not be moving behind them"; "The Referee should not place himself in a disadvantage by trying to remain on the open side when close to the corners of the ring. Doing so could cause erratic movement or positioning and bout interference" (`WBA`, The 'Open or Dominant Side').
- [reported] He shortens his distance when a fighter moves toward or fights on the ropes, in infighting, holding or clinching, and when "a stoppage may be imminent" (`ABC-2022` p. 6, `ABC-2025` pp. 4–5), while staying alert "so as not to interfere with or be trapped between the ropes and the boxers" (`ABC-2022` p. 5).
- [reported] His movements "should be neutral, easy and calm"; "eccentric waving or swinging of the arms, bending at the waist, clapping, touching or holding the ropes" are to be avoided (`WBA`). No manual describes a fixed standing posture.
- [reported] The fastest a man in his twenties walks is 253.3 cm/s on average over 7.62 m (`BOHANNON`, abstract); the comfortable speed for that decade was not readable.

Law [inference from the passages above]: the referee stands across the fighters' line from the midpoint between them, on the side he already holds, so he sees both men side-on and neither fighter's back. He stays as far out as the ring allows while still keeping pace. The study reads the WBA's "move in a direction opposite to that of the boxers" by the sentence that follows it, "It is said that the greater distance affords a better view of the boxers", and by the one after, that he stay "at hand": read this way, "opposite" means keeping his distance, so when the pair come toward his side he gives ground away from them, and when they travel along the ring he moves with them. The same manual ranks its "principal requirement" above that, to face the boxers' inner side and "not be moving behind them", so the study does not read "opposite" as walking round against a turning line: when their line turns, as when one fighter circles the other, he walks round the same way as the line turns, staying across it. When holding his side would trap him near a corner, as the manual warns, or against the ropes, he gives it up and goes around behind the action to the other side, passing behind it only in transit, never between the fighters or between a fighter and the ropes. On the ropes, in a clinch and when a fighter is hurt, he closes in; to break a clinch he steps between the men for that moment only. He walks; he does not bounce, run or wave his arms.

- [choice] In this film he works the side of the fighters' line away from the hard camera, standing toward one end of it so that he keeps off the line of the camera in use. When the line's turn would carry him onto the hard camera's side, he stays across the line through any live exchange and passes behind the action back to his side once no exchange is live. When the ropes or a corner leave no room on his side, he gives it up as the law above gives, going round behind the action to the other side, where there is room, in transit and while no exchange is live; there he stands as he would on his own side, across the line and off the line of the camera in use. Once the fighters are back in the open ring, he returns to his working side, walking round with the line if it turns that way, or passing behind the action again while no exchange is live.

## Breaking a clinch {#clinch-break}

- [reported] "Use the word 'Stop' as your first command, followed by 'Break'"; "break at a full arm's length", "never walk through the boxers after a break", "take a full step straight back" (`ABC-2025` p. 5); "this should be done at an angle (between the boxers) NOT from behind" (`ABC-2022` p. 8); "avoid touching the boxers when separating them" (`WBA`).
- [reported] Under GLORY, "Break" makes both athletes step back, the referee steps between them, and the fight resumes when he steps back, without "fight" (`GLORY` §5.05.A.2).

Law [inference]: if a clinch holds after its one knee, the referee closes from the side at an angle, calls "Break" with an arm reaching between the two men at arm's length, steps between them as they step back, then takes one step straight back out to his side of the line.

## Commands and signals {#commands}

- [reported] GLORY's commands are "Fight", "Break" and "Stop" (`GLORY` §5.05.A), and a knockdown is signalled "by the Down call and gesture" (§5.05.B.2). The ABC's equivalent of "Fight" is "Box" (`ABC-2022` p. 5).
- [reported] "The referee will command 'Down!' and signal by pointing to the canvas" (`ABC-2011` PDF p. 6, §15). The standing boxer is motioned to the corner: "Motion the scoring boxer to the furthest neutral corner" (`ABC-2011` PDF p. 16); "by pointing to that corner" (`FAC` (2)(a)1.b).
- [reported] The glove touch: "SHAKE HANDS - this command is used by the Central referee at the beginning of the fight to give an order to kickboxers to touch the gloves and show respect to each other"; "FIGHT" starts the fight; the referee "must give all commands in ENGLISH" (`WAKO-REFEREE` Art. 3.4.2, 3.4.4, p. 8). GLORY's rules have no glove-touch clause; boxing codes allow a touch of gloves only before the bout begins (`EB-2020` 11.1.6–11.1.12, p. 42; `ONTARIO-KB` s. 6).
- [choice] The referee speaks only the ring's commands and the count: "Shake hands!" for the glove touch, "Fight!", "Break!", "Stop!", "Down!", the numbers and, at the knockdown, "Neutral corner!" as he points, a spoken direction no manual scripts but none forbids, chosen so the audience hears where the winner is sent.

## Knockdown {#knockdown}

- [reported] "Direct the standing boxer to the farthest Neutral Corner", then "keep an eye on the standing boxer to assure they stay in the neutral corner. If boxer comes out of the neutral corner, STOP THE COUNT" (`ABC-2025` p. 5). "The referee must position himself so he can see that the other boxer stays in this neutral corner" (`ABC-2022` p. 15).
- [reported] "Position yourself so that you can focus on the downed boxer, the other boxer, and the Timekeeper"; for the evaluation "Be at about one arm's length from him. Don't touch the boxer" (`ABC-2011` PDF p. 16), which the WBA manual gives as "When evaluating the fighter do not touch him. Be at about one arm's length from him" (`WBA`). He "should kneel to the fallen boxer so he can hear and see the referee" when necessary (`ABC-2025` p. 6).
- [reported] The count is shown with the fingers: "always use hand signals (fingers) during their count" (`ABC-2025` p. 6); "Use both hands when counting six through ten. Position your hands in line with the boxer's eyes. Do not fan, wave, etc. your hands" (`ABC-2011` PDF p. 16, `WBA`). Florida pairs each second with "a downward motion of his arm" (`FAC`).
- [reported] GLORY's count procedure is in the [rules study](../kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown).

Law [inference, with the representation's limit as a choice]: at the knockdown the referee calls "Down!" pointing at the canvas, sends the standing fighter to the farther neutral corner with his arm, and moves to stand a step from the fallen man on the side that keeps the corner in front of him. He waits until the standing fighter faces the centre, then counts aloud, one number a second, each number marked by his counting hand dropping toward the fallen man's eyes, and both hands from six on. The polygon man's hands carry no articulated fingers, so the number is carried by the voice and the arm, not by raised fingers.

## After the knockout {#after-knockout}

- [reported] At a knockout "the referee shall wave their arms above their head to signal the bout is over" (`ABC-2025` p. 6). The referee stays with the hurt fighter until the physician arrives (`ABC-2025` p. 7; `WBA`).
- [reported] The referee keeps an unconscious kickboxer "lying on the floor" until the doctor arrives and does not "remove the tooth guard or rotate the unconscious kickboxer" (`WAKO-RULES` Ch. 7 Art. 9.1, pp. 135–136).
- [choice] At ten the referee waves both arms crossing over his head, then kneels beside the fallen fighter and stays with him, one hand on his shoulder keeping him lying still, watching him, while the winner celebrates. The ring doctor's entry is in the [knockout study](../kickboxing/knockout.md#aftermath).

## Measurable targets {#targets}

Each target is the production's reading of the manuals above [choice], measured on the composed film:

- He holds a side view: for at least 90% of the time the fighters are exchanging, the angle between his direction from their midpoint and their line is at least 60°.
- He never stands on the segment between the two fighters, except for the moment he steps between them to break a clinch, and never between a fighter and the ropes behind that fighter.
- He walks at no more than 2.5 m/s, below the 253.3 cm/s maximum of men in their twenties (`BOHANNON`), and never shows a flight phase.
- He keeps moving: at least 1 m of walking in every fifteen-second phase before the knockdown.
- From the knockdown to the result his feet stay at least one of his steps from the lying body, and the standing fighter stays within 60° of his facing while he counts.
