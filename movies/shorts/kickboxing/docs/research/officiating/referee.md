# docs/research/officiating/referee.md: the championship kickboxing referee

This study records how a championship kickboxing referee works a round: where he stands and how he moves, how he breaks a clinch, what he says and signals, how he runs a knockdown count to a knockout, how he cares for a knocked-out fighter, and what marks an experienced referee. It serves the brief's referee record. The rules he enforces are in the [rules study](../kickboxing/rules.md). Moment-to-moment staging across the round belongs to the screenplay (see [scope](#scope)).

GLORY publishes no referee manual among the originals retrieved. It gives the referee's commands (GLORY 5.05.A, p. 12), the knockdown procedure (5.05.B, pp. 12–13) and two foul gestures (5.08.A, p. 18). For mechanics this study relies on the following, each with its translation:

- The ABC referee manuals are written for professional boxing. They transfer to kickboxing because the ring, the neutral corners, the count and the referee's place inside the ropes are the same. One difference matters: kicks and knees reach farther and lower than punches, so the referee's "prudent distance" must also keep him clear of round kicks and of a man's kicking leg [inference].
- WAKO's referee rules cover amateur ring kickboxing. Its commands and photographed hand signals carry over. Its count starts immediately after the fall, which GLORY does not follow (see [the knockdown](#knockdown-procedure)) [observation].
- Ontario's rules cover professional kickboxing ("boxing with hands and feet"). Its knockdown gestures carry over. Its knockdown timekeeper does not, because GLORY has none (rules study, [anchors](../kickboxing/rules.md#anchors)) [observation].

Two further manuals with positioning guidance could not be retrieved. Both are listed as unavailable under [scope](#scope).

## Qualification, language and attire {#attire}

- WAKO requires an official age of at least 21 for a central referee at title tournaments (WAKO-REF art. 1, p. 5) [measurement]. Its world and continental championships use only A or B grade referees, who have attended at least two international championships and a referee seminar (WAKO-REF art. 4.1, p. 14) [measurement].
- The referee must give "all commands in ENGLISH" (WAKO-REF 3.4.2, p. 8). International referees must have a good command of spoken and written English (WAKO-REF art. 1, p. 5) [observation]. This production's English-speaking referee fits that [inference].
- Dress: ABC specifies a grey or blue shirt (short or long sleeves) with a black or blue bow tie, dark blue or black trousers, an optional black belt, boxer-type shoes (preferred black) and plastic gloves (ABC-REF25 pp. 1–2; ABC-REF22 p. 2) [observation]. WAKO's ring referee "must use medical gloves" and changes them after any fight with blood (WAKO-REF 3.4.2, p. 8). Its ring-signal photographs show a white short-sleeved shirt, black bow tie, black trousers, dark shoes and pale medical gloves (WAKO-REF App. 11.4, pp. 47–48) [observation of photographs]. GLORY is silent. The brief chooses one of these two uniforms. This study suggests the WAKO pattern (white shirt, black bow tie, black trousers, black shoes, pale medical gloves), which reads against both a white-gloved and a black-gloved fighter [choice].
- He wears no jewellery, has neat hair and nails, and does not chew gum (ABC-REF25 p. 2) [observation].

## Before the first exchange {#before-the-round}

- He checks both men's mouthguards (WAKO-REF 3.4.3, p. 8). He checks the gloves, trunks and cup and keeps each team in its own corner (ABC-REF25 p. 3; ABC-REF22 p. 4) [observation].
- His instructions in the centre are "brief and to the point". He does "not use 'catchy' phrases or say things that will bring attention to [himself]". He reminds both men to obey his commands at all times and to protect themselves at all times (ABC-REF25 p. 4) [observation].
- He is "positioned in the CENTER of the ring before the bell rings ... and maintain[s] control to keep boxers in their corners until the bell sounds" (ABC-REF25 p. 4; ABC-REF22 p. 5) [observation].
- He starts the action with "Fight" once both men stand at an equal distance from him (GLORY 5.05.A.1, p. 12) [observation]. Where a touch of gloves follows "Fight", he steps back off the line between the men as they close (rules study, [round start](../kickboxing/rules.md#round-start)) [choice].

## Positioning {#positioning}

### The side view {#side-view}

"To best observe the boxers', referees should position themselves at appropriate distances, while maintaining a side view, perpendicular to the boxers" (ABC-REF25 p. 4) [observation]. "If possible, the referee should try to maintain the pace of the circling boxers, maintaining a side view of both boxers which allows the referee to observe a greater degree of the offenses and defenses of both" (ABC-REF22 p. 2) [observation].

In geometry: call the line through the two fighters the fighters' line. The referee stands off that line, near the perpendicular through its midpoint, so his line of sight crosses the fighters' line at roughly a right angle. Both men are then in profile and at about the same distance from him [inference]. Two orthodox men facing each other each turn their chest towards their own right. From one side of the line the referee sees one man's chest and the other's back; from the other side he sees the reverse. That is one reason he keeps moving round them instead of holding one spot [inference].

### A window between them, never behind {#window}

- "Keep a window between boxers. Position is important. Never get caught behind the boxers or the action" (ABC-REF22 p. 5) [observation]. The window is the open space between the two men that he sees through from the side [inference].
- "The referee should avoid close contact with the boxers and should maintain their position as far away from the boxers as possible. The referee should utilize the perimeter of the ring" (ABC-REF22 p. 1) [observation].
- "In order not to disturb a close fight, the Central referee should not stand in the way or step in too early" (WAKO-REF 3.4.4, p. 9) [observation].
- Near the ropes, he should be "alert to their exact position so as not to interfere with or be trapped between the ropes and the boxers" (ABC-REF22 p. 5) [observation].

### Distance {#distance}

"There is no set rule" (ABC-REF22 p. 5). "Experience and instinct will give the referee the sense of knowing when to move in closer and when to stay at a prudent distance". He keeps "a distance that allows for a clear view of the action and the ability to move quickly if needed" (ABC-REF25 p. 4) [observation]. He shortens the distance (ABC-REF25 pp. 4–5; ABC-REF22 p. 6) [observation]:

1. when the men move towards the ropes or fight on them;
2. when one man's ability to fight on is in doubt and a stoppage may be near;
3. when they mostly infight, hold or clinch;
4. when the noise is loud enough that his commands may not be heard;
5. at the timekeeper's ten-second warning.

No retrieved source gives the distance in metres. The production's working ranges are under [measurable targets](#targets).

### Movement {#movement}

- "The referee should move around the ring in a smooth manner; they should not bounce or give the appearance by their actions that they are also boxing" (ABC-REF22 p. 2). He moves "in a smooth and graceful manner" (ABC-REF25 p. 4). "They should not interfere with the boxers by over-playing their part" (ABC-REF22 p. 1) [observation].
- He keeps pace with the circling men to hold the side view (ABC-REF22 p. 2) [observation]. When the fighters pivot or circle, he moves on a wider circle around the same centre, so he stays off their line and does not cross between them [inference].
- At the ten-second warning he gets "into a centered position between the boxers" for the bell (ABC-REF25 p. 5) [observation]. This round ends by knockout before that point (rules study, [bells](../kickboxing/rules.md#bells)).

## Clinch breaks {#clinch-break}

The GLORY rule: "The command 'break' is used by the Referee to interrupt an action of the Athletes and to separate the Athletes after which they must take a step backwards. The Referee steps in between both Athletes. As soon as he steps back the Athletes resume the fight without the instruction 'fight'" (GLORY 5.05.A.2, p. 12) [observation]. Under GLORY a hold is lawful only for one immediate knee (GLORY 5.07.A.11). So the referee's cue is a hold that outlasts its single knee, or a hold with no knee at all (rules study, [clinch](../kickboxing/rules.md#clinch)) [inference].

The procedure, combining GLORY with ABC's mechanics:

1. **Close in.** Clinching is one of the cases where he shortens his distance (ABC-REF25 p. 5) [observation].
2. **Command.** "Break!" is the GLORY command (5.05.A.2). ABC recommends "Stop" as the first word, followed by "Break", when breaking physically (ABC-REF25 p. 5). GLORY reserves "Stop" for stopping the fight, at the end of a round or before a caution or warning (5.05.A.3), so this referee says "Break!" alone [observation; choice].
3. **Physical separation, if they do not part at the word.** He breaks "at an angle (between the boxers) NOT from behind". He breaks "at a full arm's length", and he breaks "both boxers, not just one" (ABC-REF22 p. 8; ABC-REF25 p. 5) [observation]. Neither manual says where his hands go. This study puts one open hand on each man's chest or near shoulder and the forearms between them, pressing them apart as he steps in on the angle [choice].
4. **Step out.** "The referee should never walk through the boxers after a break, simply take a full step straight back and assume your normal distance" (ABC-REF22 p. 8). "Never walk between boxers after breaking a clinch" (ABC-REF25 p. 15) [observation]. As he steps back the men resume without "Fight" (GLORY 5.05.A.2) [observation].
5. **Repeat holding.** It draws a caution without stopping the action, or "Stop", the opponent to the farthest neutral corner and an official warning (GLORY 5.08.A.1–2). The "No holding" signal is shown in the photographs (WAKO-REF App. 11.4, p. 48) [observation].

## The knockdown procedure {#knockdown-procedure}

Step by step, under GLORY with ABC's, Ontario's and WAKO's mechanics, placed against the commission's anchors. The seconds are film time.

1. **Recognition (about 150–151 s).** He sees the kick land from his side view, and sees B's body touch the canvas with something other than his feet (GLORY 5.05.B.1.a) [inference]. He calls "Down!" and points to the canvas (GLORY 5.05.B.2; ONT 17) [observation].
2. **Between them, and sending A (about 151–153 s).** He steps in between the two men, "always protect[ing] the injured boxer" (ABC-REF25 p. 7) [observation]. He sends A to the farthest neutral corner with "Neutral corner!" and a pointing arm (GLORY 5.05.B.2) [observation; choice of words]. "DO NOT RUSH!" (ABC-REF22 p. 6) [observation].
3. **Holding the count (about 153–157 s).** He watches A walk. He does not begin "until the opponent is in the neutral corner facing the center of the Ring" (GLORY App. 3 D) [observation]. Meanwhile he moves to B. This is the 5–6 s delay the rules study accounts for ([anchors](../kickboxing/rules.md#anchors)) [inference].
4. **Position for the count (157 s).** He places himself so that "the fallen boxer can clearly see and hear their count" (ABC-REF25 p. 6; ABC-REF22 p. 6). Where necessary he kneels to the fallen man "so he can hear and see the referee" (ABC-REF25 p. 6), or bends down (ABC-REF22 p. 7) [observation]. He must also "keep an eye on the standing boxer" in the corner (ABC-REF25 p. 5; ABC-REF22 p. 6) [observation]. Both can be in his view at once if he stands at B's head on the side away from A's corner and faces B with the corner beyond him. With B lying on his right side, he faces B's face from the front [inference]. Under PKG `KnockdownCount.corner` the corner lies across the ring from B, so this position faces the length of the ring [inference].
5. **The count (158–167 s).** One word a second, "One" to "Ten", "in a loud clear voice", each shown "with a motion of their arm and upraised fingers" (ONT 17; WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9) [observation]. "The referee should always count in a clear and straightforward manner" and "should not just rely on their voice" (ABC-REF25 p. 6) [observation]. His gaze rests on B's eyes, with glances up to A's corner. If A left the corner he would stop counting and send him back (GLORY 5.05.B.2; ABC-REF22 p. 6) [observation]. He "should not assist the downed fighter in any way" during the count (ABC-REF25 p. 6) [observation]. PKG `KnockdownCount.number` already places the words.
6. **Readiness at eight (165 s).** GLORY obliges him to count to eight, then assess (5.05.B.3). At eight he may check "the Athlete's eyes and balance" (5.05.B.6) [observation]. WAKO's proof of readiness is a man "on his feet, in fighting stand with his arms raised in fighting position", "stable and balanced, with clear vision" (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9, p. 135) [observation]. B is still down, so he counts on [inference].
7. **Ten and the wave-off (167.0–167.6 s).** At an uninterrupted ten the man loses by knockout (GLORY 5.05.B.5) [observation]. The knockout signal follows the word "Ten" by 0.6 s [commission]. It is the [knockout signal](#signals) above [choice].
8. **Calling the doctor, and between the men (from about 167.6 s).** "The referee shall rule the bout over and immediately signal for the doctor" (ABC-REF25 p. 6) [observation]. The signal releases A from the corner. Through the count the referee knelt with A beyond B (step 4), which leaves B between them. So as he waves the bout off he moves round B's head to kneel on A's side of B, close at his head, so that his own body is between B and A as A's celebration begins ("always protect the injured boxer", ABC-REF25 p. 7) [choice built on the observation]. The doctor is the only person who may enter during an examination (GLORY 5.01.E.4) [observation].
9. **The bell (170.1 s).** The timekeeper sounds it for the knockout (GLORY 5.05.B.5) [observation]. The referee does not signal for it [inference].

The early stop is a tension. A referee who sees a man "go down hard" with closed eyes or twitching may stop the count at once (ABC-REF25 p. 7; GLORY 5.06.E.2). The full count therefore needs B to read as conscious and failing to rise (rules study, [anchors](../kickboxing/rules.md#anchors), tension 2) [inference].

## Care of a knocked-out fighter {#care}

What the sources tell the referee to do:

- **Signal for the doctor immediately** (ABC-REF25 p. 6) [observation].
- **Stay with him.** "Time is precious. Always stay with a hurt boxer until the doctor enters the ring to attend to the boxer" (ABC-REF25 p. 7) [observation]. "Remember, the referee has this boxer's life in their hands" (ABC-REF25 p. 7; ABC-REF22 p. 6) [observation].
- **Keep him lying still.** "If the kickboxer is unconscious or semi-conscious after the knock down on the floor, the Central referee, after calling the doctor, will keep the kickboxer lying on the floor and will not allow him to get up until the arrival of the official doctor" (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9.1, p. 136) [observation].
- **Keep the ring clear.** Only the referee and the doctor stay in the ring with an unconscious man unless the doctor needs help (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9.1, pp. 135–136). Cornermen may not enter during the match (GLORY 5.03.B, p. 10) [observation].
- **Protect the hurt man from the other.** "When stopping a bout, try to step in between the two boxers while trying to block the injured boxer from any further punches—always protect the injured boxer" (ABC-REF25 p. 7) [observation]. The winner stays in the neutral corner until released (GLORY 5.05.B.2) [observation].
- **Watch for the signs.** ABC lists concussion signs a referee should know: loss of consciousness, confusion, dizziness, a dazed look, delayed response and others (ABC-REF25 pp. 13–14) [observation]. The commission forbids an asserted injury mechanism, so the film shows the referee watching, not diagnosing [inference].

What the sources tell the referee not to do:

- **Do not move him or remove the mouthguard.** WAKO: "The Central referee will not otherwise intervene (remove the tooth guard or rotate the unconscious kickboxer to the lateral position). All interventions on the kickboxer will be carried out by an official doctor" (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9.1, p. 136) [observation]. ABC's mouthpiece rule likewise gives the handling of the mouthpiece to the corner, "not the referee" (ABC-REF22 p. 8) [observation]. Removing the mouthguard is the doctor's task, not the referee's [inference].
- **Do not help him up** (ABC-REF25 p. 6, during the count; WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9.1, after it) [observation].
- **Do not let the corners in** (GLORY 5.03.B; 5.08.A.4) [observation].

As staged: once he has waved off the bout he moves round B's head and kneels close at it on A's side, so that his body is between B and A (step 8). He beckons the doctor with one arm and rests the other hand lightly on B's upper arm or shoulder to keep him lying still, without turning him. He speaks a few short words to him ("Stay down"). His head turns between B, A and the doctor's steps, and a raised palm toward A keeps A's celebration away from B. He stays kneeling as the doctor arrives by the neutral steps (ONT 3) [choice built on the observations above]. This is the commission's "support": physical care within what the rules let a referee do.

## Scope {#scope}

Statements of the research contract this study does not cover, and why:

- **1. Technique laws.** The referee performs no fighting technique. His break, point, kneel and count are movements the motion vocabulary composes. The fighters' techniques: *the screenplay's later study owns them.*
