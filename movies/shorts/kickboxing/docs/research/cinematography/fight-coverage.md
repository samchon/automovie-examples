# How ring fights are shot

How live broadcasts and fiction films cover a ring fight: camera positions, movement, continuity and light. The brief chooses the film's camera grammar from these practices, and the screenplay states each beat's camera. Sources and locators are in the [research index](../index.md).

Observation kinds: *reported* for a production account by its makers or the trade press, *analysis* for a critic's reading of films, *inference*, *choice*.

## Live broadcast coverage {#broadcast}

- A major boxing broadcast mixes hard cameras on tripods for the wide view, handheld cameras at ringside and in the stands, a robotic camera and a jib in the air; for one 2017 event HBO ran two 4K handheld cameras at ringside, close enough for 4K zoom from a couple of feet away, where a 4K camera had previously sat about 100 ft from the action (SVG, Golovkin–Jacobs 2017). *reported*
- The main camera platform usually carries two cameras, three with a second broadcaster (SVG Europe, Joshua–Klitschko 2017). *reported*
- In the United Kingdom nobody may stand on the apron outside the ropes, so handheld cameras work from boxes built out at two corners; in the United States camera operators walk the apron along the side of the ring (SVG Europe, Joshua–Klitschko 2017). *reported*
- For the main event the arena is blacked out, so the lit ring stands in darkness and stray lights in the stands are switched off (SVG Europe, Joshua–Klitschko 2017). *reported*

## Fiction film coverage {#film}

- Until the 1970s a fight was cut from a wide shot of the ring to a medium two-shot to a close-up and back, with a mostly stationary camera; *Body and Soul* (1947) and *Rocky* (1976) set the camera low among the audience and shot the fight through the ropes, after newsreel coverage (ProVideo Coalition 2015). *analysis*
- *Raging Bull* (1980) put the camera inside the ring for the whole fight, moving constantly to keep up with the fighters; later fight films largely followed (ProVideo Coalition 2015). *analysis*
- *Creed* (2015) shot a whole professional fight as one continuous take inside the ring, with no cutaways; a Steadicam operator who had taken boxing lessons wove between the fighters, and the fight and the camera were blocked together for about a week in a practice ring (Slashfilm 2015; ProVideo Coalition 2015). *reported*

## What these practices offer this film {#choices}

- The ringside broadcast view, from outside the ropes and side-on to the fighters, holds both men's feet, hands and heads on one horizontal axis, the readability the commission requires. *inference*
- The inside-the-ring camera brings the audience into the exchange but loses the side view whenever it circles behind a fighter; the continuous take keeps time and space unbroken, which the commission requires for the decisive kick, the fall and the count. *inference*
- The brief combines them: a camera at ringside height that travels along the ring's sides, relates the two fighters side-on, reacts to each exchange and holds continuous takes where the commission forbids cuts. *choice*
