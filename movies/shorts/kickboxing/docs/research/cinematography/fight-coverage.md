# docs/research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md

How a combat-sports ring is actually shot, in live broadcast and in narrative film, and the camera laws and measurable targets this film's one continuous 180-second ring scene writes to. The consumers are the brief (the look and the camera's laws), the screenplay (where the camera stands in each beat) and the camera behavior that programs it. The commission rulings this study serves are [the camera relates the fighters](../../instructions/commission.md#camera), [the finish](../../instructions/commission.md#finish), [the fall](../../instructions/commission.md#fall), [the count](../../instructions/commission.md#count), [the sound ruling's exclusion of replay and time compression](../../instructions/commission.md#sound) and [the wide ending](../../instructions/commission.md#ending). The picture is landscape 16:9 and every person is a faceless polygon man, so what the camera must keep readable is posture, head and trunk turns, and the contacts.

Retrieved 2026-11-13. Every original is listed with its creator, local file and rights in [Sources](#sources); bytes and SHA-256 are in the [research index](../index.md).

## Observation marks {#marks}

Each observation carries one mark:

- **R**: a reported measurement, rule or documented practice, stated by the cited source at the cited locator.
- **O**: a direct observation by this study. There is none: no footage was acquired (see [Unavailable](#unavailable)), so nothing here describes footage from memory.
- **I**: an inference this study draws from cited values, with its reasoning shown.
- **C**: the production's own choice, proposed for the brief to adopt or change.

Locators: "¶n" is the n-th paragraph of the article body in the saved page, counted from the first paragraph after the headline; PDF locators give the section and the page as the PDF viewer numbers it.

## Live broadcast of boxing and kickboxing {#broadcast}

No retained original describes a GLORY or other kickboxing broadcast's camera plan (see [Unavailable](#unavailable)). The broadcast evidence comes from boxing, which is shot in the same kind of roped square ring, from the same kinds of positions, under the same kind of referee. Its translation to kickboxing is stated where it matters: a kickboxing exchange uses the legs as weapons and targets, so the feet must stay in frame, which a boxing waist-up shot does not need.

Officials' places constrain the camera ring: the referee "stays inside the Ring for the duration of the Match" (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.01.A, PDF p.10), and during a round no one but the contestants, the referee and the ringside doctor may be in the ring (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §4). A live camera therefore never stands inside the ropes during a round. The Ontario kickboxing rules also seat three judges on separate sides of the ring and the timekeeper on a fourth (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §3).

### Lenses, sizes and the ring {#lenses}

- The HBO-model main camera in Southpaw carried an Angenieux HR 25–250 mm (T3.5) zoom; the high camera 40–50 ft (12–15 m) up carried an Angenieux Optimo 24–290 mm; the ringside handhelds carried Fujinon Cabrio 19–90 mm (T2.9) zooms with manual zoom (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶15–17).
- Fujifilm's specification of the Cabrio 19–90 mm gives an angular field of 71°41' × 44°14' at 19 mm and 17°20' × 9°48' at 90 mm on a 27.45 × 15.44 mm (16:9) image area (R: `fujifilm-zk-cabrio-spec`, ZK19-90mm Specifications). So a ringside operator's vertical field runs from about 44° to about 10°.
- The GLORY ring is 6.40 × 6.40 m or 7.00 × 7.00 m, its floor 90–122 cm above the ground, with four ropes at 40, 80, 120 and 150 cm above the canvas, each 3–5 cm thick and wrapped in soft material; opposite corners carry the athletes' posts with two neutral corners between them (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §4.06, PDF p.9). Boxing's 22 × 22 ft (6.7 m) square (R: `svg-mayweather-pacquiao-2015` ¶6) falls inside that range.
- Translation of "above the second rope" (I): in the GLORY rope plan the second rope is 80 cm above the canvas. HBO's boxing rope heights are not in the source, so the rule is carried over as "the main camera is high enough that no rope crosses a fighter's trunk or head".

### The main side and the reverse {#main-side}

Broadcast coverage is built around one main side: the main camera and the two ringside handhelds face the ring from it ("one camera is in the middle of the audience, and you get left and right of the ring", R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶15). Cameras on the far side exist mainly for replays: super-slow-motion cameras sit "right alongside the main game camera and opposite the main game cameras for a reverse angle" to feed slow-motion replays (R: `svg-mayweather-pacquiao-2015` ¶7), and the 4K X-Mo was "placed opposite the game camera" for replays (R: `svg-showtime-xmo-2014` ¶5, ¶7).

Why the main camera stays on one side is not stated in any retained source (see [Unavailable](#unavailable)). The inference (I) is screen direction: with every live shot from the same side, the corner that starts screen-left stays screen-left, so a viewer never loses which man is which; a reverse angle is used where the viewer already knows the action, in replay. This film has no replay and no cut, so the problem is solved differently ([CL6](#cl6)).

### How coverage follows the fighters {#following}

- Sky's boxing director, Sara Chenery, describes the change from "the long lens head-to-toe in-out coverage of many years ago" with "ropes taking up half your screen" to a corner jib with "lateral movement... where we can move around the referee", plus more handheld (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶22).
- With only distant cameras and one ringside handheld, "every time the referee gets in shot you have to cut"; with the jib "they can just move around past him" (R: same ¶22).
- "I'll usually go to the jib head-to-toe rather than come all the way out to a long lens camera 60 feet [20m] away", but "the fight dictates the coverage": lighter boxers move fast, "so we'll cut the cameras a lot less"; heavier boxers "stay in pockets for a bit longer, so perhaps we cut round it a little bit more" (R: same ¶29).
- "We tend to come out head-to-toe when the referee says 'break' and everyone's had to move back, not when a boxer is in the middle of a really big exchange"; the novice failure is that "they just keep cutting the cameras because they're moving so fast" (R: same ¶33).
- A ringside operator: "You try to anticipate the direction the boxer will go"; gloves have hit his camera (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶10).
- Referees "often walk backwards, so they are looking at the boxing, but their bodies are facing away", which is why a camera worn by the referee points the wrong way (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶27). The referee's own rule is to keep "a side view, perpendicular to the boxers", to shorten his distance "when boxers are moving towards or fighting on the ropes", and to be centred between the boxers at the round's ten-second warning (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` pp.4–5). Translation (I): the ABC manual is a boxing manual; the GLORY referee's equivalent duties are breaking, sending to a neutral corner and counting (`glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05), so the side-view positioning is carried over as the referee's habit, not a GLORY rule.

### Knockdown, count and finish in broadcast {#knockdown}

- Mat-level cameras exist for the fall: ESPN's bumper cams catch fighters "coming down perfectly when they hit the mat", and NBC's Mattcams were added for "when a boxer is knocked out" (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-video-2020` ¶14; `svg-nbc-primetime-look-2015` ¶12). These are replay and iso angles, not the live line cut (I, from their being slow-motion or POV units).
- In Southpaw's HBO-model coverage, a crane 12 ft (3.7 m) up in one corner was "used in conjunction with the [ringside] cameras... for alternate-angle knockout shots", and the knockouts were also shot in slow motion (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶21).
- The count itself is a rule-bound procedure the camera must hold: the referee calls "Down" with a gesture, sends the opponent "to the farthest removed neutral corner" and only then starts the count; if the opponent leaves the corner the count stops (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.2, PDF p.14); GLORY's rules meeting adds that the count does not begin "until the opponent is in the neutral corner facing the center of the Ring" (R: same, rules-meeting appendix, PDF p.27). The referee "should kneel to the fallen boxer so he can hear and see the referee" and "always use hand signals (fingers)"; at ten he waves "their arms above their head" (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.6; `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §17).
- In broadcast, a downed man and the standing man in the far neutral corner are two subjects up to a ring diagonal apart (9.05–9.90 m for the GLORY ring sizes; I, from §4.06), which multi-camera coverage solves by cutting between them, as the ringside operators each "take a fighter" in the corners (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶19). One continuous camera must instead hold both, which is the hardest framing in this film ([CL9](#cl9)).

### Shot length and cut cadence {#cadence}

- No retained original measures broadcast shot length or cuts per minute in boxing or kickboxing, and no footage was acquired to measure it (see [Unavailable](#unavailable)). The broadcast evidence is qualitative: cut less for fast-moving lighter men, more for heavier men in pockets; come out head-to-toe at a break, not mid-exchange (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶29, ¶33). The bout in this film is 80 kg men ([fighters](../../instructions/commission.md#fighters)), the lighter of Sky's two cases (I).
- The underlying camera work is continuous: Southpaw's HBO operators covered "two to four rounds of boxing, with each round being three minutes long without a cut, covering it like a live televised fight" (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶9), so a live ringside camera runs 360–720 s without stopping and the cutting is the director's switching between such feeds (I).

### Replays, slow motion and graphics {#replays}

Broadcast coverage adds what this film excludes ([sound ruling](../../instructions/commission.md#sound): no replay, time compression or commentary): super-slow-motion replays ("extreme slow motion is very important in boxing", R: `svg-showtime-xmo-2014` ¶2; 300 fps in practice, ¶6), 360° replay systems with 120 or 32 cameras above the ring (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-video-2020` ¶15; `svg-nbc-primetime-look-2015` ¶14), and virtual graphics from a dedicated camera (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-video-2020` ¶20). They are recorded here only so the brief can name what the film leaves out.

## Narrative film inside the ring {#film}

### Creed (2015), Maryse Alberti {#creed}

- The first of the two fights was "two rounds in a single shot with a Steadicam rig", operated by Benjamin Semanoff; each "movie round" was 1.5 minutes "instead of the full three", with a one-minute break; "We did 12 takes, each lasting four minutes" (R: `asc-creed-2015` ¶10). So one uncut take ran 240 s, longer than this film's 180 s scene (I).
- The motive was isolation: "a boxer is completely alone when he enters the ring" (R: ¶11). The style: "a very inquisitive and curious camera that moves and searches... not a quiet and composed camera" (R: ¶8); realism over Raging Bull's stylisation, with A Prophet's motivated mix of Steadicam, handheld and static shots as the model (R: ¶7).
- The camera's priority in a fight: "being there with the camera at the right moment — seeing the movement and the glove connecting with the face — and preserving the human drama", choreographed with the fight coordinator "for the camera to be in the right place at the right time for the most impact" (R: ¶12).
- Depth of field: "In the ring I tried to keep a stop of T5.6 so we could have both fighters in focus" (R: ¶9).
- Ropes: a MōVI was "passed... through the ropes" and used "for circling the fighters in and out of the ring as they were against the ropes"; the final fight added a Technocrane, a Steadicam and two handhelds (R: ¶13).
- Light: rings lit "more 'HBO than Raging Bull'" from a perimeter truss of 120 Par cans focused narrow, medium and wide, "a very even lighting all across the ring" (R: ¶16).
- The article gives no camera-to-fighter distance, lens focal length or height for the one-take; those values are unavailable.

### Southpaw (2015), Mauro Fiore {#southpaw}

The first fight was deliberately "very observational, with an objective viewpoint" and modelled on HBO World Championship Boxing, with two HBO ringside operators and HBO's camera placement (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶5, ¶9, ¶15). The fighters went "all the rounds of each fight without stopping, and we were rolling the whole time" (R: ¶7). Later fights move into subjective cameras, a MōVI that can go "almost on the ground and lift it up to somebody's face", and point-of-view rigs (R: ¶29, ¶34). The objective first-fight model is the one this film's ringside camera follows (C).

### Raging Bull (1980), Michael Chapman {#raging-bull}

- Chapman's own account: the fights "were like dances", "very, very elaborately choreographed with camera moves and... steps for the actors", and "each fight was done in a different style – one was done all with long lenses, one was done... tried to make long... long takes as long as we could" (R: `wos-chapman-13522`, transcript).
- "The actual boxing would all be at 24 frames"; only when LaMotta was not boxing, walking to the neutral corner breathing, did the shot go to 48 fps "all in one shot", returning to 24 fps when he went to fight again (R: `wos-chapman-13523`, transcript). Real-time action with no slow motion during boxing is the practice this film's real-time count already requires (I).
- "In the ring it's extremely elaborate and full of all sorts of camera tricks and moves", against a static camera outside it (R: `wos-chapman-13524`, transcript).
- In the final LaMotta–Robinson fight a measured analysis counts "a total of fifty cuts... in a row" at the climax, "the average cutting time drops below 1 second", and the next shot holds "about 7 seconds" (R: `dergipark-raging-bull-rhythm`, PDF p.11). That stylised burst, with its added animal sounds, is the opposite of this film's continuous real-time take (I) and is excluded (C).

## The camera's laws for the screenplay {#laws}

Each law is what the screenplay writes and the camera behavior programs. A law marked C is a proposal for the brief; its evidence is cited.

1. <a id="cl1"></a>**CL1. One camera, one take, real time.** The scene is one uncut shot from 0 to 180 s, at normal speed throughout. Basis: the commission requires the kick and fall without a cut and the count in real time; Creed held an uncut 240 s take (R: `asc-creed-2015` ¶10); Raging Bull kept boxing at 24 fps (R: `wos-chapman-13523`). Mark: C. What broadcast does with cuts, this camera does with movement, as Sky's jib does (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶22).
2. <a id="cl2"></a>**CL2. Ringside, outside the ropes.** The camera lives on the ring's perimeter outside the ropes, never inside them during the round, matching the commission's ringside audience position ([direction](../../instructions/commission.md#direction)) and the live rule that only fighters, referee and doctor are in the ring (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §4). Its lens is 1.8–2.2 m above the canvas, so that from 0.5 m behind the top rope (150 cm, R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §4.06) the sightline to a man's feet 3.5–6 m away passes over the rope (I: at 1.8 m lens height the sightline is at 1.54–1.65 m above the canvas where it crosses the rope; at 1.6 m it would cross at 1.37–1.47 m, through the rope). Mark: C, with the clearance as I.
3. <a id="cl3"></a>**CL3. Side view on the fighters' line.** During every exchange the camera's optical axis is within 30° of the perpendicular to the line joining the two men's hips, so the fighters stand side by side along the frame's horizontal axis and their feet, the struck target and both heads lie on that one screen axis, as the commission requires. Basis: a 16:9 frame tall enough for a standing man is 1.78 times as wide, so two men side by side fit only when the camera looks across their line; looking along the line stacks one man behind the other (I). The ringside operators' "close and wide" coverage (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶19) is the precedent for keeping both men whole. Mark: C, with the geometry as I.
4. <a id="cl4"></a>**CL4. The referee beyond the fighters.** The referee keeps his own side view, perpendicular to the boxers (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.4), so the camera takes the perpendicular on the opposite side and he reads in the background between and beyond the men, his head and trunk turns visible. When he steps across the camera's view (a break, the clinch-and-knee release, the knockdown call), the camera arcs around him rather than let him cover a head, a foot or the target, as Sky's jib "move[s] around past him" instead of cutting (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶22). Mark: C.
5. <a id="cl5"></a>**CL5. Reframing as the fighters move.** When the line rotates (circling, pivots, angle changes), the camera travels around the perimeter to stay within CL3's 30°; when the pair translate, it pans and tracks. It anticipates the direction of travel, as ringside operators do (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶10). Speeds are in [Targets](#targets). Mark: C.
6. <a id="cl6"></a>**CL6. Changing sides only in the open.** Broadcast holds one live side and keeps the reverse for replay (R: `svg-mayweather-pacquiao-2015` ¶7; I for the reason). A continuous take may change sides, because the viewer sees the camera travel; it does so only while the men reset, circle or are separated by a break, never during an exchange, the timing Sky uses for its widest shot (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶33). Mark: C.
7. <a id="cl7"></a>**CL7. On the ropes and in the corners.** When one man is driven onto the ropes, the line between the men meets the ropes nearly at a right angle, so the side view looks along the rope line: the camera moves to the adjacent side of the ring, 90° around the corner from the ropes in use, at 3–5 m from the pair. Basis: CL3's geometry (I); Creed's MōVI "circling the fighters... as they were against the ropes" (R: `asc-creed-2015` ¶13); the referee shortens his distance on the ropes (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.4), so CL4's arc is likely there. In a corner the adjacent sides both look along a rope line at 45° to the corner's diagonal (I). Mark: C.
8. <a id="cl8"></a>**CL8. The tell and the finish from the kick side.** The exchanges that build B's tell, and the finish at 150.000, are seen from A's right side, which is B's left: A's rising right knee, B's dropping left arm and, at the finish, A's instep and lower shin landing over that arm behind B's left ear all face the camera (I, from the commission's [tell](../../instructions/commission.md#tell) and [finish](../../instructions/commission.md#finish)). For the finish the lens rises to 2.0–2.5 m and looks down 20–30° (C), because B falls on his right side, away from this camera, and only a raised view shows the canvas he falls onto. The frame holds both men head to foot through the kick, plus the canvas on B's far side, from the loss of support to the body at rest, with no cut (commission [fall](../../instructions/commission.md#fall)). Mark: I and C.
9. <a id="cl9"></a>**CL9. The count in one frame.** Between 150 and 157 the camera travels while the referee calls "Down" and sends A to the farthest neutral corner (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.2), so that from 157 to 167.6 B on the canvas, the kneeling, finger-counting referee (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.6) and A in the neutral corner facing the ring's centre (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` PDF p.27) share one frame without a cut. The camera stands beyond B, looking from B toward A's corner, elevated 2.0–2.5 m, so B is foreground and A background and the distance between them runs into depth rather than across the frame (I: the B-to-corner distance can reach the ring's centre-to-corner 4.5–4.9 m or, if B falls near a corner, the 9.05–9.90 m diagonal, which across a 16:9 frame would shrink a standing man to a third of the frame height or less). Mark: C, with the distances as I.
10. <a id="cl10"></a>**CL10. The wide ending.** From 168.1 the camera widens and slows to hold A's celebration with raised gloves, B on the canvas and the referee attending B in one frame to 180, as the commission's [ending](../../instructions/commission.md#ending) requires. Mark: C.
11. <a id="cl11"></a>**CL11. No broadcast additions.** No slow motion, replay, overhead switch, point-of-view, graphics or rig in frame (commission [sound](../../instructions/commission.md#sound) ruling for replay and time compression; C for the rest, since a single real-time take cannot jump to an overhead or a mat camera).
12. <a id="cl12"></a>**CL12. Both men sharp, ring evenly lit.** Depth of field holds both fighters and the referee in focus, as Creed's T5.6 did (R: `asc-creed-2015` ¶9), and the ring is lit evenly across, as in Creed's "HBO" ring (R: ¶16), so no man drops into darkness when the pair move. Target (C, no retained source measures ring light): illuminance on the canvas within one third of a stop, about ±25 %, of its value at the centre, everywhere inside the ropes; the hall beyond the first rows at least three stops darker than the canvas; measured as relative exposure on rendered frames. Mark: C.

## The look {#look}

The look the brief can adopt: observational realism in the HBO model, the way Southpaw's first fight is "very observational, with an objective viewpoint" (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶5) and Creed is "more realistic and less stylized" than Raging Bull (R: `asc-creed-2015` ¶7), but with Creed's moving, searching single camera (R: ¶8) in place of broadcast switching. The camera is a ringside presence that reacts to the action, keeps the two men related along one screen axis, holds both sharp in an evenly lit ring, and never cheats time (C, from the sources cited).

## Sequence and tempo across the round {#sequence}

How the camera chains through the twelve phases ([phases](../../instructions/commission.md#phases)), by the laws above (C unless marked):

1. Glove touch and the first exchanges: the camera is set on the perpendicular opposite the referee (CL3, CL4) at the wider end of the frame-height range, because the opening situation is new to the viewer.
2. The exchanges before 150: the camera follows the men around the whole ring ([whole ring](../../instructions/commission.md#whole-ring)), changing sides only at resets and breaks (CL6), moving to the adjacent side on the ropes (CL7) and arcing around the referee (CL4). The exchanges that build the tell favour A's right side (CL8).
3. 150–157: the finish and fall from the kick side, held to the body at rest (CL8), then the travel to the count position while the referee calls "Down" and sends A to the corner (CL9).
4. 157–167.6: the count held in one frame (CL9).
5. 167.6–180: the knockout signal, the celebration from 168.1 and the match-ending bell at 170.1, widening to the last frame (CL10).

Sky's guidance on tempo (cut less with fast lighter men, widen at breaks) translates into this camera's motion: fewer, longer moves during fast exchanges and repositioning at the breaks (I, from `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶29, ¶33).

## Markers of professional coverage {#level}

What separates professional ring coverage from a beginner's, as the sources state them:

- It does not chase every movement: the novice director "keep[s] cutting the cameras because they're moving so fast"; the professional slows down and changes view at a break, "not when a boxer is in the middle of a really big exchange" (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶33).
- It keeps the fighters clear of the ropes in the main view (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶15) and no longer lets "ropes tak[e] up half your screen" (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶22).
- Its operators anticipate where the boxer will go (R: `asc-southpaw-2015-p2` ¶10).
- The referee does not force a cut or a blocked view: a moving camera goes around him (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶22).
- It holds one side live and keeps the reverse for replay (R: `svg-mayweather-pacquiao-2015` ¶7).
- It shows the contact and the face's drama in the same move: "seeing the movement and the glove connecting with the face — and preserving the human drama" (R: `asc-creed-2015` ¶12). For faceless men the drama is the head and trunk (commission [referee](../../instructions/commission.md#referee) ruling on expression), so the translation is the target and the head in the same frame (I).

## The invented coverage and its analogue {#analogue}

This film's camera is a virtual camera with no operator, cable or rig. Its closest real analogues are Sky's corner jib, a ringside camera with lateral movement around the referee (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶22, ¶31), and Creed's single-take Steadicam (R: `asc-creed-2015` ¶10). It departs from them in three ways, each a choice (C): it travels the whole perimeter, where a jib's lateral movement is limited to one corner; it never enters the ring, where Creed's Steadicam and MōVI did; and its speed is held to the measured limits in [Targets](#targets), so it moves no faster than a real rig could.
