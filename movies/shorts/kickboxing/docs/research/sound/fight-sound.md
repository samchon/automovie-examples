# Fight sound

This study records what a televised fight sounds like and who is heard, so the screenplay's sound layers follow broadcast practice. The accounts are boxing and MMA productions; no kickboxing-specific account was found. Each observation carries its kind as the [index](../index.md#observation-kinds) defines it; source ids are in the [index](../index.md#sources).

## Who is heard {#voices}

- [reported] The referee wears a microphone: "wireless lavaliere … mics worn by both trainers and the referee" (`SVG-AUDIO-MAYWEATHER-PACQUIAO`); "lavaliere microphones on the referee and trainers in both corners" (`SVG-AUDIO-PBC`); lavs "on all the referees for each of the six bouts on the card" (`SVG-AUDIO-WILDER-FURY`). A referee's warning to a fighter was "caught … loud and clear" (`SVG-AUDIO-DELAHOYA`); a mix aims at "the referee screaming 'he's out, he's out'" (`TVTECH-GORDON`, a vendor interview).
- [reported] Corners are heard during the fight: "wired mics on the corners to pick up coaches' calling out directions during the matches" (`SVG-AUDIO-DAZN`); a ringside camera operator "can hear the corner people (trainers, friends, family) yelling" (`FERRO-HBO`); without a crowd, "you could hear the shouts coming from the corner" (`SVGE-BT-BOXING`).
- [reported] The bell is heard: the announcers' headsets "pick up transients, such as the bell signaling the start and end of rounds" (`SVG-AUDIO-PBC`).

## Contact and movement {#contact}

- [reported] Microphones over the ring "pick up most of the footwork and punches — '99.5% of that'"; the array hangs about 9.5 ft above the ring, with four of its six microphones facing out to the ringside crowd (`SVG-AUDIO-WILDER-FURY`). "The punch sounds are the real focus of boxing" (`SVG-AUDIO-CANELO-GGG`).
- [reported] "Even if you're sitting ringside you're not going to hear the punches" as the broadcast does; punch levels are mixed "lower for wide shots, louder for close-ups" (`SVG-AUDIO-DELAHOYA`).
- [reported] "A kick-drum type of microphone … used to amplify the thumps of bodies hitting canvas floor" (`SVG-AUDIO-UFC`).

## The crowd {#crowd}

- [reported] Floor-seat fans are "the most rambunctious" (`SVG-AUDIO-WILDER-FURY`); a mixer "could hear the crowd roaring inside the MGM Grand through the walls of the truck" (`SVG-AUDIO-DELAHOYA`); the mix captures "the audience screaming … one fan reacting interestingly" (`TVTECH-GORDON`).
- [reported] No source measures crowd level at a knockdown, or states that the ten-count is audible on air.

## Law {#law}

[choice, from the practice above]: the film sounds like a broadcast mix with no commentary track. The referee is close and clear, as a referee's microphone makes him. Contact is heard closer than a ringside seat would hear it, and louder in close framings than in wide ones; footwork scuffs and the thump of a body on canvas are part of the mix. The bell is heard at its strokes. The crowd is a constant bed that swells with landed blows and near-misses, and a few corner shouts carry over it without distinct words. The count, being the referee's, stays audible over the crowd.

[choice]: no source describes contact sounds by surface; the commission asks for "distinct contacts", so the film gives each kind of contact its own sound, distinguishing glove from shin, a landed blow from a blocked one, and the body from the head, and the finishing kick a sound heard nowhere else in the round. The brief's [ring sound](../../body/brief/sound/ring-sound.md#contact) owns the palette.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

Each target is checked on the film's sound plan and, once it exists, by the user's ear [choice, from the law above]:

- Every word the referee speaks is intelligible over the crowd, including every number of the count.
- Every contact the picture shows has its sound, and no contact sound plays without a contact the picture shows or the scene has established.
- No two kinds of contact share a sound, and a blocked body kick never sounds like a landed one.
- No sound is heard that the ring, its people or the arena could not produce.
