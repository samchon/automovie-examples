# docs/research/sound/ring-sound.md

What a live kickboxing ring sounds like without commentary, how that sound is captured at ringside, and the sound palette, rules and targets the film's one continuous 180-second ring scene writes to. The consumers are the brief (the palette and its rules), the screenplay (which sounds each beat names) and the sound behavior that places them. The commission rulings this study serves are [the sound of the film](../../instructions/commission.md#sound) (start and match-ending bells, English referee words, breaths, mat friction and distinct contacts carry the film; no replay, time compression or commentary; every sound one the scene could produce), [language](../../instructions/commission.md#language) (the fighters never speak; the referee's words are the only speech), [the count](../../instructions/commission.md#count) and [the referee](../../instructions/commission.md#referee).

Retrieved 2026-11-13. Every original is listed with its creator, local file and rights in [Sources](#sources); bytes and SHA-256 are in the [research index](../index.md).

- **O**: a direct observation by this study. There is none: no recording or footage was acquired (see [Unavailable](#unavailable)).
- **I**: an inference this study draws from cited values, with its reasoning shown.
- **C**: the production's own choice, proposed for the brief to adopt or change.

## How ring sound is captured live {#capture}

Broadcast sound is the best evidence of what is audible at a ring, because its microphones are placed to catch exactly the ring's own sounds. All of it is boxing or MMA; no retained original describes a GLORY or other kickboxing broadcast's audio (see [Unavailable](#unavailable)).

- **Overhead.** ESPN's "Tiffany" array is six Sennheiser 8060 mini-shotguns flown over the ring, "four facing outward to pick up ringside-crowd noise and two facing directly downward"; the downward pair "pick up most of the footwork and punches — '99.5% of that'". It normally hangs 9.5 ft (2.9 m) above the ring (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶8). NBC strung two gradient condensers "nearly 8 ft. above the center of the ring" (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶4); HBO used shotguns "that pick up ambient effects sounds over the ring and from neutral corners" (R: `svg-hbo-fiber-audio-2014` ¶5). All three plans hang their ring microphones over the ring, not at its edge (I).
- **Corners.** Shotguns in each corner (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶4), long shotguns on fishpoles "at the red and blue corners" (R: `svg-hbo-fiber-audio-2014` ¶5), lavaliers run up the ring pads to the bottom rope, "pointed toward and about 2 ft. away from each fighter's corner" (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶6), lavaliers on corner posts and microphones face down on the mat (R: `svg-ufc-fox-sound-2013` ¶7).
- **People.** Lavaliers on the referee and the trainers (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶4; `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶5); wired corner microphones "to pick up coaches' calling out directions during the matches" (R: `svg-dazn-audio-2019` ¶5).
- **The canvas and the fall.** UFC productions use "a kick-drum type of microphone... used to amplify the thumps of bodies hitting canvas floor in the ring" (R: `svg-ufc-fox-sound-2013` ¶3); DAZN considered "placing a microphone under the mat to pick up foot movement" (R: `svg-dazn-audio-2019` ¶10).
- **Crowd.** Microphones on a robotic camera "in the far neutral corner and in the far corners of the arena to get the sounds from the floor seats, fans whom Wimer calls 'the most rambunctious'" (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶10); additional shotguns in the audience (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶4); six audience shotguns plus a hot house PA mix in UFC (R: `svg-ufc-fox-sound-2013` ¶5).
- **Limits.** Parabolic microphones are not allowed at the sidelines and fishpoles "show up in the camera shots" (R: `svg-dazn-audio-2019` ¶6); punch and mat sounds are easy to catch near the ring's periphery and "harder to get... in the center of the ring" (R: `svg-ufc-fox-sound-2013` ¶6).
- **Perspective follows the camera.** Sky's director: "the sound operator follows where the boxers are. So if I'm on camera three handheld, he'll be on that camera's microphone... If I go to the Steadicam in the crowd, they'll be on that Steadicam so they get all the cheering effects. It's much harder, but it's much more real" (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶23).
- **Everything, all the time.** "Boxing... takes its audio in from everywhere all the time" (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶2), which is why a single profanity forces the whole soundtrack to be muted.

From these, the film's listening position (C): the mix is heard from the camera's place at ringside, as Sky's mix follows the camera, so a contact is louder and drier when the camera is near it, and the crowd surrounds the camera at all times. One continuous camera ([fight coverage CL1](../cinematography/fight-coverage.md#cl1)) gives one continuous listening position.

## The bell and the timekeeper's signals {#bell}

- **The instrument.** The timekeeper is equipped with "a bell with a metal hammer or similar device" (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §7); BIBA asks for "a Bell or some form of Acoustic Instrument to sound the rounds" (R: `biba-timekeeper-guidelines` p.1); GLORY names a "gong/bell" (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.4–5, PDF p.14). The bell is struck: "Strike the bell" (R: `british-army-boxing-timekeeper-cpd` slide 3).
- **Start of the round.** "By sounding the start of the Round gong, the Timekeeper signals the beginning and end of the Rounds for the Referee" (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.01.F, PDF p.11); "On the referee's signal, the timekeeper shall begin the first round of a bout by sounding the bell" (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §7). Before it, the referee is "positioned in the CENTER of the ring before the bell rings" and keeps the boxers "in their corners until the bell sounds" (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.4). The glove touch comes at the referee's final instructions, before the men return to their corners (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §6; `biba-timekeeper-guidelines` p.1). Translation for this film (I): the commission's glove touch opens the exchanges ([glove touch](../../instructions/commission.md#glove-touch)), so in this film it falls after the start bell; a touch of gloves at the start of a round is not a rule requirement in the retained sources, and the commission's ruling decides it.
- **Ten-second warning.** GLORY's rules give the timekeeper a signal ten seconds before the end of the one-minute interval, followed by the referee's "seconds out", and name no warning inside a round (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.01.F, PDF p.11). Ontario's kickboxing rules give "warning to the contestants by sounding the wooden clapper" ten seconds before the end of each round and a whistle ten seconds before the end of each rest (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §7). In British boxing the timekeeper will "bang the gavel twice and raise his left arm" at 2:50 (R: `biba-timekeeper-guidelines` p.2); English amateur boxing uses the gavel (R: `british-army-boxing-timekeeper-cpd` slide 3). The referee uses the warning to centre himself between the boxers (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.5).
- **No bell during a count.** The bell does not end a round while a man is being counted (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §7 and §17, "A [contestant] may not be saved by the bell"; `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.4; `biba-timekeeper-guidelines` p.3).
- **The match-ending bell.** After a count to ten, "the Timekeeper will sound the gong/bell indicating the end of the Match" (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.5, PDF p.14). The commission places it at 170.1, 2.5 s after the knockout signal at 167.6 ([count](../../instructions/commission.md#count)).
- **Number of strikes and duration.** None of the retained rules says how many times the bell is struck or how long it rings; each says only "sound" or "ring" or "strike" the bell. No measured recording of a ring bell was obtained. Both values are unavailable and are left to the production ([palette](#palette)).
- **Bell as a transient.** NBC's broadcast found that announcers' headsets "pick up transients, such as the bell signaling the start and end of rounds" (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶7): the bell is a sharp-onset sound that carries through every open microphone (I).

For this film (I): the round starts at the film's opening and the knockout signal comes at 167.6, so an in-round ten-second warning, which a boxing-rules round would sound at 170 s of round time, would fall after the knockout; under GLORY's rules there is none anyway. No clapper or gavel is heard in the film (C).

## The referee's voice {#referee}

- **Commands.** GLORY: "fight" (begins or resumes, "only once the Athletes are separated an equal distance from the Referee"), "break" (separates; the men step back and resume without a new "fight"), "stop" (stops the fight, at the end of a round or before a warning) and the "Down" call with a gesture (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.A–B, PDF p.14). Boxing's basic commands are "STOP-BREAK-BOX"; to break, "use the word 'Stop' as your first command, followed by 'Break'"; at the bell, "call out 'TIME!'"; when a man rises, "Are you OK? Do you want to continue?" (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` pp.4–6). Translation (I): GLORY's word for resuming is "fight", where boxing uses "box", so this film uses "fight".
- **Knockdown call.** On a knockdown from a legal blow the referee "shall immediately call 'down' and signal by pointing to the ring floor", orders the opponent "to the farthest neutral corner", and counts "one-second intervals, in a loud clear voice, indicating the count... with a motion of their arm and upraised fingers" (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §17). He "should kneel to the fallen boxer so he can hear and see the referee" and "should not just rely on their voice" (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.6). At ten he waves "both arms above their head to indicate that the contestant has been knocked out" (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §17). GLORY counts only after the opponent is in the neutral corner "facing the center of the Ring" (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2`, rules-meeting appendix, PDF p.27). The commission's cadence, "One" at 158 through "Ten" at 167, is that one-second interval (I).
- **Voice against noise.** The referee shortens his distance when "there is a good deal of noise, and verbal commands may not be easily heard" (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.5); a referee "wearing a microphone for television" is warned to watch what he says (R: same p.15); the referee's voice reaches broadcast "through both those [headset microphones] and other close-in microphones and as ambient sound picked up through the PA system" (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶7). Sky wishes for a referee microphone because "you'd hear all those punches close up" (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶28).
- **Other officials' voices.** Ontario's rules seat a knockdown timekeeper who, when a man is down, "shall immediately stand and count one-second intervals in a loud clear voice" until the referee takes up the count (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §17); BIBA's timekeeper does the same and also announces "Round One" and "Seconds Out" (R: `biba-timekeeper-guidelines` p.2). GLORY's rules name no knockdown timekeeper and give "seconds out" to the referee (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.01, PDF pp.10–11). Under the commission's [language](../../instructions/commission.md#language) ruling no official but the referee speaks (C, consistent with GLORY's rule text).

## The fighters' breath and effort {#breath}

- **The sharp exhale on a strike is real and functional.** Twenty trained MMA/Muay Thai practitioners (at least one year's training, several with professional experience) kicked a heavy bag harder with a kiai (shouted exhale) than without: 24.2 g against 22 g, a 9% increase (R: `sinnett-2018-grunting` pp.3–4).
- **The kick's delivery time.** "A conservative estimate of the time it takes for a kick to leave the ground till contact... is approximately 300 ms", derived from the kicks in the study's video clips (R: `sinnett-2018-grunting` p.8).
- **Exhale timing against contact.** No retained source measures when the exhale starts or ends relative to contact; the timing target is a choice ([targets](#targets)).
- **Breathing rate through a round.** No retained source gives breaths per minute in kickboxing or boxing; unavailable.
- **Audibility.** The breath is heard at ringside microphones only when the ring overheads are near (I from [capture](#capture)): the downward overheads "pick up most of the footwork and punches" (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶8), and breath, which the source does not mention, is quieter than either; the film sets its level in [targets](#targets) (C, no measurement retained).
- **No words.** The fighters' voices are breath and effort only (commission [language](../../instructions/commission.md#language)).

## Contacts {#contacts}

No retained original measures the sound of glove-on-glove, glove-on-body, shin-on-arm or shin-on-head contact; no recording was obtained (see [Unavailable](#unavailable)). What the sources fix:

- Contact sounds are the main thing the ring overheads exist to catch (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶8) and they fall off away from the microphones (R: `svg-ufc-fox-sound-2013` ¶6), so in this film their level follows the camera's distance ([capture](#capture)).
- Film sound has replaced real contacts with stylised effects: Raging Bull's ring blows are "amplified in the non-diegetic universe with sound effects", with "animal sounds" added (R: `dergipark-raging-bull-rhythm` PDF p.11). The commission requires every sound to be one the scene could produce, so no such effect is used (C).

The distinctions the screenplay writes are inferences from what strikes what (I), held as rules until a recording is acquired:

| Contact | What meets what | Distinct character the film gives it (I); level and duration in [targets](#targets) |
|---|---|---|
| Glove on glove (touch, parry, block of a punch) | Padded leather on padded leather | Short, light, paired sound; the lightest contact |
| Glove on head (a punch landing on the head, glancing or clean) | Padded glove on the bare head | Sharper and shorter than glove on body, with no breath forced out |
| Shin on body (a round kick landing on the ribs) | Bare shin on bare torso | A thick, heavy slap, heavier than any glove contact, with the struck man's breath forced out at the same instant |
| Shin on leg (a low kick landing on the thigh) | Bare shin on the bare thigh | A heavy slap, lower than shin on arm |
| Glove on body | Padded glove on bare torso | Duller and heavier than glove on glove, with the skin of the torso in it; a breath is often forced out of the struck man at the same instant (I, from the commission's ruling that body contacts "interrupt breath and guard", [contacts before the finish](../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish)) |
| Shin on arm (a kick met by the guard) | Bare shin on bare forearm and the glove behind it | Harder and sharper than any glove contact; a check (shin on shin) is the same family, bone against bone through skin |
| Shin or instep on the side of the head and neck (the finish) | Bare instep and lower shin on the neck below and behind the ear | The heaviest single contact of the film, with no glove in it; followed at once by the fall, not by a voice |
| Knee to the body in the clinch | Bare knee on the torso | Heavy and short, close to glove-on-body but harder |

Rule (C): no two contact types share one sound; each named contact in the screenplay is one of these classes, and its loudness follows the camera's distance.

## Canvas, footwork, the fall, the ropes and the corners {#ring}

- **Canvas and footwork.** The ring floor is canvas over a raised platform, and the canvas "should not be slippery" (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §4.06, PDF p.9). Footwork is audible to the overheads (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶8) and was considered worth a microphone under the mat (R: `svg-dazn-audio-2019` ¶10). So the film's mat friction is the scuff and pivot of bare feet on canvas, with weight changes in it (I; commission [sound](../../instructions/commission.md#sound) names "mat friction").
- **The fall.** A body hitting the canvas is a low thump worth its own kick-drum microphone (R: `svg-ufc-fox-sound-2013` ¶3), and the ring floor moves when men hit it: ESPN stabilised its mat cameras so "there's not a lot of bounce... when the fighters hit the mat" (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-video-2020` ¶14). B's articulated fall ([fall](../../instructions/commission.md#fall)) is therefore a sequence of contacts with the canvas, from the first to the body at rest, the heaviest of them a low thump with the platform's give in it (I; their count, spacing and levels are in [targets](#targets), C).
- **The ropes.** GLORY's ropes are 3–5 cm thick, wrapped in soft material and held by cloth ties (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §4.06, PDF p.9). No source describes their sound; a man driven onto padded ropes makes a soft, low strain rather than a ring (I; its level in [targets](#targets) is C).
- **The corners.** In broadcast the corners are miked because coaches call out directions during the round (R: `svg-dazn-audio-2019` ¶5; `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶6). Under the commission's language ruling their calls cannot be heard as words (C): the corners are present in the sound only as the crowd's nearest voices, wordless (C). During the count, cornermen may not approach their athletes or throw in the towel (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.2 and B.7, PDF pp.14–15).

## The crowd {#crowd}

- **Size and nearness.** At the Muay Thai shows where crowd noise was tested on live judges, "crowd sizes varied from 500 to 3000, with proximity between judges and crowd varying from two to several meters" (R: `myers-balmer-2012-muay-thai-crowd-noise` p.3). Translation (I): Muay Thai shows are the nearest retained evidence for a kickboxing ring's crowd; a GLORY arena's size is not in any retained source.
- **The crowd reacts to strikes and takes sides.** Live crowd noise shifted qualified judges' scoring by 0.53 points per bout toward the home fighter (R: same p.4); the authors relate this to judges reading "noise equating to more effective strikes" (R: same p.6, citing Unkelbach and Memmert). So a ringside crowd rises with effective strikes and leans to one man (I).
- **Boxing crowds in broadcast.** "We had a lot of audience in the mix, and there's a lot of excitement in there, but you will get a few words that we have to cut"; "a persistent and annoyingly loud whistler in the crowd penetrated the mix"; "It's a very noisy place" (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶3, ¶7, ¶9). The floor seats are "the most rambunctious" (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶10). Between rounds promoters play "very high volume music" and with some songs "the crowd will go absolutely crazy" (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶24); this film has no interval and no music (commission [sound](../../instructions/commission.md#sound)).
- **The crowd's reaction delay.** No source measures how fast a crowd reacts to a knockdown. The nearest measurement: viewers pressing a key to report a kick's direction from video responded in 245–472 ms (R: `sinnett-2018-grunting` p.6). Translation (I): that is a manual response by naive viewers in a laboratory, not a vocal response by fight fans, so it gives only the order of magnitude, a few tenths of a second, for the crowd's first sound after the finish lands.
- **Crowd level and its change.** No retained source measures a combat-sports crowd's level or its change at a knockdown or knockout; unavailable. The measurement method is retained: a crowd outburst should be measured with fast time-weighting (LAF,max) and equivalent level (LAeq), not as an instantaneous peak, because in one measured football goal the peak reading (121.7 dB) overstated the fast-weighted maximum (110.1 dB) by 11.6 dB (R: `osses-2026-crowd-noise` pp.2–3). Translation (I): that is a stadium football goal, used here only for the method, not for a level.
- **What the crowd may not supply.** Words: a crowd's intelligible shouts would be speech the commission excludes (C), as the broadcast mute shows crowd words do carry (R: `svg-primetime-mute-2015` ¶2–3).

The crowd's behaviour for this film (C, built on the evidence above): a constant bed through the round that swells with landed and near-landed strikes and leans audibly toward neither man early; a sharp rise beginning a few tenths of a second after the finish lands; a sustained roar through the fall; held below the referee's count from 157 to 167.6, which stays intelligible because he kneels close to B and counts loud and with his fingers (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` pp.5–6); a second surge at the knockout signal at 167.6 and through A's celebration from 168.1; the match-ending bell at 170.1 cutting through it; no words. The levels and times of each stage are chosen in [targets](#targets).

## The sound palette and its rules {#palette}

| Sound | When | Rule | Mark and source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start bell | The round's start, before the glove touch | One struck ring of a metal bell, heard over the crowd | Bell by rule: R `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.01.F, `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §7; one strike: C (the strike count is unavailable) |
| Match-ending bell | 170.1 | A rapid series of strikes on the same bell, distinct from the start's single strike | End-of-match bell by rule: R `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.5; the series: C (unavailable) |
| Ten-second clapper or gavel | Never | Not heard: GLORY names none in a round, and a boxing-rules warning would fall after the knockout | R `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.01.F; I |
| Referee's words | Throughout | English only, the only speech in the film: "Fight", "Break", "Stop", "Down", the count "One" to "Ten", and the procedure words the brief writes (for example sending A to the neutral corner); his voice rises with the noise and is closest at the count | R `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05; R `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` pp.4–6; commission [language](../../instructions/commission.md#language) |
| Referee's knockout signal | 167.6 | Both arms waved above the head; it needs no word | R `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §17; `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.6 |
| Fighters' breathing | Throughout | Breath only, never words; heavier and more broken after landed body shots and as the round goes on, at the rates in [targets](#targets); A's excited breath in the celebration ([ending](../../instructions/commission.md#ending)) | Commission; C |
| Strike exhale | On committed strikes, above all the kicks | A short forced exhale ending at contact ([targets](#targets)) | R `sinnett-2018-grunting` pp.3–4 (it is used and adds force); timing C |
| Contacts | Every landed or blocked strike | One of the classes in [contacts](#contacts); loudness follows the camera's distance | I; C |
| Mat friction | Every footwork phase, pivot and weight change | Bare feet scuffing and pivoting on canvas | R `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶8; `svg-dazn-audio-2019` ¶10; I |
| B's fall | From the loss of support to the body at rest | A sequence of canvas contacts, the heaviest a low thump with the ring floor's give; count, spacing and levels in [targets](#targets) | R `svg-ufc-fox-sound-2013` ¶3; `svg-wilder-fury-2-video-2020` ¶14; I |
| Ropes | When a man is driven onto them | A soft, low strain of padded rope, at the level in [targets](#targets) | R `glory-rules-2026-v2` §4.06; I |
| Corners | Throughout the round | Present only as the nearest wordless voices in the crowd bed; silent in the count | C; R `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.2 |
| Crowd | Throughout | See [the crowd](#crowd): no words, no music, no PA | C |
| Excluded | Always | Commentary, music score, replay voice, PA announcer, walk-out music, stylised effects such as animal sounds | Commission [sound](../../instructions/commission.md#sound); R `dergipark-raging-bull-rhythm` PDF p.11 for the excluded film practice |

Rule (C): the mix's perspective is the camera's ringside position at every moment ([capture](#capture)); nothing is heard that no source in the ring or the crowd could make.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

| Target | Range | Method and uncertainty | Mark and source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Count cadence | One word per 1.0 s, "One" at 158 through "Ten" at 167, onset of each word within ±0.1 s of its second | Word onsets on the final mix's waveform. The one-second interval is the rule; the ±0.1 s tolerance is a choice. | R `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §17; commission [count](../../instructions/commission.md#count); C |
| Knockout signal to match-ending bell | 2.5 s (167.6 to 170.1) | Commission anchors; GLORY rule order (count to ten, then the bell). | Commission; R `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.5 |
| Start bell strikes | 1 | Count onsets. Rules give no number. | C |
| Match-ending bell strikes | 3–6 strikes, 0.2–0.4 s apart | Count onsets and intervals. No source; a choice that only has to differ from the start. | C |
| Bell ring-out | Unavailable as a measurement; the production holds each strike audible until the next sound of equal level masks it | Measure the decay on the final mix. | C |
| Kick delivery that the exhale accompanies | About 300 ms from the foot leaving the canvas to contact | As reported, a conservative estimate from video. | R `sinnett-2018-grunting` p.8 |
| Exhale timing against contact | Onset 50–200 ms before contact, ending 0–150 ms after it | Onset and offset against the contact transient on the final mix. No source measures it; the range is a choice set inside the ~300 ms delivery. | C |
| Contact levels by class | Peak level at the same distance from the camera, relative to the finish's contact: non-overlapping bands: instep and shin on head and neck 0 dB; shin on body -3 to -5 dB; shin on arm and shin on shin -6 to -8 dB; shin on leg -9 to -11 dB; knee to body -12 to -14 dB; glove on body -15 to -17 dB; glove on head -18 to -20 dB; glove on glove -22 to -26 dB. Durations, also non-overlapping where levels are near: finish 100 to 150 ms; shin on body 90 to 130 ms; shin on arm and shin on shin 40 to 70 ms; shin on leg 80 to 110 ms; knee to body 70 to 100 ms; glove on body 60 to 90 ms; glove on head 30 to 50 ms; glove on glove 20 to 40 ms, two onsets 10 to 30 ms apart | LAF,max per contact on the final mix, corrected to a common distance; no recording was retained, so every value is a choice that only has to keep the classes apart and in this order | C |
| Breath level and rate | 15 to 25 dB below a glove-on-body contact at the same distance, audible at every working distance of the camera, up to about 8 m; 25 to 40 breaths a minute early in the round, 40 to 55 after the middle and for 3 to 6 s after each landed body kick | Breath onsets per minute and LAeq over 5 s on the final mix. No breathing rate or breath level in combat sport was retained; the heart rates of 178 to 185 bpm measured after K1 rounds (R: `rydzik-2021` Tables 2–3, pp.3–4) show the intensity, not the breathing, so the values are choices | C |

## Sequence across the round {#sequence}

How the sound chains through the scene (C unless marked): the start bell over a waiting crowd; the glove touch, a light glove-on-glove pair; exchanges carried by footwork on the canvas, breaths, strike exhales and contacts whose classes change with what lands, the crowd rising and settling with each; heavier breathing as the round goes on and body contacts take their toll ([contacts before the finish](../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish)); the finish at 150.000, the heaviest contact, then the fall's canvas contacts and the crowd's surge; the referee's "Down" and the procedure words while A goes to the corner; the count from 158 to 167, intelligible over a held crowd; the knockout signal at 167.6 and a second surge; A's excited breath from 168.1; the match-ending bell at 170.1; the crowd and breaths to 180.

## Markers of the depicted level {#level}

- Professional officials work by rule-fixed words and cadence: one-second count intervals in a loud, clear voice with fingers (R: `ontario-pro-kickboxing-part-iii-2025` §17); the count waits for the opponent in the neutral corner (R: `glory-rules-2026-v2` §5.05.B.2); the referee practises counting (R: `abc-boxing-referee-manual-rev-2025-08` p.5) and avoids "catchy" phrases (p.4).
- Trained strikers exhale forcefully with committed strikes, which measurably increases kick force (R: `sinnett-2018-grunting` pp.3–4).
- Professional broadcast sound keeps the ring's own sounds in perspective with the picture (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶23) and catches footwork and contacts from close overheads (R: `svg-wilder-fury-2-audio-2020` ¶8).

## The invented elements and their analogues {#analogue}

The film's sound is not a broadcast: it has no commentary, no PA and no microphone in any fixed place. Its closest real analogue is Sky's mix that follows the camera's own microphone (R: `svge-joshua-klitschko-sky-2017` ¶23), heard without commentary. It departs (C) in having one continuous listening position, in silencing every voice but the referee's, and in giving contacts their distinct classes from inference rather than from a recording.
