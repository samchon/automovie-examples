# docs/research/kickboxing/exchanges.md: the exchanges of round one, their outcomes, tempo and the laws the vocabulary lacks

This is the screenplay's own study. It holds what the screenplay needs to decide each exchange beat of the round: which installed action performs each fighting, officiating and aftermath action, the outcome table, how exchanges chain, the tempo and distance targets, and the laws of the three movements the vocabulary does not perform: the Brazilian kick, the fall to the right side and the failed attempt to rise. It adds to the four studies already written and does not repeat them. The rule text is in the [rules study](rules.md), the two styles, the tell and the Brazilian kick as a set-up are in the [styles study](styles.md), the referee's procedure is in the [referee study](../officiating/referee.md), and the camera, the sound and the crowd are in the [fight-coverage study](../cinematography/fight-coverage.md). It is written under the research skill's [when to study](../../../../../../.agents/skills/story/planning/research.md#when-to-study) rule: the screenplay names the actions, so this study gathers their outcomes and the laws of those the vocabulary lacks, and nothing a later consumer alone would use.

## How to read this study {#reading}

Each observation carries its kind in brackets:

- **[observation]**: what the original's text states, read directly in the saved copy. For a fight report this is the reporter's account of what he saw. For a coaching text it is the coach's account of practice. For a paper it is the authors' statement of method or finding.
- **[measurement]**: a value with its unit, method and group that the original reports.
- **[inference]**: this study's reasoning from the cited facts, including arithmetic on cited values.
- **[choice]**: a decision this production makes where the sources leave room. A story decision among them is the screenplay's to adopt. A value for code, such as a joint angle, a duration in hundredths of a second or a distance in metres, is a working range the code that realises it may refine against the picture; it is never handed to the brief.
- **[commission]**: a value or ruling the commission fixes, such as an anchor second.

The vocabulary is the installed motion package, `packages/motions` (its [README](../../../../../../../packages/motions/README.md) and the declarations under `lib/`). It is cited as PKG with the action or table name. It is not a research original, so it carries no row in the index.

Sources are cited by key with a locator. Web articles are located by their heading or by the opening words of the paragraph. Papers are located by section, table or page. Most of the measured sources are amateur kickboxing, boxing, Muay Thai or general fall research; each value taken from them states its translation where it is used. No footage could be decoded on this machine, so nothing here is read from moving pictures, and every value only footage would give is marked as inferred, chosen or unavailable. Every original was retrieved on 2026-11-13 and is listed, with its creator, URL, saved path, rights and the locators used, in the research index's [exchanges section](../index.md#originals-exchanges).

## The vocabulary the round is written in {#vocabulary}

The screenplay names each action in fight words. This section says which installed action performs it and with which of its parameters, so that the screenplay can name an action the scenes can compose, and so that it knows which actions it names have no installed law. A movement the vocabulary performs is not restudied here: its timing is the package's, cited by name.

### Punches and punch feints {#vocab-punches}

Every punch is `punch` (PKG `punch`). Its technique is straight, hook or uppercut, its hand lead or rear, and its target a region of the other man [observation: PKG `punch.IParams`]. In fight words:

- **Jab**: a lead-hand straight to the head. **Cross**: a rear-hand straight to the head. **Hook**: a lead or rear hook to the head. **Uppercut**: an uppercut, rear hand unless the lead is named.
- **Body shots**: any of these aimed at a body region. The package lowers the body through the knees for a body punch [observation: PKG `punch`].
- **A punch that glances**: the same punch with its accuracy declared as a graze, which passes one weapon width beside the aim point and glances on [observation: PKG `StrikingQuality.Accuracy`]. **A punch that misses**: accuracy declared as a miss, or a target out of reach, which the package plays as the declared miss at maximum reach [observation: PKG `punch`].
- **A punch feint**: the same punch with a feint share, which shows that share of the delivery without contact and returns in the same time [observation: PKG `punch`].
- **A dip of the shoulders**: Visser's trunk feint before he steps in is a body-punch feint at a small share: the package lowers the body through the knees for a body punch, so the start of a body punch shown and returned is a dip of the shoulders and knees with little of the arm [inference: PKG `punch`]. It is a vocabulary action, not a departure.

Timing is the package's: a lone punch takes 0.405 s from onset to contact; a following punch lands 0.35 s after the last contact with the other hand or 0.45 s with the same hand; the hand returns to the guard in 0.35 s [measurement: PKG `StrikingSkill.JAB_DELIVERY`, `ALTERNATING_INTERVAL`, `SAME_HAND_INTERVAL`, `HAND_RECOVERY`].

### Kicks and kick feints {#vocab-kicks}

Every kick is `kick` (PKG `kick`), technique round or push, leg lead or rear [observation: PKG `kick.IParams`]:

- **Low kick**: a round kick to a thigh region. Outside and inside low kicks differ only by the thigh region aimed at.
- **Body kick**: a round kick to a rib or flank region. Benlloch's right body kick is a rear-leg round kick to Visser's left ribs.
- **High kick**: a round kick to a head region.
- **Push kick (teep)**: a push kick, lead leg by default, to a body region only, since a linear kick to the legs is a GLORY foul the package checks [observation: PKG `KickboxingRules.LEG_REGIONS`; rules study, [technique additions](rules.md#technique-additions)].
- **Kick feint**: a kick with the feint flag, which "shows its chamber and returns without extending" [observation: PKG `kick.IParams.feint`]. Benlloch's knee-rise feint is a rear-leg round kick feint. Visser's hip-snap feint of the low kick is a shallower kick feint; the package shows a fixed chamber, so a feint shorter than the chamber is the code's to compose [inference].

The round kick's timing is Gavagan and Sayers': preparation 0.074 s, chamber 14 % and extension 11 % of a 1.02 s execution, recoil 0.65 s, applied alike to low, body and high kicks [measurement: PKG `kick`, `StrikingSkill.KICK_*`]. The kick lands about 0.33 s after its preparation starts (0.074 + 0.143 + 0.112 s) and the foot is replanted about 0.65 s later [inference: arithmetic on PKG values].

### The kick redirect {#vocab-redirect}

A round kick may be cut before its chamber key and redirected into another round kick, "a middle kick switched to a high kick", the new kick planned whole from the source's clock and line and blended in from the cut to the contact [observation: PKG `KickRedirect`, `kick.interrupts`]. That is the package's nearest form of the Brazilian kick. It rises into the head: the high band's travel at contact is upward (vertical weight +0.1) with the least turnover of the three bands (azimuth 15°), because the rig's hip abducts at most 45° [observation: PKG `KickForm.TRAVEL`, `KickForm.AZIMUTH`]. The Brazilian kick's shin comes down onto the head, so its law is the departure recorded under [the Brazilian kick's law](#brazilian-kick-law).

### The knee and the clinch {#vocab-knee-clinch}

- **The clinch**: `clinch` with the two-handed neck tie (`plum`), both palms on the back of the other man's neck, held for a declared time and let go [observation: PKG `clinch`]. A clinch may be cut into a knee once the grip is closed, and into standing at any time [observation: PKG `clinch.interrupts` as compiled].
- **The knee**: `knee`, rear leg by default, at a body region; with a person target the hands go to either side of the other's nape [observation: PKG `knee`]. It lands at the round kick's chamber key, about 0.22 s after it starts, and recoils over 0.65 s [measurement: PKG `knee`, from `StrikingSkill`]. The package forbids a knee touching another strike of the same fighter, which is GLORY's one-knee clinch [observation: PKG `KickboxingRules`].
- **A tie-up** (arms tangled with no intent to hold) has no law of its own [observation: PKG `HumanActionName` lists none]. The screenplay names it as the men's arms meeting at close range; the code composes it [choice].

### The defences {#vocab-defend}

Every defence is `defend` against the anchor of one placed strike, with its kind and an optional forced outcome [observation: PKG `defend`]:

| Fight word | Kind | What it does (PKG `defend`) | Answers, at this level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cover | `cover` | The glove on the attacked side over the attacked head region, elbow down | Hooks and straight punches to the head |
| Parry | `parry` | The glove opposite the punching hand meets the fist a forearm ahead of the region and turns it aside | Straight punches only [inference: the law meets a fist on its line] |
| Elbow block | `elbowBlock` | The elbow on the attacked side dropped over the body region, glove kept at the jaw | Body punches and body kicks |
| High block | `highBlock` | Forearm braced beside the head, glove above the temple | High kicks and wide hooks |
| Slip | `slip` | Head moved off the line outside the punching arm | Straight punches |
| Roll | `roll` | Head dropped under the line by two head radii and up | Hooks |
| Sway | `sway` | Upper body leaned back over the rear foot until the head clears | Straight punches and high kicks at range |
| Check | `check` | Leg on the attacked side lifted, knee up and out, shin turned into the kick | Low kicks and, lifted higher, body kicks |

The "answers" column is this study's reading of the kinds against the coaching the styles study cites ([check and counter](styles.md#check-and-counter), [why a guard drops](styles.md#tell-formation)) [inference]. A defence holds until just after the strike's contact and returns to the guard over one hand recovery (0.35 s); a punch or another defence may follow it from the strike's contact on [observation: PKG `defend`, `defend.interrupts`]. That is the package's check-and-counter and block-and-counter.

Two departures bear on the screenplay:

1. **The degraded elbow block.** The vocabulary's elbow block keeps the glove at the jaw [observation: PKG `defend`]. Visser's tell in its forming and fixed stages takes the left glove down from the head with the elbow ([Visser](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#tell)). That cover has no installed form; the code composes it [inference].
2. **A defence against a feint.** `defend` refuses a strike that "has no contact or shown peak" [observation: PKG `defend.plan`]. A kick feint has no contact, so, as the declaration reads, Visser's drop at Benlloch's feint and Benlloch's check lifted to Visser's hip-snap feint cannot be written as `defend` [inference]. The screenplay still names them; the code realises them [choice].

### Footwork and guard {#vocab-footwork}

- **The stance**: `guard`, orthodox, facing the other man, set in 0.6 s, with a bounce through the knees at two a second while held [observation: PKG `guard`].
- **Steps**: `step` forward, back, left or right in the stance's own frame, the foot on the side of travel first, half a stance per step and 0.35 s per step [observation: PKG `step`]. Footwork may chain into strikes, defences and more footwork at any time [observation: PKG `step.interrupts`].
- **Step-in and step-back**: steps forward and back. A punch also steps in by up to one stance length when its target lies beyond the arm, and a kick steps its support foot up by up to half a stance [observation: PKG `punch`, `KickForm.STEP_SHARE`].
- **Circle**: lateral steps with the guard re-faced at the other man [inference]. **Angle-out** (Benlloch's 45-degree exit): a step left or right, or back and to the side, then the guard re-faced [inference]. **Cut-off** (Visser's): lateral steps that keep him square in front of the circling man, with steps forward between them ([styles study](styles.md#pressure-ring-and-tempo)) [inference].
- **Pivot**: the vocabulary has no pivot about the lead foot [observation: PKG `step.IParams` lists four directions]. The nearest form is `guard` re-faced at a new target, each foot stepping to its new stance place [observation: PKG `guard`]. A pivot is therefore a departure the code composes [inference].

### Flinch and stagger {#vocab-reactions}

- **Flinch**: `flinch` away from a threat or a blow, peaking at 0.2 s and returning by 0.6 s, its size scaled by an intensity in (0, 1] [measurement: PKG `flinch`, from Brown et al. 1991]. It is the vocabulary's reaction to a feint that draws one, and the small give of the head and trunk under a glancing blow [choice].
- **Stagger**: `stagger`, one unplanned capture step after a push, landing at 0.35 s and settled by 0.8 s [measurement: PKG `stagger`, from Maki and McIlroy 1997]. It is the step back after the strike behind the feint, and the step a landed push kick forces [choice]. A push the package judges uncapturable in one step is refused and named a knockdown [observation: PKG `stagger`], so before 150 s the screenplay never asks for more than one step [inference, under the commission's [contacts before the finish](../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish)].

### The glove touch {#vocab-glove-touch}

The glove touch is the vocabulary's mutual meeting: `highFive` with its fist variant, in which both men bring one hand to one shared point on the floor midpoint between them, at the mean of their chest heights, meet at one shared instant, stay 0.1 s and go back [observation: PKG `highFive`, built on `AutoMovieMotionMeeting`]. It is a one-glove touch. A two-glove touch, both gloves of each man meeting, has no installed form [observation: PKG `highFive.IParams` takes one side]. The commission asks only for "an actual glove touch" ([glove touch](../../instructions/commission.md#glove-touch)), so the one-glove fist meeting satisfies it [inference]. The handshake is the other mutual meeting, but it closes a grip a gloved hand cannot make [inference]. Both men then take `guard`; the first exchange may start once both gloves are back ([styles study](styles.md#glove-touch)).

### Benlloch after the finish {#vocab-benlloch-after}

- **The walk to the neutral corner**: `walk` to the far neutral corner with via points round Visser's feet, at the slow or normal pace, and an arrival heading facing the centre [observation: PKG `walk.IParams`: `via`, `pace`, `face`]. At a 0.93 m hip the normal pace is 1.35 m/s and the slow 1.05 m/s [measurement: PKG `walk`, from Bohannon 1997], against the rules study's 0.8–1.3 m/s average ([anchors](rules.md#anchors), tension 1).
- **The celebration**: `selfAction` victory (both arms raised overhead outside the shoulders) or fist pump, and `gesture` cheer (both fists raised and pumped) [observation: PKG `selfAction`, `gestureStroke`]. The steps are `walk` at the fast pace with a raised arm-swing scale, or `step` [choice]. A self action may be cut into a walk only from its relax [observation: PKG `selfAction.interrupts` as compiled], and a walk carries only its own arm swing. So walking with the gloves held up is a departure the code composes; the vocabulary alone gives raised arms while standing, then steps [inference].

### The downed man {#vocab-downed}

- **Lying on a side**: `lieDown` to the right side. Its route from standing is sit, then supine, then onto the side, each phase 1.2 s, ending in a side lie with hip 40°, knee 60°, shoulder 70° and elbow 60° [observation: PKG `lieDown.ROUTES`, `POSES`, as compiled]. It is a willed lying down, not a fall.
- **Hands and knees**: `crawl`, whose first phase gets the body onto hands and knees over 1.5 s; it may cut a settled `lieDown` [observation: PKG `crawl`, `lieDown.interrupts`, as compiled]. Its cancel table is empty: nothing may interrupt a crawl [observation: PKG `crawl.interrupts`].
- **Kneeling**: `kneel` goes down from standing and rises the way it went [observation: PKG `kneel`].
- **Rising**: `rise` is standing up from a seat [observation: PKG `rise`]. No installed action rises from the floor; `lieDown` and `crawl` list rising from the floor as not yet built [observation: PKG `lieDown`, `crawl`].
- **The knockdown**: `knockdown` folds the legs and lies the body supine away from the blow; its cancel table is empty [observation: PKG `knockdown`].

So the vocabulary has no action for a man on the floor who tries to rise and fails. The nearest analogues are crawl's first phase (from a lie to hands and knees) and kneel's lowered state, but neither starts from a fall's resting pose, and neither may be cut back down [inference]. Their law is in [failing to rise](#failing-to-rise).

### What the vocabulary lacks {#vocab-lacks}

The actions the screenplay names that the installed vocabulary does not perform, each with where this study gives its law or leaves it to the code:

1. The Brazilian kick, whose shin descends over the arm onto the side of the head and neck: law in [the Brazilian kick's law](#brazilian-kick-law).
2. The fall to the right side from a standing fighter struck on the left side of the head: law in [the fall](#fall-law).
3. The conscious failed attempt to rise, and stillness without limpness: law in [failing to rise](#failing-to-rise).
4. Visser's degraded elbow block with the glove leaving the head, fired at the rising knee and at a feint: [vocabulary, defences](#vocab-defend); its formation is the styles study's ([tell](styles.md#tell-formation)).
5. A pivot as one footwork action: [footwork](#vocab-footwork).
6. A tie-up: [knee and clinch](#vocab-knee-clinch).
7. The referee's knockout signal, and his gestures and touches from a kneel: [the referee](#vocab-referee).
8. Steps with the gloves held up: [Benlloch after the finish](#vocab-benlloch-after).
9. The "downed" state for the no-downed-attack rule. The package treats a performer as down "between the start of their knockdown and the start of their next action" [observation: PKG `KickboxingRules`]. A fall written as anything other than `knockdown` leaves that rule unchecked unless the code marks it [inference]. No strike follows the finish in this round ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#ruleset)), so the rule is kept by the screenplay whatever the code does [inference].
10. A cornerman's coaching gesture made from his seat. The vocabulary's `gesture` is performed standing, with the feet planted, and a seated performer may leave the seat only by standing [observation: PKG `gesture`, `sit.interrupts`]. The brief keeps the cornermen seated during the round ([ringside](../../body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#corners-seated)), so a raised hand or a point to the head from the seat is a departure the code composes, the gesture's arm and hand over a seated body [inference].
11. The timekeeper's strike of the bell from his seat. The vocabulary strikes with a hammer through `useTool`, which works its point while standing [observation: PKG `useTool`]. The brief seats the timekeeper at the officials' table with the bell in front of him ([ringside](../../body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#officials-table)), so the single strike at 0.000 and the three quick strikes at 170.1 from the seat are a departure the code composes, the tool's arm stroke over a seated body [inference].
12. The referee's step round the fallen man's head in a crouch. The vocabulary's `crouch` is held in place over planted feet, and `kneel` goes down from standing [observation: PKG `crouch`, `kneel`]. Moving from one kneeling place to another round Visser's head, crouched and without standing up fully ([referee](../../body/brief/cast/referee.md#knockdown), step 7), is a departure the code composes from crouch, short steps and kneel [inference].
13. The doctor's hand on the fallen man from a kneel, and his head bent close. As for the referee in item 7, `touch` is performed standing [observation: PKG `touch`], so the doctor's touch to Visser's shoulder while kneeling, and his lowered head held near Visser's, are a departure the code composes [inference].
## Exchange outcome table {#outcomes}

The outcome classes the screenplay may use. Each row gives the intents and timings that yield it, the class it takes in the vocabulary's defence law and in GLORY's rule outcomes, and its visible consequence, tied to the brief's state records.

The vocabulary decides a defence from timing alone: the defender starts at launch plus a reaction time less his anticipation lead times the strike's telegraph, and the margin to the strike's contact makes the defence in time (in place at contact), late (the strike "lands through the half-made guard") or a fail (it lands "as if undefended") [observation: PKG `DefenceOutcome`]. The screenplay may force any of the three, and the package realises it with plausible timing [observation: PKG `DefenceOutcome.decide`]. A round kick's chamber shows most (telegraph 1.0), a push kick or knee 0.8, a rear-hand punch 0.6 and a jab 0.4 [observation: PKG `defend.telegraph`, provisional]. An elite defender's simple reaction time is about 0.29 s and his anticipation lead 0.08–0.15 s [measurement: PKG `StrikingSkill.reactionLaw`, `anticipation`]. The rule classes are the rules study's ([rule-outcome table](rules.md#outcomes)): legal landed strike, blocked strike, foul, clinch with one knee, knockdown and knockout.

Before 150 s no outcome is a knockdown, a standing count or a stop by the referee to look at a man; head contacts are glancing or taken on the guard ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish); [bout](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#costs)) [commission].

| Outcome | Intents and timings | Classes | Visible consequence | State record |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A punch lands clean on the body | A body punch thrown as the defender is planted or committed to his own strike; no defence, or the defence fails | Defence fail; legal landed strike | A heavier, duller thud and a forced breath; the elbow on that side clamps; the struck man steps out on an angle or does not press for a step or two | [Benlloch, after a punch to the body](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#state); [bout, costs](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#costs) |

Three notes on the table:

- **Why body kicks land early and the cover fails at the finish.** Against a round kick fully telegraphed, an elite defender starts 0.14–0.21 s after the kick's launch (0.29 s less a 0.08–0.15 s lead) and his cover is in place 0.20 s later, at about 0.34–0.41 s [inference: PKG `DefenceOutcome` law with `StrikingSkill.reactionLaw`, `anticipation` and `defence` at elite]. Against a body kick landing at about 0.33 s that is late by 0.01–0.08 s, inside the late band (half the 0.20 s movement time); the package's own note is that "a purely reactive defence cannot be in time against a fast kick" [observation: PKG `DefenceOutcome`]. So early in the round Benlloch's body kicks land through a half-made cover, and Visser's cover is in time only once it starts at the rising knee, as a learned guess before the kick's line shows ([styles, anticipation](styles.md#anticipation-evidence)) [inference]. The screenplay forces "in time" for the blocked body kicks; the package then realises the outcome with plausible timing or names the shortfall in a warning [observation: PKG `DefenceOutcome.decide`]. At the finish, once his elbow is going down, a correction to the head needs a second reaction to the turnover he sees at about 0.22–0.30 s, so no glove can be back at the head before about 0.7 s, while the Brazilian kick lands at about 0.40–0.55 s ([timing](#bq-timing)) [inference].
- **Low kicks and the planted leg.** GLORY's coach advises kicking a puncher "every time he tries to attack you with punches", when "he needs to plant his feet heavy" ([styles, what it costs him](styles.md#pressure-costs)). So Benlloch's landed low kicks come during Visser's combinations, and Visser's checks come when Benlloch kicks first [inference].
- **Rule classes.** A blocked strike is not scored and play continues; a tie-up is the round's one foul and its caution is the "Break!" itself ([bout, clinch](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#clinch)); no deliberate foul occurs ([bout, never done](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#never-done)) [observation of the brief].

## Chains and set-ups {#chains}

The style-level patterns are the styles study's: Visser's combinations finished by low kicks and his cut-off ([pressure repertoire](styles.md#pressure-repertoire), [ring use and tempo](styles.md#pressure-ring-and-tempo)), Benlloch's kick at range, check and counter, angle-out and range-finding cross ([kicker repertoire](styles.md#kicker-repertoire), [check and counter](styles.md#check-and-counter), [angles, pivots and feints](styles.md#kicker-angles-feints), [how he answers pressure](styles.md#kicker-answers-pressure)), and the tell's formation, test and exploitation ([tell](styles.md#tell)). This section adds how long each chain takes in the vocabulary, and how one exchange hands the next a changed situation.

### What one exchange is {#exchange-unit}

Ouergui's notation defines the unit the tempo targets count: a high-intensity action starts "when 1 foot or hand moved to initiate the attack" and ends when "the foot/hand that delivered the last kick/punch of the action returned to the floor/guard", when the limb was retracted, when a knockdown followed or when the referee signalled stop (OUERGUI, Procedures, criteria a.) [observation]. Everything between is low-intensity activity, "stable positions or movements" such as footwork ("step, slide, bounce") and deceptive movements ("foot, head/body, or hand feint") (OUERGUI, Procedures, criteria b.; Table 1, p. 3539) [observation]. This study uses the same unit: an exchange beat is one such action by either man or both, with its defence and counter; a feint alone belongs to the observation between exchanges, and a feint followed at once by a strike belongs to the exchange it opens [choice].

### Visser's chains {#visser-chains}

- **Combination finished by a low kick.** Jab, cross, hook and a right low kick: the punches land at 0, 0.35 and 0.70 s; the hook returns to the guard by 1.05 s; the kick launches then and lands at about 1.38 s; the foot is replanted at about 2.0 s, so the whole exchange takes about 2.4 s from the jab's start [inference: PKG `StrikingSkill` intervals; a kick follows a punch once the punch has returned, since the punch's cancel table names only punches]. That sits inside Ouergui's 2.2 ± 1.2 s ([tempo](#tempo)). Shorter families, 1–2–low kick or hook–low kick, take about 1.6–2.0 s [inference]. He never repeats one twice running ([Visser](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#repertoire)).
- **Body work.** A body hook or cross inside the combination lowers the body through the knees and draws Benlloch's elbow down, which opens the head for the next punch ([styles](styles.md#pressure-repertoire)) [inference].
- **Cut-off to the ropes.** Lateral steps square in front of Benlloch, closing slowly, take 1–3 steps a phase of 0.35 s each between exchanges [inference: PKG `step.PERIOD`; [styles](styles.md#pressure-ring-and-tempo)]. A cut-off is the observation time before a combination, not an exchange itself [choice].
- **The clinch knee on the ropes.** With Benlloch's back to the ropes and Visser inside after a combination: grip, one rear knee to the body, release. From the grip to the release takes 0.8–1.5 s [choice: the knee lands about 0.22 s after it starts and recoils over 0.65 s (PKG `knee`); the grip's reach and the release are added]. The rules study left the duration of a single knee to this study ([clinch](rules.md#clinch)).
- **His feints.** A jab feint or a dip of the shoulders before he steps in; a hip-snap feint of the low kick so that Benlloch lifts his check, then a low kick to the leg as it lands ([Visser](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#repertoire); [styles, testing the reaction](styles.md#tell-test)). The kick to the landing leg launches as the checking foot comes down and lands about 0.33 s later, while that leg takes weight [inference: PKG `kick` timing].
- **His answer to a kick.** A check or a step, then his own low kick to Benlloch's landing leg ([styles](styles.md#pressure-repertoire)).

### Benlloch's chains {#benlloch-chains}

- **Kick at range, then out.** A right body kick or a low kick as Visser steps in, then a step off at 45 degrees and the guard re-faced; the exchange lasts about 1.0–1.5 s, the exit another 0.35–0.7 s [inference: PKG `kick` and `step` timing; [styles](styles.md#kicker-answers-pressure)].
- **Range-finding cross into the right kick.** A rear-hand cross that touches as it returns, then the right kick from the same side: the cross lands at about 0.4 s, returns by 0.75 s and the kick lands about 0.33 s after it launches, near 1.1 s [inference: PKG timings; [styles, range-finding](styles.md#kicker-repertoire)]. It is a real strike, landed glancing or on the guard.
- **Check and counter.** A check in time, held until just after the kick's contact, then a cross launched from that contact that lands about 0.4 s later, while the kicker is still in his 0.65 s recoil [inference: PKG `defend.interrupts` and `kick` recoil; [styles, check and counter](styles.md#check-and-counter)]. Or an inside low kick to Visser's support leg as his kicking foot lands.
- **Short combinations when the pressure stalls.** Two or three punches, then out on an angle ([Benlloch](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#repertoire)).
- **The feint, then the strike.** The knee rises as for the right body kick (the chamber key comes about 0.22 s after the kick's start), the foot comes back down, and a strike to the left side of Visser's head lands within the drop. Which strike is the screenplay's; a right cross launched as the foot replants lands about 0.6–0.8 s after the knee began to rise [inference: PKG `kick` feint and `punch` timing]. The drop must still be open then; the brief's stage 3 makes the drop follow every rise of the knee ([Visser's tell](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#tell)).
- **The finish** is the [Brazilian kick](#brazilian-kick-law).

### Resets {#resets}

A reset is the state the camera may cut on ([camera](../../body/brief/cinematography/camera.md#cuts)): both men apart, out of range, back in guard and re-squared to each other. Three things make one [choice]:

1. One man steps or angles out beyond kicking range after an exchange, and both re-face.
2. The referee's "Break!" after a tie-up: both step back, he steps between and out, and the round resumes as he steps back ([referee study](../officiating/referee.md#clinch-break)).
3. A pause in pressure after a landed body kick, when Visser does not press for a step or two ([Visser](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#state)).

Ouergui's referee pauses lasted 5.4 ± 4.5 s, but WAKO's referee stops the round with a hand signal and restarts it with "begin" (OUERGUI, Procedures, criteria c.; Table 2) [measurement]. GLORY resumes without a command as the referee steps back ([rules study](rules.md#clinch)), so this round's break is shorter, 2–4 s [inference; choice].

### How one exchange hands the next a changed situation {#handover}

The commission asks that each phase hand the next a changed situation ([progression](../../instructions/commission.md#progression)). At the scale of the exchange, each outcome changes at least one of four things the next exchange starts from [choice]:

| What changes | Changed by | Next exchange starts from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distance and place in the ring | Cut-offs and step-ins close; angle-outs, push kicks and breaks open; a combination that lands drives the other man back toward the ropes | The new range ([distance](#tempo-distance)) and the new place relative to ropes and corners ([ring](../../body/brief/sets/ring.md#use)) |
| Who leads | A landed body kick takes Visser's pressure for a step or two; a checked kick gives the checker the counter; a stalled combination lets Benlloch throw | The man with the initiative throws first |
| The defender's readiness | A planted lead leg, a drawn check, a dropped elbow, a turned head; each lasts 0.2–0.6 s after the strike | The follow-up or counter aimed at that opening |
| What each man has learned | Each landed right body kick moves the tell a stage on; the feint proves it; each check makes the kicker kick less | The next phase's choice of strike ([Visser's tell](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#tell), [Benlloch's knowledge](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#knowledge)) |

An exchange whose outcome changes none of these is a repeat, which the commission's "do not loop the first minute" forbids at the scale of the phase [inference].

### The length of an exchange {#tempo-length}

High-intensity actions at the WAKO world championships lasted 2.2 ± 1.2 s, and 2.1 ± 1.2 s in round one (OUERGUI, Table 2, p. 3539) [measurement]. The same work reported that "kickboxers tend to use combined actions" in later rounds, lengthening each sequence (OUERGUI, Discussion, p. 3542) [observation]. Translation: WAKO full-contact is amateur, with 2-minute rounds and no low kicks (styles study, [departures](styles.md#conflicts)); low kicks and knees add up to one kick's execution, about 1 s, to a GLORY exchange [inference]. Target: **one exchange 1.0–3.5 s**, a single kick at the short end and a four-strike combination finished by a low kick at about 2.4 s ([Visser's chains](#visser-chains)); a clinch knee with its grip and release 0.8–1.5 s [choice].

### Idle and observation time {#tempo-idle}

Low-intensity activity lasted 2.2 ± 0.7 s, and 2.4 ± 0.8 s in round one; the interval between two consecutive high-intensity actions was 3.4 ± 1.2 s, and 3.2 ± 1.2 s in round one (OUERGUI, Table 2) [measurement]. Fighting took about 40 % of the time and non-fighting, with pauses, 60 % (OUERGUI, Discussion, p. 3541) [measurement]. Target: **1–5 s between exchanges**, filled with footwork, feints, the bounce, breath and attention; **no stretch longer than about 6 s without an exchange** except a reset after a break or after a landed body kick [choice]. In boxing, the boxers spent 85.7 ± 15.8 s of a 180 s round one interacting, punching or defending, and 11.1 ± 10.0 s in clinches (DUNN, Table 3) [measurement]; translated, the share of a round spent in contact is near half, as Ouergui's 40 % [inference].

### The high to low intensity ratio {#tempo-ratio}

The ratio of high-intensity time to low-intensity and pause time was 0.7 ± 0.4, which the authors report as 1:1, the same in all weight classes and rounds (OUERGUI, Table 2; Results) [measurement]. The review of the field found "The activity-to-rest ratio in amateur kickboxing matches has been ranging from 1:2, with an average of 6 seconds of effort and 12 seconds for pauses [ 2 ], to 1:5 in official and simulated amateur and national-level combats" (SLIMANI, "Activity profile", para. 1) [observation]. Translation: the elite value is the depicted level; the amateur ratios mark what a club round looks like [inference]. Target: **high to low about 1:1, between 1:1.5 and 1:0.7 per phase** [choice].

### How round one differs {#tempo-round-one}

- At the WAKO world championships round one had the longest low-intensity time (65.1 ± 10.2 s against 58.0 and 56.5 s), the most high-intensity actions (27.1 ± 7.1 against 25.1 and 24.9) and the fewest pauses (2.2 ± 1.8, totalling 12.8 ± 11.4 s, against 22.3 and 24.6 s) (OUERGUI, Tables 3 and 4, p. 3540) [measurement]. The authors read the low-intensity time as a round-one "strategy based on the observation of opponent's movements before initiating attacks" (OUERGUI, Discussion, p. 3542) [observation].
- In the GLORY 14 bout round one was the quietest of three, 40 strikes against 55 and 75 (GLORY-BTN14) [measurement].
- In boxing round one had the shortest clinch time and the least guard drop (DUNN, Table 3; Results) [measurement].
- Against these, K-1 athletes "often want to terminate the fight quickly in the first round" (RYDZIK, Discussion, cited in [styles](styles.md#pressure-ring-and-tempo)) [observation], and the Dutch pressure fighter works hard from the bell ([styles](styles.md#pressure-ring-and-tempo)).

So round one is the round of reading and of many short exchanges, with few tie-ups [inference]. For this film: Benlloch reads while Visser presses, which yields many short exchanges early, Visser's longer combinations from the second minute, and at most one or two tie-ups ([bout, clinch](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#clinch)) [choice].

### How many exchanges a 15-second phase can hold {#tempo-phase}

From Ouergui's round one, 27.1 ± 7.1 high-intensity actions in 120 s of running time is 2.5–4.3 per 15 s, and one action every 3.2 ± 1.2 s is 3–7 per 15 s at the extremes (OUERGUI, Tables 2 and 4) [inference: arithmetic]. With GLORY exchanges about a second longer for their low kicks ([length](#tempo-length)) [inference], the target per 15-s phase is [choice]:

- **2–5 exchange beats**, with **3–4 typical**;
- **at most 2** in a phase that holds a tie-up and its break, or the long walk-out and glove touch of phase one (0–15 s), where the bell, "Fight", the walk to the centre and the touch take the first 4–7 s;
- **the strikes in them**: 4–8 thrown by both men, as above;
- **phase ten (135–150 s)** ends in the finish at 150.000 s and holds the feint, which must come late enough that the tell is fixed and early enough to be seen proved before the finish ([Benlloch's knowledge](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#knowledge)).

Across 150 s that makes about 25–40 exchanges in all, against Ouergui's 34 for 150 s of a round one by straight scaling [inference].

## The Brazilian kick's law {#brazilian-kick-law}

The styles study holds what the kick is, why it beats a guard drawn low, what its chamber shows and the knockouts it has produced ([the Brazilian kick](styles.md#brazilian-kick); [what it is](styles.md#brazilian-what), [why it works](styles.md#brazilian-why), [the chamber](styles.md#brazilian-chamber), [knockouts](styles.md#brazilian-knockouts), [the contact](styles.md#brazilian-contact)). This section adds only its law: the phases and key poses in Benlloch's body frame, the contact surfaces, support and weight, timing against the vocabulary's round kick, and the travel from kicking range.

The body frame: Benlloch stands orthodox, left foot forward; his right leg is the kicking leg and his left the support. Forward is along the line toward Visser; his right is the kicking side [choice].

### Phases and key poses {#bq-phases}

1. **Preparation, as the body kick's.** The weight goes onto the left foot and the right foot leaves the canvas, as in Gavagan and Sayers' preparation phase, "from toe down of the support leg until toe off of the kicking leg" (GAVAGAN, Methods, kick phases) [observation]. Nothing here differs from his right body kick, and it must not ([Benlloch](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#brazilian-kick)) [choice].
2. **The body-kick chamber.** The right knee rises across the body toward Visser's left ribs, the heel folded toward the thigh (the package's chamber, knee about 115° flexed), the left heel lifting and the support foot beginning its turn on its ball [observation: PKG `KickForm.CHAMBER_FLEXION`, `KickForm.PIVOT`]. This is the cue on which Visser's elbow drops ([Visser's tell](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#tell)).
3. **The knee rises higher and the support foot turns further.** The knee continues upward past the body kick's height toward the level of Visser's shoulder, as the coaches put it: "As you bring your knee up high, turn your front foot quickly to the side. This opens up your hips and enables you to rotate your kick over" (LK-QM, "The back leg question mark kick") [observation]; "Bring your knee up and pivot your front foot to open up your hips as you throw the kick" (EV-QM, section 1) [observation]. "Quickly to the side" puts the support toes turned about 90° or more from the line, beyond the package's 60° support turn (PKG `KickForm.PIVOT`) [inference]. This is the knee "moving in a different direction" that a trained eye can catch (MTG-QM, section "Deceiving your opponent with the teep") [observation].
4. **The hip turns over.** The pelvis rolls over the support leg toward the left, so the right hip comes above the left and the thigh points over Visser's lowered arm rather than at his ribs [inference from LK-QM's "rotate your kick over"]. For scale, in taekwondo roundhouse kicks "the pelvis tilted posteriorly and laterally through approximately 60 deg, and rotated through approximately 130 deg" (GAVAGAN, Discussion, citing Kim et al.) [measurement]. The Brazilian kick tilts further than a round kick to bring the shin above the head; no source measures how far ([scope](#scope)) [inference]. The trunk leans back and away to the left to balance the raised leg [inference; PKG `KickForm.LEAN_MOST` gives the package's most lean as 40°].
5. **The lower leg descends.** From the turned-over chamber the knee extends and the shin and instep come down and across, over Visser's lowered left arm and onto the side of his head and neck below and just behind the left ear ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#finish)). The coaches call it a snap: "The question mark kick requires you to snap your kick karate style" (EV-QM, intro para. 3); the thrower "whips the foot higher to throw the kick now aimed for the head" (SK-ALLAZOV, para. beginning "A question mark kick is") [observation]. Feitosa "brought his leg down in a sort of slingshot fashion" onto the area "behind Williams' right ear" (SH-USA05, p. 1, Williams semi-final) [observation]. In a snap the knee extension is fast: karate experts' roundhouse kicks extended the knee at −947 ± 94 deg/s against −706 ± 200 deg/s for Muay Thai experts (GAVAGAN, Abstract) [measurement].
6. **Contact.** The surfaces are the instep and the lower shin ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#finish)). Gavagan and Sayers defined the same contact area for their head-height kicks, "the dorsal surface of the foot to the mid-anterior surface of the shin", with the pad "positioned at the height of each participant's mastoid process" (GAVAGAN, Methods; Abstract) [observation], which is the height of the commission's landing site [inference]. The package's round kick lands with the lower shin seven tenths of the way from the knee (PKG `KickForm.SHIN_SHARE`); the Brazilian kick lands lower, the instep and the shin just above it [inference]. The coaching sources land it with the ball of the foot on the chin; the commission's landing is kept ([styles, the contact](styles.md#brazilian-contact)).
7. **Follow-through, recoil and landing.** A snap recoils along its path rather than kicking through (EV-QM, intro para. 3: "Muay Thai fighters typically kick through their target instead of using their legs as whips") [observation]. In the finish the leg comes down past the head with the falling man and is re-chambered, and the right foot replants under Benlloch's hips, near where it left or a half step forward on the line; he lands balanced and in guard, as his level requires ([Benlloch](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#level)) [choice].

### Support and weight {#bq-support}

All of the kick is thrown from the ball of the left foot, the heel raised (the package's support heel raise is 15°) and the foot turned further than for the body kick [observation: PKG `KickForm.PIVOT`; inference for the further turn]. The centre of mass stays over the support foot: the package carries the root a set share of the way from its entry toward a place over the support ankle at contact, so that the centre of mass stays inside the support foot's length (PKG `KickForm.CENTRE_SHARE`) [observation]. Expert kickers move their centre of mass upward during the extension, Muay Thai experts at 1.24 ± 0.15 m/s against 0.78–0.93 m/s for karate and taekwondo (GAVAGAN, Abstract) [measurement]. For a downward kick the rise comes earlier, in the knee's lift, and the body settles as the shin descends [inference]. The guard stays up through the kick, as the package keeps both gloves in guard during every kick (PKG `kick`) and as Benlloch's level requires; the right glove may drop toward the hip as a counterweight only by the code's choice [choice].

### Timing against the round kick {#bq-timing}

No measured timing of a question-mark kick was found ([scope](#scope)). The law is therefore built on the package's round kick and the coaching cues [inference; choice]:

| Phase | Round kick (PKG, from Gavagan and Sayers) | Brazilian kick, working range [choice] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation | 0.074 s | 0.074 s, identical |
| Chamber to the body-kick key | 0.143 s (to about 0.22 s) | identical (to about 0.22 s) |
| Knee higher, support turn, hip over | none | 0.10–0.20 s more |
| Extension to contact | 0.112 s (contact about 0.33 s) | 0.08–0.12 s, the snap (contact about 0.40–0.55 s) |
| Recoil to replant | 0.65 s | 0.6–0.9 s (karate recoil in GAVAGAN was 0.85 ± 0.307 s against Muay Thai 0.65 ± 0.173 s; Results) |

The round kick's phases are measured: chamber 14 %, extension 11 %, preparation 6 % and recoil 69 % of execution, and Muay Thai preparation 0.074 ± 0.018 s (GAVAGAN, Results) [measurement]. The coaches' half-second pause with the knee up (LK-QM, "Tips"; [styles](styles.md#brazilian-chamber)) is a training cue for selling the fake [observation]. Against a cover already committed it is not needed, and a pause would give a reacting defender time to bring the glove back ([outcomes, notes](#outcomes)); the finish has none [choice].

So against the commission's 150.000 s contact, the kick starts (support toe-down) at about 149.45–149.60 s, the knee reaches the body-kick chamber at about 149.67–149.82 s, Visser's elbow is going down from then, and the shin lands at 150.000 s [inference from the table]. The camera holds from the chamber's start to the end of the fall without a cut ([camera](../../body/brief/cinematography/camera.md#cuts)).

### Travel from kicking range {#bq-travel}

Benlloch throws it from the distance of the body kick Visser has learned to expect: kicking range ([distance](#tempo-distance)) [choice]. The package's kick reaches a target beyond its band by stepping the support foot up by up to half a stance (PKG `KickForm.STEP_SHARE`) [observation]; the Brazilian kick's support foot turns in place on its ball and may slide a few centimetres forward with the turn, but does not step [choice]. A Muay Thai kicker starts "closer to the target, reducing the distance travelled by the kicking leg" (GAVAGAN, Discussion) [observation]. At the finish Visser stands near the centre, just on the steps' side of the white–black diagonal, and Benlloch stands at kicking range from him toward the steps' corner ([ring](../../body/brief/sets/ring.md#corners)) [choice]. Benlloch's walk from there round Visser's feet to the far neutral corner is then about 6.5–7.5 m: kicking range plus Visser's small offset from the centre, the 4.3–4.6 m from the centre to a standing place in the far corner of the 7.00 m ring, and the detour round the feet [inference: arithmetic on [distance](#tempo-distance) and the rules study's [ring](rules.md#ring)]. From the call at about 151.5 s to 157 s, leaving time for his turn in the corner, that is about 1.3–1.5 m/s, between the vocabulary's normal walk (1.35 m/s) and its fast walk (1.71 m/s) ([Benlloch after the finish](#vocab-benlloch-after)): a steady, brisk walk, not a hurry, and above the rules study's estimate for a man starting at the centre ([anchors](rules.md#anchors), tension 1) [inference].

### Departure from the package {#bq-departure}

The package's redirect turns a middle kick into a high kick that rises onto the head with little turnover, its travel at contact upward and its azimuth 15° because the rig's hip abducts at most 45° (PKG `KickRedirect`, `KickForm.TRAVEL`, `KickForm.AZIMUTH`) [observation]. The Brazilian kick departs in three ways [inference]:

1. Its travel at contact is downward and across, not upward.
2. It turns over further: the support foot turns about 90° or more against the package's 60°, and the pelvis rolls over the support leg so the thigh rises above the target through the pelvis's tilt rather than the hip's abduction alone.
3. Its knee extension is a snap, faster than the round kick's, with a recoil along its path.

The redirect's mechanism, a blend from a body-kick plan cut before its chamber key into a second complete plan, fits the Brazilian kick's first half exactly; only the second plan's carriage and travel differ. That is the code's to build [inference].

## The fall to the right side {#fall-law}

The articulated fall of a fighter struck on the left side of the head by a high kick, from the loss of support to rest on his right side. It is studied as movement only. No cause of the fall is stated or implied, as the commission forbids ([no injury mechanism](../../instructions/commission.md#no-injury-mechanism)).

### What fight reports show {#fall-reports}

- Head kicks drop men at once and turn them as they go. Brestovac's head kick: "The towering Egyptian fell to the canvas instantly" (GLORY-BRES, para. beginning "The towering Egyptian") [observation]. Bonjasky's head kick "actually causes him to pivot mid-air before hitting the canvas" (GLORY-BONJ, para. beginning "Here we see") [observation].
- The direction varies with the kick. Beztati's right head kick: Cabri "toppled forward to the canvas" (GLORY-BEZ, para. beginning "Cabri ate the blow") [observation]. Feitosa's downward kick behind the ear sent Williams "to the canvas" (SH-USA05, p. 1) [observation]; Goodridge, kicked on the right side of the head, went "crumbling into the ropes" (SH-USA05, p. 1, the final) [observation].
- None of the retrieved reports describes the order in which the body met the canvas; that would need footage, which could not be decoded ([scope](#scope)) [observation].

### What fall research shows {#fall-research}

- In experimentally induced sideways falls from a 10° lean onto a crash pad, young adults were told "to land on the hip first by restraining from landing on the hand first, taking a step, or kneeling" (WOOD, Methods 2.2) [observation]. "The young group underwent 88 falls, in which 11.4% resulted in head impact" (WOOD, Abstract) [measurement]; so in about nine sideways falls in ten a young adult's head did not reach the pad [inference]. Translation: these are expected, instructed falls by untrained young adults; a fighter's fall from a blow is neither expected nor instructed [inference].
- The package's knockdown sits the seat on the floor at 0.45 s, "about the free-fall time of the pelvis from its height slowed by the buckling legs", and lays the back on the floor by 1.1 s, "the arms trailing" [measurement: PKG `knockdown`, from Maki and McIlroy 1997 for the buckle]. A pelvis about 0.93–0.95 m high falls freely to the floor in about 0.44 s [inference: t = √(2h/g)].

### The phases {#fall-phases}

Times are from the contact at 150.000 s. They are a working range for the code, built on the package's knockdown timing and on free fall [choice]:

1. **The head and trunk turned by the blow (0–0.15 s).** The shin's travel at contact is across, from Visser's left to his right, and down (the package's round kick arrives "mostly across, slightly forward", PKG `KickForm.TRAVEL`; the Brazilian kick adds the downward component) [inference]. His head is pushed and turned toward his right and down, his trunk follows it, and his lowered left arm stays low; his right glove does not rise to the blow [choice]. The camera reads this as the head moving away from the shin, which is how the landing point reads at all ([work, what it cannot show](../../body/brief/world/work.md#what-it-cannot-show)).
2. **The knees buckle (0.1–0.45 s).** Both knees and hips fold, the right leg, now under the weight, folding first; the pelvis drops behind and to the right of the feet. His feet stay where they stood until the body is low, as in the package's buckle (PKG `knockdown`) [inference; choice]. It is a buckle, not a dive or a step: he takes no capture step, since a push the package cannot catch in one step is named a knockdown (PKG `stagger`) [inference].
3. **The body falls away from the blow (0.3–0.8 s).** The trunk tips to his right, turning a little so that his right side leads and his front stays toward where Benlloch stands, which leaves his back toward the far neutral corner, as the ring record requires ([ring](../../body/brief/sets/ring.md#corners)) [choice]. The pivot is the kind the GLORY report describes (GLORY-BONJ) [inference].
4. **The parts that meet the canvas, in order (0.45–1.0 s).** The right knee or the outside of the right thigh first, then the right hip, then the right side of the trunk with the right shoulder and upper arm under it, the right forearm lying along the canvas in front of or beside the head, and the head last, coming down onto the right arm or just clear of the canvas [choice, built on the hip-first sideways fall of WOOD and the package's seat-first knockdown]. The left arm trails: already lowered to the ribs, it falls across the front of the body and comes to rest on the canvas in front of the chest or over the hip [choice]. Neither arm reaches out to break the fall [choice]. He is "Down" from the first touch of anything but his feet, about 150.5–150.8 s ([rules study](rules.md#down-definition); [bout](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#fall)) [inference].
5. **Rest on his right side (by 1.0–2.0 s).** A small settle after the last contact, then the side lie: right shoulder and hip on the canvas, hips and knees flexed, the upper (left) leg resting on or in front of the lower, the head on or near the right arm, the face toward the referee's place at his head ([referee](../../body/brief/cast/referee.md#knockdown)) [choice]. The package's side-lying pose (hip 40°, knee 60°, shoulder 70°, elbow 60°) is a fair resting target (PKG `lieDown.POSES`) [inference].

The whole fall is real speed and uncut ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#fall); [coverage, no slow motion](../cinematography/fight-coverage.md#no-slow-motion)). Its sounds are the kick's contact, the body's thud on the canvas and then breath ([ring sound](../../body/brief/sound/ring-sound.md#palette)).

### Departure from the package {#fall-departure}

The package's `knockdown` folds the legs, sits the seat onto the floor and rolls the body back to lie supine, its back falling toward the blow's direction [observation: PKG `knockdown`]. This fall departs [inference]:

1. It ends on the right side, not supine.
2. It goes down sideways and away from a blow to the side of the head, with the head and trunk turned first, rather than sitting and rolling back from a blow ahead.
3. It never passes through the supine posture, which `lieDown`'s route to a side lie does (PKG `lieDown.ROUTES`).

The timing of the buckle (seat or hip on the canvas at about 0.45 s) is kept from the package [choice]. The code builds the side fall and keeps the downed state for the rules ([what the vocabulary lacks](#vocab-lacks), item 9).

## Failing to rise {#failing-to-rise}

How a conscious downed fighter tries to beat a count and fails, as movement only. The brief requires that Visser is conscious throughout, tries to rise and fails, never reads as limp and still, and is still down at "Eight" and at "Ten" ([bout](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#unable-to-rise)). Nothing here says why he cannot rise ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#no-injury-mechanism)).

### What fight reports show {#rise-reports}

- Men who beat the count rise slowly and barely. Williams, after Feitosa's downward kick behind the ear, "barely was able to bring himself back up to his feet at referee Steve Mazzagatti's count of nine" (SH-USA05, p. 1) [observation]. Goodridge "was barely able to climb back up to his feet" (SH-USA05, p. 1) [observation]. Cabri managed "to regain his feet" before the referee waved it off (GLORY-BEZ) [observation].
- Men who do not rise are counted out while they move. Hari "would not make it up in ten seconds" (BE-HARI, para. beginning "Up against the ropes") [observation]. Mauceri, dropped by a liver kick, "lay face down in agony as referee Dan Miragliotta counted him out" (GLORY-CHIK) [observation]. Translation: a liver kick, a body blow; only the fact of a conscious man counted out on the canvas is taken [inference].
- No retrieved report describes the parts of a failed attempt in order; that would need footage ([scope](#scope)) [observation].

### What floor-rise research shows {#rise-research}

- Healthy younger adults (20–50 years) stood up from lying on the floor in 4.2 s (interquartile 3.8–5.2 s, range 3.1–6.0 s) measured at the lower back, and 4.5 s (4.0–5.4 s, range 3.3–6.4 s) at the sternum, from lying on the back, the front and each side (SCHWICKERT, Table 2) [measurement]. The elevation phases "were mostly located at the end of the motion sequences" (SCHWICKERT, Discussion) [observation].
- A rise that struggles breaks into pieces: "older persons tend to reposition their hands and feet more often than younger subjects during elevation movements, thus splitting up elevation into several components" (SCHWICKERT, Discussion) [observation]. And "the faller might attempt to stand up or at least orient to a sitting position several times without recovering successfully" (SCHWICKERT, Discussion) [observation].
- Translation: voluntary rises by uninjured adults, and a remark about real falls. They give the duration a successful rise needs and the shape of a broken one: repeated placements of the hands and feet, with the lift itself last [inference].

### The attempt and how it fails {#rise-phases}

Under the commission's anchors the count runs from "One" at 158 to "Ten" at 167 ([count](../../instructions/commission.md#count)). A successful rise needs 3.1–6.4 s ([research](#rise-research)), so a man who would be up by "Ten" must start lifting by about 161–163 s [inference]. Visser's attempt is therefore timed to start in the count and to give way before 165, "Eight" [choice]. The signs, each a movement the representation can show ([work](../../body/brief/world/work.md#faceless-polygon-men)), in a working order the screenplay may cut down [choice]:

1. **Turning toward the referee (from about 152–158 s).** His head lifts a little off the right arm and turns toward the referee kneeling at his head; the turn may come again on the first numbers. This reads as attention ([work](../../body/brief/world/work.md#faceless-polygon-men)) [choice].
2. **A glove pressing the canvas (from about 158–161 s).** The free left glove comes down flat on the canvas in front of his chest and presses; the right elbow, under him, tries to prop the shoulder up; the shoulder lifts a few centimetres and comes back [choice]. Heard as the slide of a glove on the canvas ([ring sound](../../body/brief/sound/ring-sound.md#palette)).
3. **A knee drawn up that does not bear weight (about 160–163 s).** The upper, left knee draws toward his chest and the foot or knee finds the canvas, but the hips do not rise over it [choice].
4. **An attempt to get to hands and knees that gives way (about 161–165 s).** He rolls a little toward his front, pushing with both gloves; the hips begin to lift toward the hands-and-knees posture; the arms or the legs fold and he settles back onto his right side, short of the posture [choice]. This is the vocabulary's crawl getting down onto hands and knees, interrupted part-way and reversed ([vocabulary](#vocab-downed)) [inference]. It ends on his right side, because the brief has him on his right side from the result to the end ([Visser](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#state)).
5. **Down at "Eight" and to "Ten" (165–167 s).** He is back on his side, moving a glove or his head, breathing loudly, and does not attempt again in the last two numbers [choice].

**Stillness and limpness.** He is never limp and never still for long: some part moves at least every 2–3 s from the end of the fall until 180 s, a head turn, a glove's slide or a breath that lifts the shoulders [choice]. A man who reads as motionless invites the referee's early stop for the doctor, which the commission's full count excludes ([rules study](rules.md#anchors), tension 2).

**The interruption, against the vocabulary.** Crawl's first phase takes 1.5 s from lying to hands and knees and may follow a settled side lie, but nothing may interrupt a crawl, and kneel goes down from standing ([vocabulary](#vocab-downed)) [observation: PKG]. So the interrupted attempt, rising toward hands and knees and folding back to the side, is composed by the code; the screenplay names the signs above [inference].

### After "Stay down." {#rise-after}

From the referee's "Stay down." after the knockout signal at 167.6 s, Visser stops trying to rise. He stays on his right side; his head turns toward the men kneeling at him; one glove moves on the canvas; his breath stays ragged and loud. He does not try again and does not go limp ([Visser](../../body/brief/cast/visser.md#state)). The referee's hand rests on his shoulder or upper arm and does not turn him; the doctor kneels at his head ([referee, care](../../body/brief/cast/referee.md#care); [referee study, care](../officiating/referee.md#care)). The working rule for his movement after 168 s is smaller and slower than in the count: a head turn or a glove's movement every 2–4 s, and the breath throughout [choice].

## The winner's conduct after a knockout {#celebration}

What a championship kickboxer does in the ring after the knockout, kept to steps on his own feet ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#ending)).

### What winners do {#celebration-display}

- Winners at the 2004 Olympic and Paralympic judo tournaments, photographed by an official photographer repeatedly for about 15 s during and after each match, showed more of each of these in response to winning than to losing: "head-tilt back", "smile", "arms out from the body", "arms raised", "hands in fists", "chest expanded" and "torso pushed out" (TRACY, Results, para. beginning "Among sighted athletes"; Methods) [observation]. The display was the recognised pride expression with one exception, "the absence of hands on hips" (TRACY, Discussion) [observation]. Translation: judo is a combat sport whose winners, like a kickboxer, have just beaten one opponent in front of a crowd; the display is reported as innate, held across 36 nations and by congenitally blind athletes, so it transfers to the kickboxing ring [inference]. A polygon man has no smile; the head tilt, raised arms, fists and expanded chest are what the representation shows as excitement ([work](../../body/brief/world/work.md#faceless-polygon-men)) [inference].
- A GLORY winner can climb the corner: Beztati "ran to mount the corner ring ropes and stood there, arms folded, surveying the scene of his victory" (GLORY-BEZ, para. beginning "But referee Nicholls") [observation]. The rules study reads GLORY's ban on approaching or inciting the crowd as keeping the celebration inside the ring and off the ropes ([technique additions](rules.md#technique-additions)), and the brief follows it ([bout, result](../../body/brief/world/bout.md#result)). The film's winner therefore does not do what Beztati did [inference].

### Benlloch's celebration {#celebration-benlloch}

From 168.1 s, released from the neutral corner by the knockout signal ([rules study](rules.md#anchors)) [commission]:

- **Raised gloves.** Both gloves raised overhead, outside the shoulders, fists closed, the head tilted back and the chest out, held 1–3 s at a time [choice, from TRACY's display and PKG `selfAction` victory].
- **Steps.** Quick steps on his own planted feet, each landing and bearing him: 2–3 steps a second in short runs of three to eight, at the fast walk or the fighting step (PKG `walk` fast pace, 1.71 m/s at a 0.93 m hip; PKG `step` 0.35 s a step) [choice]. Between runs he stands and raises his gloves again, since the vocabulary raises the arms standing ([vocabulary](#vocab-benlloch-after)).
- **Turning to his corner.** His head and trunk turn toward his own white corner, where his team rises with raised arms at their seats ([ringside](../../body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#corners-after)); the white corner lies on the edge of the far half, so the turn keeps him in it ([ring](../../body/brief/sets/ring.md#corners)) [inference].
- **Not on the ropes.** He does not climb the ropes or turn to the crowd (rules study, as above).
- **Not over the fallen man.** He stays in the far half and comes no nearer than a stride short of the referee kneeling at Visser's head, and does not stand over Visser; the referee's raised palm keeps him there ([ring](../../body/brief/sets/ring.md#corners); [referee, care](../../body/brief/cast/referee.md#care)). His joy is for himself and his corner, not against the fallen man ([Benlloch](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#challenger); [styles, respect](styles.md#respect)).
- **Breath.** Loud, excited breath through the celebration, and no word ([Benlloch](../../body/brief/cast/benlloch.md#voice)).

No retrieved source describes a GLORY winner's conduct toward the fallen man after a knockout in more detail; that would need footage ([scope](#scope)) [observation].
