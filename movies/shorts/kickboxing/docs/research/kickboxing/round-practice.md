## Rules and procedure {#rules-and-procedure}

Direct rule reading, R1: §5.07 permits a clinch for one immediate legal knee followed by release; it prohibits elbows, throws, sweeps and attacking a downed opponent. Curved leg kicks are used here. §5.05.A gives Fight, Break and Stop; after Break, both step back and resume when the referee withdraws. §5.05.B.2 and appendix 3.D place the opponent in the farthest neutral corner, facing centre, before counting. The count reaches eight for assessment and ten for a fighter who remains unable to resume; §5.05.B.5 gives knockout and an ending bell. The full rulebook was checked rather than importing the installed package's earlier study.

The screenplay aims at the lateral area below and just behind the left ear lobe, never the rear skull, with the target readable in profile. Neither this study nor the screenplay supplies an injury mechanism.

Authored procedure: the referee calls the knockdown, moves the standing man away, watches the fallen man while travelling and begins the count only after neutral-corner compliance. The visible preparation fills the interval before 157. He assesses the man on the floor at Eight without suspending the audible pulse. The winner does not leave until the result gives permission. After signalling, the referee kneels and steadies a shoulder as the fallen man tries to move; he does not lift the man to his feet. The crowd remains a wordless seated presence, and corner teams do not enter. These fictional performances realise the commission; they are not medical advice or a documentary account of a real event.

The screenplay describes the actual targets, connecting steps and outcome of each use rather than relying on technique names alone.

The default orthodox base is left foot forward with hands at the head. Punches transfer weight from the supporting foot through the turn, then return the striking glove; a defender's roll bends the knees under a hook and rises into the next position rather than folding the head down towards a knee. A checked kick meets the raised shin, after which the defender replants before the counter that needs that foot's support. These are applications of the installed laws. A body kick at a low forearm still has a visible collision and cost; the screenplay never makes a blocked kick pass through an arm.

This is a qualitative fictional outcome, not a claim that any contact necessarily causes it. The installed generic supine knockdown cannot supply the commissioned ending unchanged. The authored right-side sequence instead shows Marek's rightward recovery step fail to bear him, knees fold, right hip drop, outer right thigh and hip meet the canvas, then right ribs and shoulder settle. His head follows the torso down; his right arm stays bent in front rather than becoming a locked supporting post. The legs finish folded with the left resting ahead of the right. No body part passes through the floor, no sideways flight replaces the collapse, and no instantaneous lying pose conceals it.

Through the count, breath and small attempts to bring the upper left glove beneath the chest supply change without an invented recovery. Owen's victory steps each finish with a planted foot carrying his body; the raised gloves, exhausted breath and the downed opponent share the closing frame.
