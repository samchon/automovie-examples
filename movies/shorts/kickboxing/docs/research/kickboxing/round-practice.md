# The official and the clock

## Rules and procedure {#rules-and-procedure}

Under §5.04.A a single match is scheduled for three rounds of three minutes. GLORY can approve five. Under §5.04.C a title match is scheduled for five three-minute rounds, with three possible at GLORY's discretion in a tournament. Here the knockout ends the bout in its opening round. The film's 180 seconds include the count and the time we spend with the men after the result. [GLORY, §5.04.A/C](https://glory.pinkyellow.network/assets/rules/glory-rules-2026-v2.pdf#page=13)

GLORY's rulebook makes the referee visible in a break. On his word the athletes move back a step. He goes between them. His withdrawal lets the contest continue. The detailed foul provision allows a single immediate knee and requires immediate release. [GLORY, §§5.05.A and 5.07](https://glory.pinkyellow.network/assets/rules/glory-rules-2026-v2.pdf)

The screenplay determines where Daniel intervenes and what he can see when the athletes separate. [Break](../../body/screenplay/kickboxing/010-the-round.md#p03-b02) · [An inch to spare](../../body/screenplay/kickboxing/010-the-round.md#p06-b03)

On a down the referee calls it, gestures and sends the other athlete to the furthest neutral corner. The standing athlete faces centre there. The seconds remain seated during the bout and may not approach their athlete during the count. The referee judges the athlete at eight. A count that continues without interruption through ten gives a knockout. The timekeeper sounds the end of the match. These are observations from the published text. [GLORY, §§5.03.B, 5.05.B and appendix 3.D](https://glory.pinkyellow.network/assets/rules/glory-rules-2026-v2.pdf)
