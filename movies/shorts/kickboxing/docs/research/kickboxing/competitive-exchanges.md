# Pace and attention at ringside

## Pace {#pace}

Calabrese and colleagues analysed video of 17 men's professional GLORY bouts from 2019 in the 85 kg category. The authors distinguished high activity, low activity and pauses. I read the abstract. Its results cover bouts of different lengths and give no timetable for this pair of 80 kg men. [Calabrese et al., Methods and Results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39191384/)

The earlier edition used the distinction between activity and pauses to ask what a spectator might notice when the fighters stopped travelling. Its timing and ringside responses were dramatic choices. The current screenplay gives each pause its duration, sound and consequence. ## Camera and allegiance {#camera}

Maryse Alberti describes planning the *Creed* single take with the fight coordinator and camera operator. She recalls the difference between a boxer alone in the ring and the same man with his trainer in the corner. Where the camera stood had to serve both the contact and the emotion. This is her account in print. [Alberti, interview with Michelle McCue](https://www.wearemoviegeeks.com/2015/12/cinematographer-maryse-alberti-talks-creed-and-women-in-film/)

A still published by GLORY brings one fighter's face into the space between two people bending in from either side. A shoulder fills much of the left foreground. White rope runs behind him. Where the other two people lean tells the eye where to go. I used the direction of those bodies. The timing and sound of the moment are outside the still. [GLORY 19 event photograph](https://glory.pinkyellow.network/assets/news/rules-and-regulations-the-glory-19-main-event-stoppage/cover_image.jpg)

Notes for the earlier edition brought Alberti's account of the boxer and trainer together with the still's use of bodily inclination. They explored whether the direction of one person's attention would read across a full-body view. I did not view film footage or hear event audio for either study. The current screenplay sets the sightlines, gestures and sound of the two encounters.
