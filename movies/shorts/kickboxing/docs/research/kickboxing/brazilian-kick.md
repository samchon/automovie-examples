# docs/research/kickboxing/brazilian-kick.md

This study gives the law of the Brazilian kick, the technique the commission rules for the finish: at 150.000 A's right Brazilian kick, starting as the right body kick B has learned to expect, brings the instep and lower shin down over B's lowered left arm onto the side of the head and neck below and just behind B's left ear lobe, and B falls on his right side. The installed motion vocabulary has the round kick with the shin, the push kick and a redirect of one round kick into another before its chamber key (`packages/motions/src/striking/kick.ts`), but no kick whose path turns over and comes down onto the target; that is what this study supplies. How B's habit of dropping the arm forms is in [exchanges.md](exchanges.md#tell). The retained originals and their hashes are listed in the [research index](../index.md).

Each observation carries one of four marks: **reported** (given by the cited source: a measurement, a rule, a coach's instruction or a reporter's account of a real bout), **observed** (a direct observation by this study), **inferred** (reasoned by this study, with the reasoning stated) or **chosen** (the production's own choice). No footage was acquired, so nothing here is observed.

## What the technique is {#identity}

- The Brazilian kick is "also known as the question-mark kick" (KWU Senshi, Feitosa profile). Reported. It can use "the exact same chamber" as the low kick and the teep, with the knee moving in a different direction (Muay Thai Guy, "Question mark kick is the answer", article body). Reported.
- From the knee lift the kick is redirected with a hip movement; the hips turn over and the kick "goes up and over your opponent's guard looking just like a question mark symbol" (Shutts, LowKickMMA, back-leg version). Reported.
- A kick that "starts like a regular low kick" is redirected "halfway into the motion" (Evolve MMA, "How to throw a question mark kick in Muay Thai", article body); the same article's back-leg variation starts it "like you're going to the torso" before redirecting the leg. Reported.
- Glaube Feitosa, a Kyokushin karate master and K-1 fighter, is presented by his own organization as the "Master of the Brazilian kick" (KWU Senshi, leader profile). Reported. He told Sherdog he used the kick in Kyokushin before K-1 and has practised it for many years; the interviewer describes how he "snap[s] [his] shin downward" when he attacks (Sherdog interview, 2005-05-03). Reported.
- No measured biomechanical study of the Brazilian kick itself was located. Unavailable. Its law below is built from the closest measured analogue and from coaching texts, with each departure stated.

## The closest measured analogue {#analogue}

Gavagan and Sayers 2017 (PLOS ONE, CC BY 4.0) measured the round kick of eight highly ranked Muay Thai practitioners (and eight karate and eight taekwondo black belts) with 3D motion capture at 500 Hz against a pad set at the height of each kicker's mastoid process, the bony point just behind and below the ear lobe; the contact area was "the dorsal surface of the foot to the mid-anterior surface of the shin" (Methods, p. 3). Reported. That is the same height and the same contact surfaces as the commission's finish, so it is the analogue. Inferred.

- Translations and limits. The kicks were thrown at a fixed pad by fighters free of any threat, and the authors say this probably lengthened the recoil compared with competition, where the leg must return promptly (Discussion, p. 10). Reported. Muay Thai kickers "angle the shin down during impact" (p. 10), initiated their kicks closer to the target (p. 11) and moved the centre of mass toward the target, with a large vertical shift (pp. 11-12). Reported. Another study the authors cite found the pelvis tilting through about 60° and rotating through about 130° in taekwondo round kicks (Kim et al., reported on p. 12). Reported. The disagreement between the abstract's m/s and the table's m for the vertical centre-of-mass row is the source's own; this study uses neither as a displacement target. Inferred.
- The analogue departs from the Brazilian kick in its path: the round kick sweeps across to the target, while the Brazilian kick lifts the knee above the target and turns over so the shin descends onto it. The phase structure, the contact height and the contact surfaces are borrowed; the turn-over and the descent come from the coaching texts below. Inferred.

## The law {#law}

### The kicker's body frame

All poses are given for A, orthodox (left foot forward, right foot back), throwing the right (rear) leg. Axes are fixed to A's body at launch: forward points from A's support (left) foot toward B along the line between the men; left and right are A's own; up is vertical. Chosen.

### The support foot's pivot and heel turn

- Pivot on the ball of the support foot so the hips can turn over, rather than on the heel (Scher, Black Belt, round kick). Reported. For the rear-leg question mark kick, turn the front foot to the side as the knee comes up high, to open the hips (Shutts, LowKickMMA; Evolve rear-leg version). Reported.
- The pivot's goal, the back of the heel pointing toward the target, is reported for the round kick (Scher, Black Belt). Reported. No source measures the pivot angle of the Brazilian kick; the production turns the support foot at least that far, because the hips must roll further over than in the round kick. Inferred from the hip turn-over.

### The hip roll over

- The support-foot pivot "should cause the hips to turn over so the buttock of the kicking leg points toward the ceiling" (Scher, Black Belt). Reported. "To get the redirection of the kick right, you really have to turn your hips over" (Shutts, LowKickMMA). Reported.
- The round kick's pelvis rotates forward through 92 ± 23° (Muay Thai) to about 130° (taekwondo, Kim et al.) (Gavagan and Sayers 2017, Table 2, p. 12). Reported. The Brazilian kick's roll is at or beyond the upper end. Inferred; unmeasured.

### The chamber shared with the body kick or the front kick

- Same chamber as the low kick and the teep (Muay Thai Guy). Reported. "Start like you're going to the torso, then redirect your leg" (Evolve, back-leg variation). Reported. A front kick setup works because the knee lift that starts the front kick also starts this kick (Evolve, rear-leg version, front kick as setup; Muay Thai Guy, teep as "the best setup"). Reported.
- In this bout the shared chamber is the right body kick's, by the commission's ruling. The vocabulary's round `kick` can already be redirected into another round kick before its chamber key (`kick.ts`, cancel table); the Brazilian kick is that kind of continuation but with a turned-over, descending end the vocabulary does not have. Inferred.

### The downward path over the guard

- The kick goes "up and over" the guard (Shutts). Reported. In Las Vegas in 2005 Feitosa "brought his leg down in a sort of slingshot fashion" and the kick "landed swiftly behind Williams' right ear" (Sloan, Sherdog, 2005-05-01). Reported. Feitosa's interviewer describes the shin snapping downward (Sherdog interview, 2005-05-03). Reported.
- Path in A's frame: the knee rises forward and up toward B's left ribs (P2), then higher and across to above B's left shoulder (P3); the lower leg then extends and the instep and lower shin travel down and slightly forward-and-across onto the side of the head and neck (P4). The descent passes above B's left arm, which is down at his ribs, so the arm is not on the path. Inferred from the reported path and the commission's target.

### Contact surfaces

- The commission rules the instep and lower shin. The measured analogue's contact area was the dorsal foot to the mid-anterior shin (Gavagan and Sayers 2017, p. 3). Reported.
- Coaching sources disagree: Evolve's head variations land "the ball of your foot" on the chin, and Shutts says the correct way is the ball of the foot and warns that the top of the foot risks injury to the kicker (Evolve; Shutts). Reported. Feitosa's shin snap and the slingshot descent behind the ear fit the commission's surfaces. Inferred. The commission's ruling stands; the dispute is recorded.

### Reach, support and weight transfer

- Support: all weight on the support leg from toe-off to replant, on the ball of the foot, knee slightly bent (Scher; de Jong, GLORY "The Art of Low Kicks", for the standing leg). Reported.
- Weight transfer: the centre of mass moves toward the target and upward during the kick, and the kick's momentum is transferred through that movement (Gavagan and Sayers 2017, pp. 11-12). Reported. The trunk leans away from B as the knee rises above B's head height, which keeps the combined centre of mass over the support foot. Inferred.
- Reach: the turn-over adds height, not range: pelvis rotation increases hip range and kicking range (Gavagan and Sayers 2017, p. 2, reporting earlier taekwondo work), and the contact lands from the same distance as the body kick it imitates. Inferred. The vocabulary resolves the contact on the lower shin for the 180 cm bodies (`kick.ts`, contact band); the production uses that band. Chosen.

### How the body travels from the distance where it starts

- From body-kick range, where A's right body kicks have been landing on B's ribs: no step; the support foot pivots in place and the body rises and moves toward B through the kick. Inferred, because the kick must look like the body kick to the last moment.
- From farther out: Muay Thai kickers start kicks closer to the target than other styles (Gavagan and Sayers 2017, p. 11). Reported. The vocabulary allows a step-up of the support foot by up to half a stance when the target is beyond the band (`kick.ts`). The production starts the finish from body-kick range so no step-up shows. Chosen.

## Why it beats a lowered guard {#why-it-works}

- A high kick to the head is stopped by the arm on the kicked side braced at the head (Skarbowsky via Dynamic Striking). Reported. When that arm has dropped to the ribs to meet the expected body kick, the head on that side has no cover. Inferred.
- Once the opponent starts blocking the body kicks, "bring your knee up and pause for a second" before the kick goes high (Evolve, body-kick variation). Reported. The body-kick start triggers the learned drop, and the turn-over sends the shin over the arm that has just gone down. Inferred.
- The trap keeps the start the opponent has learned and changes the end, as Superbon did against Petrosyan (exchanges study, [tell](exchanges.md#tell)). Reported there.

## Legal status under GLORY {#legality}

- Kicks are legitimate when they strike "with the foot or lower leg to a legal target", the list naming high kicks and ax kicks among others, executed with full power from the correct distance and body position (GLORY Rules 2026, section 5.06 A and A.2, p. 13). Reported.
- Prohibited: "All techniques targeting the back of the head or back or the torso" (section 5.07 A.2, p. 16). Reported. The neck is not named among the prohibited targets of section 5.07 A (pp. 16-17). Reported, as an absence.
- The commission's clarification of 2026-11-13 places the contact on the side of the head and neck below and just behind the ear lobe, from a kick aimed at the side of the head, not the back of the head. A kick with the instep and lower shin to that point is therefore a legal high kick under GLORY. Inferred from the rule text and the ruling.

## The struck fighter's visible reaction and fall {#fall}

Only visible behaviour is recorded; no injury mechanism is stated, by the commission's ruling.

- Reported reactions to finishing kicks and knees at K-1 level: sent to the canvas by a kick behind the ear and up only at nine (Williams); "crumbling into the ropes", barely able to climb back to his feet (Goodridge) (Sloan, Sherdog). After Schilt's knee Feitosa "could not even raise his hands to break his fall into the ropes"; Musashi "went down and did not get back up" (Nowe, Sherdog, 2005-11-19). Reported.
- A referee reads whether the hands are up, whether the head snaps back and whether the legs are wobbly or sturdy; a fighter who goes down hard with eyes closed may have the count stopped (ABC referee manual, p. 7; boxing, the same visible signals translated to kickboxing). Reported. Under GLORY a man down is counted from the moment the opponent is in the farthest neutral corner, at least to eight, and a count to ten ends the match by knockout (section 5.05 B.2-B.5, p. 12). Reported.
- B's fall, by phase. The kick comes from B's left and descends, so it moves B's head and neck toward his right and down, and the fall goes the same way, onto his right side as the commission rules. Inferred.
