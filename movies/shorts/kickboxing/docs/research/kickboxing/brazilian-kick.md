# The Brazilian kick

The Brazilian kick, also called the question-mark kick, is a round kick that leaves like a low or body kick and turns over in mid-flight to come down onto the head over the guard. The installed `kick` action throws the round kick and can redirect a body kick into a high kick before its chamber, but it has no turnover and no descending delivery, so this study records the technique the finish needs. Each observation carries its kind as the [index](../index.md#observation-kinds) defines it; source ids are in the [index](../index.md#sources).

## Sources and their limits {#sources}

- [reported] Coaching descriptions: `EVOLVE-QMK`, `LOWKICK-QMK-2020`, `LOWKICK-QMK-2011` (archived copy) and `DS-KICKS`, with the general round-kick pivot from `BBM-ROUND-KICK` and `BB-ROBSON` (archived copy).
- [reported] One fight report describes it landing in K-1: Glaube Feitosa's kick at the K-1 USA Grand Prix of 30 April 2005 (`SHERDOG-FEITOSA-2005`); Feitosa is promoted as "Master of the Brazilian kick" (`SENSHI-FEITOSA`) and says he threw it in Kyokushin before K-1 (`SHERDOG-FEITOSA-INTERVIEW`).

## What it exploits {#exploit}

- [reported] "Those things sting, so it doesn't take much before your opponent starts dropping their hands to protect their body"; "once your opponent starts blocking your body kicks, bring your knee up and pause for a second" (`EVOLVE-QMK`).
- [reported] "They'll start dropping their hands to block body kicks", and the kick "goes up and over your opponent's guard looking just like a question mark symbol" (`LOWKICK-QMK-2020`).
- [reported] It works "against an opponent who … pays close attention to your hip movement", and "when an opponent is … tired or hurt and is dropping his … hands in the later rounds" (`LOWKICK-QMK-2011`).

Law [inference]: the kick needs an opponent who has learned to answer the body kick by lowering his arm over his ribs. He reads the rising knee as a body kick and lowers the arm, which opens the side of his head above it.

## Law {#law}

The phases, in the kicker's own frame, for an orthodox kicker throwing with his right (rear) leg at an orthodox opponent:

1. Chamber [reported]: the kick leaves as a body kick. "The technique starts like a regular low kick; then, you surprise your opponent by suddenly aiming higher halfway into the motion" (`EVOLVE-QMK`); "the athlete will shoot their leg in for a mid section kick" (`DS-KICKS`). The right knee rises folded across the body while the left foot pivots on its ball, the heel lifting: "the supporting foot pivots outwards by about 180 degrees" by the time the leg is chambered (`BB-ROBSON`); pivoting on the heel instead "results in the body moving away from the target" (`BBM-ROUND-KICK`).
2. The sell [reported]: "take a slight pause and leave your knee up for half a second to sell the fake" (`LOWKICK-QMK-2020`); "the pause is a crucial part of the technique since you want your opponent to prepare to defend against a kick to the body" (`EVOLVE-QMK`). The values are coaching words, not measurements.
3. Turnover [reported]: the support foot keeps turning "with the goal of getting the back of the heel pointing toward the target", and the pivot makes "the hips … turn over so the buttock of the kicking leg points toward the ceiling" (`BBM-ROUND-KICK`); the kicker "will pivot on their back leg, rolling their hips over to the side, and lifting their leg above their opponents guard" (`DS-KICKS`); "you really have to turn your hips over" (`LOWKICK-QMK-2020`). The knee climbs from the body line to above the receiver's lowered arm.
4. Delivery [reported]: "from here they will bring their shin across the face of their opponent on a downward angle" (`DS-KICKS`); the kicker snaps "your lower leg back, pointing your heel towards your opponent" (`LOWKICK-QMK-2011`); Feitosa "brought his leg down in a sort of slingshot fashion" and it "landed swiftly behind Williams' right ear" (`SHERDOG-FEITOSA-2005`). The sources disagree on the striking surface: the shin (`DS-KICKS`) or the ball of the foot (`EVOLVE-QMK`).
5. Recoil and replant [inference]: the lower leg retracts along its path and the foot comes back to the canvas behind the kicker, restoring the stance, as every round kick does in the installed `kick` action.

Production values [choice, within the sources]:

- The striking surface is the instep and lower shin together, the commission's ruling, which the shin-delivery coaching supports; the target is the side of the neck behind and below the receiver's ear, where Feitosa's kick landed.
- The kicker throws it from the distance of his body kick, so the receiver sees the same entry, the same knee and the same rhythm as the body kicks before it; the pause at the chamber is short enough that it reads as the body kick's own preparation.
- The kicker's guard holds through the kick.
- The timing of chamber, delivery and recoil is the round kick's in the installed `kick` action; only the pause and the turnover are added.

## The earlier production's reach measurement {#earlier-measurement}

- [reported] The earlier kickboxing production measured the finish on the original Human body and found that the instep reached the side of the neck behind the ear, but that with the pelvis and support foot held in the round kick's orientation the hip ran into its flexion, adduction and rotation limits together, so the foot could not rise over the head or come down onto it (`LEGACY-BRAZILIAN-MEASUREMENT`, "Result" and "Conclusion"). It concluded that the turnover must move the pelvis and the support pivot with the leg, as the coaching above describes.

This is a measurement of an earlier rig at an earlier revision, recorded so that motion work starts from it; it is not evidence about the current body.
