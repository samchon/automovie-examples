# Contest rules

This study records the rules that bound what the fighters and the referee may do in this fictional bout, the knockdown procedure that ends it, and what the rules leave to the production. The contest runs under the GLORY Official Rules, effective 16 March 2026 and footed "V2.2" (source `GLORY` in the [index](../index.md)), the version the official rules page still links on 2026-11-11 (`GLORY-PAGE`). The film claims no accredited GLORY event. Each observation carries its kind as the [index](../index.md#observation-kinds) defines it.

## Contest format {#format}

- [reported] An 80 kg athlete fights at Middleweight, "over 77 kg up to 85 kg" (`GLORY` §2.01.D, p. 2, PDF p. 4). Gloves are 10 ounces for every class from 65 kg up (§4.03.B, p. 6, PDF p. 8).
- [reported] "The color of the gloves should be the same as the color of the Athlete's corner" (§4.03.A). The ring's opposite corners carry "a white and black post for the Athletes with two neutral corners in between" (§4.06.D, p. 7, PDF p. 9).
- [reported] The ring floor is "6.40 x 6.40" or "7.00 x 7.00" metres, raised 90 to 122 cm above the ground, with four ropes "positioned 40, 80, 120 and 150 centimeters, respectively, above the ring floor", wrapped in soft material (§4.06.A–C). The rules state no apron width.
- [reported] A match is "three Rounds of three minutes each" with one-minute intervals; a title match is five (§5.04.A, §5.04.C, p. 11, PDF p. 13).
- [choice] The bout is a three-round non-title match in the large 7.00 m ring, the larger floor giving the fighters room to circle, cut off and use the ropes. The film shows its first round, which the knockout ends.

## Legal and illegal actions {#legal-actions}

Rule text from `GLORY` §5.07, pp. 16–17 (PDF pp. 18–19), all [reported]:

- A.4 bans "linear or thrusting kicks directed at a leg or legs (i.e. front kick, side kick, back kick)"; "an Athlete may only attack the legs with kicks that arc, as in a roundhouse or hooking motion".
- A.7 bans elbow strikes and "striking with any part of the arm or wrist above the padded portion of the glove".
- A.10 bans "throws, leg sweeps, foot sweeps or pushing of any kind as an effort to off-balance or down a opponent".
- A.11 bans clinching or holding "for any reason ... other than to immediately attack with a single legal knee strike; after the legal knee strike the Athlete must disengage the clinch immediately".
- A.12 allows grabbing a leg only "to immediately execute a single legal strike that may be accompanied by a single step in any direction".
- A.2 bans "All techniques targeting the back of the head or back or the torso"; A.3 bans "Techniques targeting the groin". The rules do not name the throat.
- B.1 bans "continuing to fight if someone has any other part of his body other than his feet on the ground"; B.2 bans techniques after "Break" or "Stop".
- C.1 counts as stalling "continuously pulling up the front leg or stretching out the front arm without carrying out a particular technique".

Reading of A.2 [inference]: read literally, "or the torso" would ban every strike to the body, the target of the clinch's "single legal knee strike" (A.11) in practice and of every body punch and body kick in kickboxing; the study reads the clause as "the back of the head or back of the torso", the rear of the body, and records that this is its reading of the text, not the text.

Law, derived from the rule text [inference]: the legal weapons are punches to the head and body; round kicks to the legs, body and head; straight kicks such as the teep to the body or head, never the legs; and one knee from a clinch, after which the clinch opens. A leg may be caught only for one immediate strike; this film blocks and checks kicks instead. Holding the front leg up or the front arm out without carrying out a technique is stalling, so a lead leg rises to kick, knee or check and comes down, and a lead arm reaches out to strike, defend, tie up for the knee or measure and comes back; neither is held. Once any part of a fighter other than his feet touches the canvas, nobody strikes him.

The Brazilian kick is an arcing kick to the head, which A.4 does not touch. Its target in this film, the side of the neck below and behind the ear, is not "the back of the head" of A.2 [inference]: the struck surface faces sideways, toward the kicker.

## Commands and the clinch {#commands}

- [reported] "Fight": the referee "begins or resumes the Match with the command 'fight' only once the Athletes are separated an equal distance from the Referee" (`GLORY` §5.05.A.1, p. 12, PDF p. 14).
- [reported] "Break" separates the athletes, "after which they must take a step backwards. The Referee steps in between both Athletes. As soon as he steps back the Athletes resume the fight without the instruction 'fight'" (§5.05.A.2).
- [reported] "Stop" stops the fight "at the end of a Round or prior to a caution or public warning" (§5.05.A.3).
- [reported] The timekeeper's gong signals the beginning and end of rounds (§5.01.F, p. 9, PDF p. 11).

Law [inference]: in a clinch the fighter who holds throws his one knee and lets go at once. If the pair stays tied after it, the referee calls "Break", both step back, he steps between them and steps out, and the fight resumes without a word.

## Knockdown and count {#knockdown}

Rule text from `GLORY` §5.05.B, pp. 12–13 (PDF pp. 14–15) and Appendix 3.D (PDF p. 27), all [reported]:

- B.2: the referee signals a knockdown "by the Down call and gesture", and "starts the Down count after he has sent the opponent to the farthest removed neutral corner". Appendix 3.D: the referee "will not begin the count until the opponent is in the neutral corner facing the center of the Ring". If the opponent leaves the corner, "the counting stops and only starts again once the opponent has returned".
- B.3: the referee "is obliged to continue counting until the eighth count and then assess whether the Athlete is capable of resuming".
- B.5: "In the case a Referee counts to ten without any interruption (which means he concluded the Athlete is incapable to resume during his count of eight), the Athlete will lose on the grounds of Knockout. In that case, the Timekeeper will sound the gong/bell indicating the end of the Match."
- B.6: if the referee instead checks the athlete at eight and then counts nine and ten, the result is a technical knockout.
- §5.06.E.2 (p. 14, PDF p. 16) records both a ten-count and a knockout declared before ten for medical assistance as a knockout.
- The referee counts; no rule relays a count from the timekeeper's table.

The rules give no cadence for the count. Other codes do:

- [reported] Florida: the referee "shall announce the passing of the seconds, accompanying the count with a downward motion of his arm", with a timekeeper giving "the correct one second interval" (`FAC` 61K1-3.033(2)(a)1.b).
- [reported] World Muaythai Council: "the referee must wait for one (1) second before he begins counting loudly from one to ten with one-second interval", showing "a hand signal for each second" (`WMC` §16.9).

Law [choice, from the codes above]: the count starts when the standing fighter stands in the farthest neutral corner facing the centre; one second later the referee calls "One", and each following number lands one second after the last, so "Ten" lands nine seconds after "One", ten seconds after the count began. A fighter who has not stood up with a ready guard by eight is not checked: the referee counts straight on to ten, which is a knockout, and the timekeeper's bell then ends the match.

## The result {#result}

- [reported] GLORY's rules name no gesture for the end of a knockout and no raising of the winner's hand; the ring announcer announces results (`GLORY` §5.01.H).
- [reported] Boxing codes signal the knockout by waving both arms: "the referee shall wave their arms above their head to signal the bout is over" (`ABC-2025` p. 6); "wave both arms to indicate that the participant has been knocked out" (`FAC` (2)(a)1.c). Washington State lets the referee "raise the hand of the opponent" (`WAC` 36-12-140(4)).
- [choice] At ten the referee waves both arms crossing above his head, the shared boxing signal, then turns to the fallen fighter. The film ends before any announcement, so no hand is raised.

## What the rules leave open {#open}

- [reported] The apron's width (no provision in §4.06).
- [reported] The referee's dress and the knockdown gesture itself ("the Down call and gesture" is not described).

Both are decided by the production in its brief.
