# docs/research/kickboxing/rules.md

The GLORY ruleset as this film follows it: one scheduled three-minute round of a single GLORY Match between two 80 kg men, ended by a knockout after an uninterrupted ten-count ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#ruleset)). The current official GLORY rules document is the study's governing source. Where it is silent, a professional kickboxing regulation of a sanctioning body is cited as a comparison and never overrides GLORY. The contest is fictional and claims no accredited event.

Each observation carries its kind:

- **[rule]**: reported rule text, quoted or closely paraphrased, with its article.
- **[obs]**: a direct observation of a retained original, such as a rendered page.
- **[meas]**: a reported measurement from a study.
- **[inf]**: this study's inference, with the reasoning stated.
- **[choice]**: the production's own choice, made or proposed here.

## The round, rest and bells {#round}

- **[rule]** A single Match is three Rounds of three minutes each, with one-minute Intervals; GLORY may approve five Rounds (G 5.04.A, p. 11). Title Matches are five Rounds (G 5.04.C).
- **[rule]** The Timekeeper "checks the duration of the Rounds and the Intervals by means of clock/stopwatch". "By sounding the start of the Round gong, the Timekeeper signals the beginning and end of the Rounds for the Referee" (G 5.01.F, p. 9). The document letters two officials "E", so the Timekeeper is item F.
- **[rule]** The only ten-second signal GLORY names is in the Interval: "Ten seconds before the end of the interval the Timekeeper signals, and the Referee then issues the command, 'seconds out'", and Cornermen leave the Ring (G 5.01.F, p. 9).
- **[rule]** GLORY names no ten-second warning before the end of a Round. Ontario does: "Ten seconds prior to the end of each round, the timekeeper shall give warning … by sounding the wooden clapper" (O 7.3).
- **[rule]** If the Round's time ends during a count, the Timekeeper waits until the Referee has counted eight and given "Fight", then sounds the gong at once (G 5.05.B.4, p. 12). After a ten-count knockout, "the Timekeeper will sound the gong/bell indicating the end of the Match" (G 5.05.B.5, p. 12).
- **[rule]** The Referee begins or resumes the Match with "fight" (G 5.05.A.1, p. 12).
- **[inf]** In the film the round runs from 0 s. The ten-count knockout is signalled at 167.6 s and the match-ending bell rings at 170.1 s ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#count)). Round time therefore never reaches its last ten seconds before the match is over. A ten-second clapper would sound at 170.0 s under Ontario practice, but GLORY has none, and by then the match has already ended. **The film plays no ten-second warning.**
- **[unavailable]** No source states the delay between the referee's knockout signal and the bell. G 5.05.B.5 says only that the Timekeeper sounds it "in that case".
- **[choice]** The delay is 2.5 s, from 167.6 s to 170.1 s ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#count)).

## Pre-fight instructions and the glove touch {#glove-touch}

- **[rule]** GLORY requires a Rules Meeting before every Event, attended by every Athlete and at least one Cornerman (G 2.04, p. 4). Its rules body contains no in-ring instruction or glove-touch provision; **GLORY is silent on both.**
- **[rule]** Ontario: "The referee shall call contestants together before the bout for final instructions … The contestants, after receiving instructions, may touch gloves and return to their corners" (O 6.2, 6.3). WAKO gives the command "SHAKE HANDS" at the beginning of the fight "to touch the gloves and show respect to each other" (WAKO Referee Rules Art. 3.4.4, p. 8; see the [referee study](../officiating/referee.md#commands)).
- **[inf]** The commission's touch, "they step into an actual glove touch and immediately exchange", comes after the start bell and the referee's "Fight". It is the courtesy touch of the round's opening, not the instruction-time touch, which is off screen before 0 s. No GLORY rule forbids it, and a touch made in the round is live action.
- **[choice]** The referee gives no instruction speech on screen.

## Legal techniques and targets {#legal-targets}

- **[rule]** "In order for a strike to be counted in scoring, the technique must be executed with full power, from the correct distance and body position, and land on legal target area" (G 5.06.A, p. 13). The document lists the following techniques but defines no positive list of legal target areas:
  - **Punches:** with the padded part of the glove: straight punches, hooks, uppercuts, and the spinning back-fist with the padded back of the glove (G 5.06.A.1).
  - **Kicks:** "Striking with the foot or lower leg": front kicks, low kicks inside and outside the leg, middle kicks, high kicks, sidekicks, back kicks, axe kicks, spinning kicks and jumping kicks (G 5.06.A.2).
  - **Knees:** front knee, round-house knee, jumping knee, and knee on the leg inside and outside, "please note the clinch limitations" (G 5.06.A.3).
- **[rule]** The only target-based fouls are:
  - "All techniques targeting the back of the head or back or the torso" (G 5.07.A.2, p. 16);
  - "Techniques targeting the groin" (G 5.07.A.3);
  - linear or thrusting kicks to the legs (G 5.07.A.4).
- **[inf]** A literal reading of 5.07.A.2 ("or the torso") would ban every body strike, which contradicts the middle kicks of 5.06.A.2. This study reads it as "the back of the head, or the back of the torso".
- **[rule]** The ABC defines permissible target areas as "front and sides of the head; front and sides of the torso above the belt; entire leg, except the knee joint by thrusting or linear kicks" (K 12; also O 9.1.1–9.1.3). The ABC's back-of-head zone runs "from the crown of the head … spreading from ear to ear in the back of the head, running down the entire rear portion of the neck … stopping at the top of the trapezius" (K 11.D). The ABC and Ontario also make "attacks to the throat" a foul (K 11.E, O 11). GLORY does not.
- **[rule]** WAKO's amateur K1 rules allow only "Head - front, forehead and side" (W Ch. 10 Art. 3, p. 151). They prohibit attacks to "the throat, kidneys, back, groin, neck, back of the head and top of the shoulders" (W Ch. 10 Art. 5, p. 152).

**The finish's landing point.** The finish lands on the side of the head and neck, below and just behind B's left ear lobe, at the junction of jaw and neck. It comes from a kick aimed at the side of the head ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#finish)).

- **[inf]** It is a legal target under GLORY. GLORY makes only the back of the head a foul (5.07.A.2) and names no neck foul, and the ABC and Ontario treat the sides of the head as permissible.
- **[inf]** Under the ABC's stricter definition, the point lies at the lateral edge of the back-of-head zone, which spreads "from ear to ear in the back" and down the "rear portion of the neck". It stays legal only while the contact sits on the side of the neck and jaw, not on the rear of the neck.
- **[choice]** The kick's contact centre stays on the lateral neck and jaw angle, forward of the rear of the neck. The interface region is `neckLeft` or `jawLeft`, never `nape` or `headBack`. In the referee's side view, the kick reads as an arc into the side of the head.
- **[rule]** The same landing would be a foul under WAKO amateur K1, which bans the neck. That is the amateur ruleset, which the film does not follow.
- **[rule]** The back of the head is a foul under GLORY (5.07.A.2), the ABC (K 11.D) and Ontario (O 11).

**The Brazilian kick.** **[inf]** The right Brazilian kick is a "high kick" with "the foot or lower leg" (G 5.06.A.2). Its instep and lower shin are the legal striking surfaces GLORY names.

## Fouls {#fouls}

- **[rule]** Prohibited striking techniques (G 5.07.A, pp. 16–17):
  - head butts;
  - back of the head or back (A.2);
  - groin (A.3);
  - linear kicks to the legs (A.4);
  - biting;
  - stomping;
  - "Elbow strikes; striking with any part of the arm or wrist above the padded portion of the glove" (A.7);
  - open-glove strikes and thumbing (A.8);
  - "Wrestling or judo techniques, strangulation techniques or submissions" (A.9);
  - "Throws, leg sweeps, foot sweeps or pushing of any kind as an effort to off-balance or down a opponent. Any attempt to off-balance or down an opponent with anything other than a legal strike may be considered a foul" (A.10);
  - clinch holding (A.11, quoted [below](#clinch));
  - "Holding on to an opponent's leg without striking", where a caught leg allows a single strike with a single step and must then be released (A.12);
  - pulling with both arms at the lower back (A.13).
- **[rule]** Prohibited Match conduct (G 5.07.B, p. 17):
  - "Continuing to fight if someone has any other part of his body other than his feet on the ground" (B.1): this is the downed-attack foul;
  - "Techniques used following the command 'Break' or 'Stop' by the Referee" (B.2);
  - attacking an opponent outside the Ring or tied up in the ropes (B.3);
  - pushing over the ropes (B.4);
  - holding the ropes (B.5).
- **[rule]** Stalling includes "Spitting out the mouthguard" (G 5.07.C.7). Unsportsmanlike conduct includes "talking" (G 5.07.D.1).
- **[inf]** The fighters' silence in the film ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#language)) is also the legal conduct.
- **[rule]** Consequences are imposed by the Referee alone (G 5.08.A–B, pp. 17–18):
  - **Caution:** for a minor or accidental foul; it "may occur without stopping the action".
  - **Official Warning:** the Referee stops the action, sends the opponent to the farthest neutral corner, and gestures, for example "right arm up and pointer finger extended".
  - **Penalty:** a point deduction, with a gesture such as "right arm raised and swinging downward in a counterclockwise motion".
  - **Disqualification.**
- **[rule]** The Athlete Overview summarises: "Clinching is not permitted. After three of any Fouls, automatic point deduction" (G Appendix 3.D, p. 2). Appendix 3 calls itself "a summary for informational purposes only".

## The clinch {#clinch}

- **[rule]** GLORY, exactly as written: "Grabbing, clinching or holding for any reason (including holding to rest or grabbing an opponent in order to stop them from striking) other than to immediately attack with a single legal knee strike; after the legal knee strike the Athlete must disengage the clinch immediately." (G 5.07.A.11, p. 17)
- **[rule]** The break: "The command 'break' is used by the Referee to interrupt an action of the Athletes and to separate the Athletes after which they must take a step backwards. The Referee steps in between both Athletes. As soon as he steps back the Athletes resume the fight without the instruction 'fight.'" (G 5.05.A.2, p. 12)
- **[rule]** GLORY gives no seconds for the clinch. The ABC and Ontario allow the referee to let a "consistent and effective" knee exchange run "for up to five seconds at which point he will separate the fighters" (K 11.T; O 10.2). WAKO K1 allows "Clinching (active, for 5 seconds only)" and "Only one knee … if the kickboxer hold opponent's neck or shoulders" (W Ch. 10 Art. 4.1, 4.3, pp. 151–152).
- **[inf]** GLORY allows one knee per clinch where the others allow an exchange of knees for up to 5 s, so a legal GLORY clinch is shorter than 5 s. Its natural length is the grip, one knee, and the release. The [referee study](../officiating/referee.md#targets) gives the target range.
- **[inf]** The clinch duty is the Athlete's ("must disengage"). A clean clinch therefore needs no "Break"; the referee breaks only a clinch that is held.

## Knockdown, the count and the neutral corner {#knockdown}

**Knockdown definition.** **[rule]** An Athlete is "Down" when, "as the result of a damaging attack, any part of an Athlete's body (other than their feet) touch the floor or would have touched the floor if not for the ropes or holding the opponent" (G 5.05.B.1.a, p. 12).

**Standing count.** **[rule]** GLORY keeps a standing count: an Athlete still standing who "appears to be in a weakened state and may be in danger of injury" is Down "(known as 'Standing Eight Count')" (G 5.05.B.1.b). The ABC and Ontario have no standing eight count (K 7; O 17.23). **[choice]** None occurs in the film, because nobody goes down before the finish ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish)).

**The Down call and the corner.** **[rule]** "If the Referee determines that an Athlete is Down, then he will indicate this by the Down call and gesture. The Referee starts the Down count after he has sent the opponent to the farthest removed neutral corner. The opponent waits in his neutral corner until the Referee gives him permission to leave this corner and resume the Match. If the opponent leaves his neutral corner before the Referee gives the sign, the counting stops and only starts again once the opponent has returned to his neutral corner. During the Down count it is not permitted for any of the Cornermen to approach their Athletes in any way." (G 5.05.B.2, p. 12)

**The count's start condition.** **[rule]** "Referee will not begin the count until the opponent is in the neutral corner facing the center of the Ring" (G Appendix 3.D, p. 2).

**The neutral corner.** **[rule]** "The opposite corners of the Ring should be equipped with a white and black post for the Athletes with two neutral corners in between" (G 4.06.D, p. 7). Glove colour matches the Athlete's corner colour (G 4.03.A, p. 6).

**Who counts and how.**

- **[rule]** In GLORY the Referee counts (5.05.B.2–6), and the document gives the Timekeeper no part in the count.
- **[rule]** Ontario gives a knockdown timekeeper a count that starts at once: "When a contestant is down, the knockdown timekeeper shall immediately stand and count one-second intervals in a loud clear voice, indicating the count to the referee with a motion of their arm and upraised fingers" (O 17.2). The referee then calls "down" while "pointing to the ring floor", orders the opponent "to the farthest neutral corner and shall thereafter pick up the count from the knockdown timekeeper" (O 17.6, 17.7), and "shall continue the count of one-second intervals, in a loud clear voice, indicating the count … with a motion of their arm and upraised fingers" (O 17.8).
- **[rule]** WAKO: "the Central referee will count from 1 to 10 with a second interval between each number, and will indicate each second with his fingers" (W Ch. 7 Art. 9, p. 135).

**What happens at eight.** **[rule]** "The Referee is obliged to continue counting until the eighth count and then assess whether the Athlete is capable of resuming the fight in a responsible manner. If that is the case, the Referee gives the command Fight." (G 5.05.B.3)

**What ends the contest at ten.**

- **[rule]** "In the case a Referee counts to ten without any interruption (which means he concluded the Athlete is incapable to resume during his count of eight), the Athlete will lose on the grounds of Knockout. In that case, the Timekeeper will sound the gong/bell indicating the end of the Match." (G 5.05.B.5)
- **[rule]** If he instead counts to eight, has to check the Athlete ("e.g. checking the Athlete's eyes and balance"), and finds him not capable, he "follows up with the nine and ten count, and the Athlete will lose the Match on the grounds of a Technical Knockout" (G 5.05.B.6, p. 13).
- **[rule]** While the Referee counts, Cornermen may not throw in the towel (G 5.05.B.7).

**Count cadence.** **[unavailable]** GLORY does not state the cadence; it says only "count". **[rule]** The comparison regulations state one-second intervals (O 17.2, 17.8; W Ch. 7 Art. 9). **[inf]** The film counts at one number per second.

**What the film must keep.** **[inf]** The production's anchors fit GLORY rather than Ontario: the count starts at 157 s with One at 158 s and Ten at 167 s, while A waits in the neutral corner ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#count)).

- The count starts only after A stands in the corner facing the centre. A therefore reaches the corner and turns by 157 s, 7 s after the landing.
- No timekeeper's count is heard before 157 s. GLORY names none, and an Ontario-style count would start at the knockdown and put "One" near 151 s.
- B must stay down through Ten with no check at eight. A pause at eight to check B's eyes and balance turns the result into a technical knockout (G 5.05.B.6). Only "One" to "Ten" without interruption is the knockout the commission names (G 5.05.B.5; 5.06.E.2).
- A must not leave the corner during the count. If he does, the count stops (G 5.05.B.2), which would break the real-time anchors.

**Knockout.** **[rule]** "If an Athlete is struck in such a way that he or she is incapable of resuming the Match within the Referee's ten-count or if the Referee declares the knockout before the count to ten in order to bring in immediate medical assistance" (G 5.06.E.2, p. 14). The ABC and Ontario likewise let the referee end the count early when the fallen fighter "requires immediate medical attention" (O 17.4, 17.12).

- **[inf]** A full real-time ten-count is coherent only if B does not present as needing immediate medical help. Otherwise a real referee would wave the bout off before ten.
- **[choice]** During the count B reads as conscious but unable to rise. He moves and fails, and never lies still with closed eyes. This shows no injury mechanism ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#no-injury-mechanism)).

**The knockout signal.** **[unavailable]** GLORY does not describe a gesture for the knockout. **[rule]** Ontario: "the referee shall wave both arms above their head to indicate that the contestant has been knocked out and declare their opponent the winner of the bout by knockout" (O 17.12). The [referee study](../officiating/referee.md#knockdown-procedure) gives the signal the film uses.

**Three knockdowns.** **[rule]** In a single Match, a TKO results when an Athlete "has been knocked down three times in the same Round or a total of four times in the same Match" (G 5.06.E.3.c, p. 15). It does not arise in the film, which has one knockdown.

**Both down.** **[rule]** If both Athletes are Down, the Referee counts both (G 5.05.B.8). It does not arise in the film.

## Medical attendance and the result {#medical-and-result}

**The Ring Doctor.**

- **[rule]** "A Ring Doctor must always be present at the Ring during Matches" (G 5.01.E.1, p. 8). The doctor "is authorized to take all measures that are considered medically necessary before, during, and after Matches" (G 5.01.E.2). "Only the Ring Doctor may enter the Ring during an examination" (G 5.01.E.4). To consult the doctor, the Referee "will stop the Match and direct the uninjured Athlete to a neutral corner" (G 5.06.E.3.a.1, p. 14).
- **[rule]** Ontario seats the ringside medical practitioner "in close proximity of the neutral steps", with a paramedic beside him (O 3.1.2, 3.1.3).

**Cornermen.**

- **[rule]** Cornermen remain seated during the Match. Entering the Ring "at any time during the Match (even during a Ring Doctor consultation)" is prohibited (G 5.03.B, p. 10), and so is approaching during the Down count (G 5.05.B.2).
- **[unavailable]** GLORY does not say when Cornermen may enter after a knockout bell.
- **[inf]** The film shows no cornerman inside the ropes before 180 s.

**After a knockout.** **[rule]** "In the event that an Athlete loses a Match by knockout or technical knockout, an Athlete is required to have a clinical assessment performed", and GLORY may require a CT scan (G 2.03.D, pp. 3–4). This happens after the Match and off screen.

**How the result is declared.**

- **[rule]** The Judge Supervisor is "responsible for authenticating the scores and informing the Announcer of the results of the Match" (G 5.01.D, p. 8). The Ring Announcer is "responsible for announcing the Match, Warnings and the scores" (G 5.01.H, p. 9). For a knockout, the Referee's uninterrupted ten-count decides it (G 5.05.B.5). In Ontario the referee's signal itself declares the opponent the winner (O 17.12).
- **[unavailable]** No GLORY text describes raising the winner's hand.
- **[choice]** The result is the referee's ten and his signal at 167.6 s. No announcer is heard before 180 s, because the referee's words are the only speech ([commission](../../instructions/commission.md#language)).

## Ring, equipment and fighters {#ring-and-equipment}

- **[rule]** The Ring is square, with ropes linked by vertical cords. The canvas is not slippery. The floor is raised 90 to 122 cm. "The Ring floor can have one of two sizes: Small size 6.40 x 6.40 square meters or Large size 7.00 x 7.00 square meters, unless otherwise determined by GLORY". There are four ropes, 3 to 5 cm thick, at 40, 80, 120 and 150 cm above the floor (G 4.06.A–C, p. 7).
- **[rule]** Middleweight is over 77 kg up to 85 kg (G 2.01.D, p. 2). Gloves are 10 ounces for every class from 65 kg up, supplied by GLORY and taped (G 4.03.A–B, p. 6).
- **[rule]** Men wear kickboxing trunks that stop above the knee, with the upper body uncovered. Shoes are not permitted. A mouthguard and groin protection are mandatory, ankle socks are optional, and jewellery is not allowed (G 4.01.B, D–F, p. 5).
- **[inf]** Both 80 kg fighters are Middleweights and wear 10 oz gloves: one in white and one in black, matching their corners.

## Professional and amateur rules {#level}

**[rule]** The level shows in the rules themselves:

| Foot sweeps | Foul (5.07.A.10) | Allowed at ankle level (Ch. 10 Art. 4.4) |

**[inf]** A film that showed sweeps or sustained knee exchanges would depart from GLORY. **[rule]** So would a headguard, because "Any other protective gear is prohibited" without GLORY's consent (G 4.01.E, p. 5).

## Disagreements with the installed vocabulary's code {#code-disagreements}

The installed ruleset code is `packages/motions/src/sport/kickboxing/` in the AutoMovie repository. It cites "GLORY Official Rules V2.2", which is the internal label of the document studied here (G, p. 1).

**1. Single clinch knee** (`KickboxingRules.ts`, rule `single-clinch-knee`). The code rejects any knee whose start or end touches another punch, kick or knee of the same fighter.

- **[inf]** It is stricter than G 5.07.A.11, which limits knees only inside a clinch. A knee thrown at range, or a combination that ends in a knee without a grip, is legal (G 5.06.A.3 lists the jumping and round-house knee). So is a strike after the clinch has been released.
- **[inf]** It is also looser than the rule. It never checks that a grip existed, that the knee followed it "immediately", or that the fighter "must disengage the clinch immediately".
- **[choice]** The film's clinch beats are therefore checked against this study, not against the code alone.

**2. Ring size** (`KickboxingRing.ts`). The doc comment says "GLORY rings are 6.1 to 7.3 m inside the ropes". **[rule]** The current document gives two sizes, 6.40 × 6.40 m or 7.00 × 7.00 m, "unless otherwise determined by GLORY" (G 4.06.B). It calls this the "Ring floor" and does not say whether it is measured inside the ropes. **[inf]** The production's ring size is either 6.40 or 7.00 m.

**3. Corner names** (`KickboxingRing.ts`). The code names the athletes' corners `red` and `blue`. **[rule]** GLORY's corners are white and black posts, with matching glove colours (G 4.06.D, 4.03.A). This is naming only; the neutral corners are correctly on the other diagonal.

**4. Foul regions** (`KickboxingRules.ts` `FOUL_REGIONS`).

- **[inf]** These agree with GLORY: `headBack`, `upperBack`, `lowerBack`, the kidneys and `groin`. The code's reading of A.2 as "the back of the head or the back" matches this study's reading.
- **[inf]** Including `nape` goes beyond GLORY's literal words, which do not mention the neck. It matches the ABC's back-of-head zone, which runs down "the entire rear portion of the neck" (K 11.D), so the study keeps it.
- **[inf]** Leaving `throat` legal agrees with GLORY but differs from the ABC and Ontario (K 11.E, O 11). The film never targets the throat.
- **[choice]** The finish is entered as `neckLeft` or `jawLeft`, which the code treats as legal. It must never be entered as `nape`.

**5. Linear kicks to the legs** (`KickboxingRules.ts` `foul`). The code flags only `technique === "push"`. **[rule]** G 5.07.A.4 also bans side kicks and back kicks to the legs. **[inf]** The finding matters only if the vocabulary gains those techniques.

**6. Knockdown count** (`KnockdownCount.ts`). The procedure agrees with G 5.05.B.2–5 and Appendix 3.D: farthest neutral corner, facing the centre, counting to eight, ten ending the bout. **[inf]** Three points differ:

- The code calls one number per second "the research's implementation … not a timing the rulebook quotes". That is true of GLORY, but the one-second interval is rule text in O 17.2 and 17.8 and in W Ch. 7 Art. 9.
- The code has no technical-knockout path for a check at eight (G 5.05.B.6).
- It does not suspend the count when the opponent leaves the corner (G 5.05.B.2).

Neither omission matters to this film, whose count is uninterrupted.

**7. Choosing the corner** (`KnockdownCount.corner`). The code picks the farther neutral corner by distance from the fallen fighter. **[inf]** GLORY says only "farthest removed neutral corner" (5.05.B.2) without naming the reference point, so "from the fallen man" is a reasonable reading, not rule text.

## What the documents do not say {#unavailable}

- **Count cadence:** GLORY does not state it. It is taken from O and W, as above.
- **Knockout gesture:** GLORY does not describe the "Down call and gesture" or a knockout gesture.
- **Ten-second warning:** GLORY has none before the end of a Round.
- **Bell timing:** No source states the delay from the knockout signal to the bell.
- **Pre-fight procedure:** GLORY has no in-ring instructions or glove-touch rule.
- **Clinch time:** GLORY gives no time in seconds.
- **After the bell:** GLORY does not say when Cornermen or medical staff may enter after a knockout bell, beyond the doctor's general authority.
- **The winner:** GLORY does not describe raising the winner's hand.
- **The crowd:** GLORY bars Athletes and Cornermen from approaching or inciting the crowd (5.02.D, 5.03.G) but says nothing about crowd behaviour itself.
- **Other GLORY versions:** The tournament amendment announced for GLORY 107 is Appendix 4 here (effective 1 January 2026). It does not apply to a single Match.
