# docs/research/kickboxing/rules.md: the GLORY ruleset of the round

This study records the ruleset that round one of the fictional title bout follows: its division and format, its start, its equipment, its ring, its legal and illegal actions, its clinch, its knockdown and knockout procedure, its officials and the outcomes the brief uses. It serves the brief's world rules, officials, ring and equipment. Technique laws, exchange outcomes and activity rates belong to the screenplay's later study (see [scope](#scope)).

## How to read this study {#reading}

Each observation carries its kind in brackets:

- **[observation]**: what the original's text or photograph states, read directly in the saved copy.
- **[measurement]**: a value with its unit that the original reports.
- **[inference]**: this study's reasoning from sourced facts. The facts it rests on are cited.
- **[choice]**: a decision this production makes where the sources leave room, offered to the brief.

## The governing ruleset {#ruleset}

GLORY's rules apply to every GLORY match, except where a mandatory regulator's rules supersede them (GLORY 1.02, p. 1) [observation]. The commission's contest is fictional and claims no accredited event. This production therefore follows the GLORY text alone and names no regulator [choice]. The current text, effective 16 March 2026, carries the version mark V2.2 on its contents page (GLORY, contents page) [observation]. This is the same version the package exports cite [observation: PKG `KickboxingRules` documentation].

GLORY is silent on several things the brief needs: pre-fight instructions and the glove touch, the referee's gestures for the knockdown and the knockout, the count's cadence, corner padding, and who may enter the ring after a knockout. For each, this study states the rule of another body (ABC, Ontario, WAKO or BIBA) and its translation into this bout. ABC and BIBA referee and timekeeper texts are written for boxing. Ontario's and ABC-KB's rules are professional kickboxing ("boxing with hands and feet"). WAKO's ring rules are amateur, with head guards and shin guards. Their equipment and target rules are not adopted.

## Division and bout format {#format}

### Division {#division}

GLORY's Middleweight class is "over 77 kg up to 85 kg" (GLORY 2.01.D, p. 2) [measurement]. Two men of 80 kg fight in it [inference]. Weigh-ins take place the day before the event (GLORY 2.02.A, p. 3) [observation]. A title "with a maximum weight can only be won or defended if both Athletes are at or under the weight limit" (GLORY 5.04.C.1, p. 11) [observation]. So both men made 85 kg or less at the weigh-in [inference].

### Title bout format {#title-format}

A title match is "five Rounds of three minutes each" unless it is part of a tournament (GLORY 5.04.C, p. 11) [measurement]. A one-minute interval separates rounds (GLORY 5.04.A, p. 11) [measurement]. The film's round is the first of five scheduled rounds. It is scheduled for 180 s, and the knockout ends it at 167.6 s of film time [inference from the commission's anchors]. A drawn title match leaves the defending champion as champion (GLORY 5.06.E.5.b.2, p. 15) [observation]. That rule is not used here.

### Bells and timekeeping {#bells}

The timekeeper times rounds and intervals by clock or stopwatch. "By sounding the start of the Round gong, the Timekeeper signals the beginning and end of the Rounds for the Referee" (GLORY 5.01.F, p. 9) [observation]. Ten seconds before the end of an interval the timekeeper signals, and the referee calls "seconds out" (GLORY 5.01.F, p. 9) [observation]. Neither applies before round one.

GLORY names no warning ten seconds before the end of a round. Ontario's timekeeper sounds a wooden clapper ten seconds before the end of each round (ONT 7, timekeeper). BIBA's timekeeper bangs a gavel twice and raises his left arm at 2 min 50 s (BIBA p. 2) [observation]. If round one starts at film second 0, its ten-second mark would fall at 170 s, after the knockout at 167.6 s, so no warning sounds [inference]. The brief fixes where the opening bell falls.

When a knockout ends the bout, "the Timekeeper will sound the gong/bell indicating the end of the Match" (GLORY 5.05.B.5, p. 12) [observation]. The rule gives no delay. The commission's bell at 170.1 s comes 2.5 s after the signal at 167.6 s [measurement from the commission]. That fits a timekeeper reacting to the referee's signal [inference]. BIBA has the timekeeper note the time of the stoppage and report it to the supervisor (BIBA p. 2) [observation].

## Start of the first round {#round-start}

GLORY's only command that starts action is "Fight": "The Referee begins or resumes the Match with the command 'fight' only once the Athletes are separated an equal distance from the Referee" (GLORY 5.05.A.1, p. 12) [observation]. GLORY holds a rules meeting before every event that every athlete and at least one cornerman attend (GLORY 2.04, p. 4) [observation]. It is silent on in-ring instructions and on a glove touch.

Other bodies place brief instructions and a touch before the first bell:

- ABC: "General instructions in the middle of the ring should be brief and to the point". The referee reminds both men to obey his commands and protect themselves, and ensures they "touch gloves and return to their corners" (ABC-REF25 p. 4; ABC-REF22 p. 4) [observation]. The referee stands in the centre before the bell and keeps both men in their corners until it sounds (ABC-REF25 p. 4; ABC-REF22 p. 5) [observation].
- Ontario: the referee calls the men together for final instructions, each with his lead second. "The contestants, after receiving instructions, may touch gloves and return to their corners" (ONT 6) [observation].
- BIBA: after the introductions, the referee calls both men to the centre and tells them "to touch gloves", sends them back to their corners and signals the timekeeper. The timekeeper announces "Round One" and rings the bell (BIBA pp. 1–2) [observation].
- WAKO: the command SHAKE HANDS orders the men "to touch the gloves and show respect to each other" at the beginning of the fight. FIGHT then starts it (WAKO-REF 3.4.4, p. 8) [observation].

The commission wants a real glove touch that both men step into, followed by the first exchange without a reset. Under the boxing practice above, a touch is followed by a return to the corners and then the bell, which is a reset [inference]. GLORY regulates neither form. A touch in the centre after "Fight" breaks no GLORY rule: no 5.07 foul names it [inference]. The sequence that fits both GLORY and the commission is: brief instructions in the centre (optional), the men return to their corners, the bell, the referee's "Fight", the step-in and glove touch, then the exchange [choice]. The referee steps back off the line as the men close (see the referee study, [positioning](../officiating/referee.md#positioning)).

## Attire and equipment {#equipment}

### Gloves {#gloves}

- "Boxing gloves of equal weight provided exclusively by GLORY" (GLORY 4.03.A, p. 6) [observation].
- Weight: "8 ounces (for weight classes under 65 kg) or 10 ounces for all other weight classes" (GLORY 4.03.B, p. 6). Both men wear 10 oz [measurement]. ABC-KB names 8 oz and 10 oz as the professional standard (ABC-KB 17) [observation].
- "In single-Match Matches, the color of the gloves should be the same as the color of the Athlete's corner" (GLORY 4.03.A, p. 6) [observation].
- The gloves are taped so that no laces or loose ends show, and stamped by GLORY or the regulator (GLORY 4.03.A, p. 6). They are put on before an inspector, who tapes and initials them (GLORY 4.03.C, p. 6) [observation].
- Hand wraps are soft bandages. Tape over the knuckles is at most 1 mm thick (3 strips), and the bandages are taped up to 2 cm under the knuckles (GLORY 4.02.A–B, p. 6) [measurement].

### Corner colours {#corner-colours}

GLORY's ring has "a white and black post for the Athletes with two neutral corners in between" (GLORY 4.06.D, p. 7) [observation]. Its scoring examples name the "White corner" and the "Black corner" (GLORY App. 2 C–D, appendix pp. 2–3) [observation]. Gloves match the corner (GLORY 4.03.A). So one man wears white gloves from the white corner and the other black gloves from the black corner [inference].

Departure from PKG: `KickboxingRing.Corner` names the athletes' corners `"red"` and `"blue"`. This production keeps the package's corner geometry and reads `"red"` and `"blue"` as the white and black corners. Which name maps to which colour is the brief's choice [choice]. WAKO's red and blue corners with white neutral corners (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 2.1, p. 121) are amateur practice and are not adopted [observation; choice].

### Trunks and the bare torso {#trunks}

- "Athletes must wear kickboxing trunks that stop above the knee". Leggings may be worn under them if they do not extend below the knee. "The upper body of male Athletes must remain uncovered" (GLORY 4.01.B, p. 5) [observation].
- Sponsors on trunks need GLORY's written consent. Writing, tattoos, logos or sponsors on the bare skin are "not permitted" (GLORY 4.01.C, p. 5) [observation].
- No jewellery or piercings (GLORY 4.01.F). Hair is secured (GLORY 4.01.H, p. 5) [observation].

### Feet, shins and protective gear {#protective-gear}

- "Shoes, or any padding on feet, are not permitted" (GLORY 4.01.D, p. 5). Ankle socks "with soft, basic padding" are allowed (GLORY 4.01.E, p. 5) [observation]. A taped ankle must be covered by an ankle stocking (GLORY 4.02.D, p. 6) [observation].
- **No shin guards.** "Any other protective gear is prohibited without the prior, written consent of GLORY or the Regulator" (GLORY 4.01.E, p. 5), and "Athletes may not wear any other protective or decorative attire" (GLORY 4.01.G, p. 5) [observation]. Shins and insteps are bare [inference]. WAKO K1 Style requires head protection and shin guards (WAKO-RULES ch. 10 art. 2.1, p. 151), which marks it as amateur and not this bout's standard [observation].
- **Mouthguard**: "All Athletes must wear a mouthguard during the Match" (GLORY 4.01.E, p. 5) [observation]. Each corner brings "one extra mouthguard" (GLORY 4.05.D, p. 7) [observation]. Spitting out the mouthguard is stalling (GLORY 5.07.C.7) and, when deliberate, unsportsmanlike (GLORY 5.07.D.7, p. 17) [observation].
- **Groin guard**: "Male Athletes must wear groin protection during the Match" (GLORY 4.01.E, p. 5) [observation].
- No grease or water on the body, and no excessive grease on the face (GLORY 4.04, p. 7) [observation].

### Corner teams and corner items {#corner-teams}

- Each athlete may designate up to three credentialed cornermen, each over 18 (GLORY 1.04.A, p. 2) [measurement]. Cornermen wear clean, sportsmanlike sportswear, with no denim (GLORY 4.01.J, p. 6). They wear matching team sportswear (GLORY App. 3 B, appendix p. 1) [observation].
- The corner brings an empty bucket, sealed ice, a clear water bottle, one extra mouthguard and a towel (GLORY 4.05, p. 7) [observation].
- During the match the cornermen sit in their designated seats and stay seated. Excessive coaching and pounding on the mat or apron are prohibited, and so is "entering the Ring at any time during the Match (even during a Ring Doctor consultation)" (GLORY 5.03.B, p. 10) [observation]. Only the chief cornerman may give care inside the ropes, and only between rounds (GLORY 5.03.D, p. 10) [observation].

### Neutral corners {#neutral-corners}

The two neutral corners lie between the athletes' white and black corners, on the other diagonal (GLORY 4.06.D, p. 7) [observation]. After a knockdown the standing athlete goes to "the farthest removed neutral corner" (GLORY 5.05.B.2, p. 12). Before an official warning or penalty, the opponent goes to "the farthest neutral corner" (GLORY 5.08.A.2–3, p. 18) [observation]. PKG `KnockdownCount.corner` already chooses the neutral corner farther from the fallen fighter. This study adds nothing to it.

## The ring {#ring}

PKG `KickboxingRing` gives GLORY rings as 6.1 to 7.3 m inside the ropes, with the size left to the production. The current GLORY text narrows this to two sizes: the ring floor is "Small size 6.40 x 6.40 square meters or Large size 7.00 x 7.00 square meters, unless otherwise determined by GLORY" (GLORY 4.06.B, p. 7) [measurement]. Both lie inside the package's range, so the package needs no change. The brief picks 6.40 m or 7.00 m [choice]. The rule does not say whether "Ring floor" means the area inside the ropes or the whole platform. This study reads it as the fighting area inside the ropes, the package's measure [inference].

Other ring facts GLORY gives (GLORY 4.06.A–D, p. 7):

- The ring is square and surrounded by ropes linked by vertical cords or bands [observation].
- The floor is covered by canvas "that should not be slippery". It is raised not less than 90 cm and not more than 122 cm above the ground [measurement].
- The ropes are wrapped in soft material and are 3–5 cm thick. There are four, at 40, 80, 120 and 150 cm above the floor. Two pieces of strong cloth per side hold them at an even spacing [measurement].
- The corner posts are white and black for the athletes, with two neutral corners [observation].

GLORY is silent on corner padding and access steps:

- Padding: the referee confirms that "the canvas, ring ropes, post tops, turnbuckles, and corner posts are suitably padded and secured". The lowest rope is "a little looser to cushion a falling boxer's head" (ABC-REF25 p. 2; ABC-REF22 p. 3) [observation]. WAKO's corner posts are "well padded" (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 2.1, p. 121) [observation]. All four corners carry padded covers [inference]. The two athletes' corners are white and black, and the neutral corners' pad colour is the brief's choice [choice].
- Steps: WAKO's ring has three sets of steps. Two are at the athletes' corners, and one at a neutral corner is "for the use of the Central referee and official doctor" (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 2.1, p. 122) [observation]. Ontario seats the ringside doctor "in close proximity of the neutral steps" (ONT 3) [observation]. In this film the doctor enters by the steps at a neutral corner [inference].

## Legal techniques and targets {#targets}

PKG `KickboxingRules` already holds GLORY 5.07's bans on elbows, throws and sweeps, back-of-head, back and groin targets, linear kicks to the legs, the one-knee clinch, and attacks on a downed opponent. Its foul regions are `headBack`, `nape`, `upperBack`, `lowerBack`, `kidneyLeft`, `kidneyRight` and `groin` [observation: PKG]. This section records only what the production adds.

### The finishing kick's target {#finish-target}

The commission places the landing "below and just behind the ear lobe", on the side of the head and neck, struck by a kick aimed at the side of the head. It is not the back of the head.

- GLORY lists the legitimate kicks as "front kicks, low kicks inside and outside the leg, middle kicks, high kicks, sidekicks, back kicks, ax kicks, spinning kicks, jumping kicks" to a legal target, struck "with the foot or lower leg" (GLORY 5.06.A.2, p. 13) [observation]. The Brazilian (question-mark) kick is a high round kick delivered after a body-kick chamber, so it falls among "high kicks" [inference].
- GLORY bans "All techniques targeting the back of the head or back or the torso" (GLORY 5.07.A.2, p. 16) and techniques targeting the groin (A.3). No GLORY rule names the side of the head, the jaw or the side of the neck as a foul [observation]. GLORY therefore treats the landing point as legal [inference]. In the package's regions it is `neckLeft`, `jawLeft` or `earLeft`, none of which is in `FOUL_REGIONS` [observation: PKG].
- Cross-check with ABC-KB. Permissible targets include "front and sides of the head" (ABC-KB 12). The foul region "back of the head or the spine" runs "spreading from ear to ear in the back of the head, running down the entire rear portion of the neck" (ABC-KB 11.D) [observation]. Ontario likewise allows "the front and sides of the head" and fouls "striking the back of the head or the spine" (ONT 9, 11) [observation]. A contact on the side of the neck just below the ear lobe lies forward of ABC's ear-to-ear line, but close to it [inference].
- Cross-check with WAKO K1 Style, an amateur ruleset. It prohibits attacking "the throat, kidneys, back, groin, neck, back of the head and top of the shoulders" (WAKO-RULES ch. 10 art. 5, p. 152). Its legal head target is "front, forehead and side" (art. 3, p. 151) [observation]. Under WAKO the neck part of the contact would be a foul. GLORY does not adopt that ban [inference].

The production must keep the visible contact forward of the line running down from behind the ear, with the instep and lower shin wrapping onto the side of the head and upper neck from the side. A contact that reads as the rear of the neck would be a foul under GLORY 5.07.A.2 as the package reads it (`nape`), and under ABC 11.D [inference; choice].

### Additions to the package's rules {#technique-additions}

- Punches strike "with the padded part of the glove". A spinning back fist counts "only if executed with the padded, back of the glove" (GLORY 5.06.A.1, p. 13) [observation]. Hitting with an open glove, the palm side or the thumb is a foul (GLORY 5.07.A.8, p. 16) [observation].
- A caught leg may be held only "to immediately execute a single legal strike that may be accompanied by a single step in any direction", and then released (GLORY 5.07.A.12, p. 17) [observation].
- Pushing "of any kind as an effort to off-balance or down an opponent" is a foul (GLORY 5.07.A.10, p. 16) [observation]. So is pulling the opponent forward with both arms on the lower back (A.13, p. 17) [observation].
- Prohibited conduct includes techniques after "Break" or "Stop", techniques on an opponent outside the ring or tied up in the ropes, pushing an opponent out of the ring or back over the ropes, and holding the ropes for any reason (GLORY 5.07.B.2–5, p. 17) [observation].
- Stalling includes "continuously pulling up the front leg or stretching out the front arm without carrying out a particular technique", passivity and deliberate falling (GLORY 5.07.C, p. 17) [observation].
- Unsportsmanlike conduct includes "spitting, cursing, talking, making rude sounds or obscene gestures" (GLORY 5.07.D.1, p. 17) [observation]. This supports the commission's fighters, who do not speak [inference].
- Athletes may not approach the crowd or incite it (GLORY 5.02.D, p. 10) [observation]. The winner's celebration therefore stays inside the ring, not up on the ropes towards the seats [inference].

### Fouls and their consequences {#foul-consequences}

The referee chooses the consequence by the foul's nature, severity, repetition and intent (GLORY 5.08.B, p. 18) [observation]:

| Consequence | Rule and procedure | Referee's gesture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caution | For a minor, accidental or unintentional foul. "It may occur without stopping the action" (GLORY 5.08.A.1, p. 18) | None given |
| Official warning | For a repeated, more significant or intentional foul. The referee stops the action, sends the opponent to the farthest neutral corner and indicates the warning to the athlete and the judges (GLORY 5.08.A.2, p. 18) | "right arm up and pointer finger extended" (GLORY 5.08.A.2) |
| Penalty (point) | For an intentional and severe foul, or any foul after an official warning. Same stop and neutral-corner procedure (GLORY 5.08.A.3, p. 18) | "right arm raised and swinging downward in a counterclockwise motion until it is facing downward" (GLORY 5.08.A.3) |
| Disqualification | For an intentional, flagrant or severe foul, any foul after two penalty points, or a cornerman entering the ring during the match other than in an interval (GLORY 5.08.A.4, p. 18) | None given |

The athletes' overview adds that after three fouls of any kind a point is deducted automatically (GLORY App. 3 D, appendix p. 2) [observation].

### Scoring, for reference {#scoring}

Five judges, or three if GLORY approves, score each round on a 10-point-must system. The criteria in order are knockdowns, damage, clean strikes and aggressiveness (GLORY 5.06.C–D, pp. 13–14) [observation]. GLORY uses open scoring, with the judges' scores made public after each round (GLORY 5.06.B, p. 13) [observation]. A knockout ends the bout before round one is scored, so no score appears in the film [inference].

## The clinch {#clinch}

GLORY makes holding a foul: "Grabbing, clinching or holding for any reason ... other than to immediately attack with a single legal knee strike; after the legal knee strike the Athlete must disengage the clinch immediately" (GLORY 5.07.A.11, pp. 16–17) [observation]. The athletes' overview says simply "Clinching is not permitted" (GLORY App. 3 D, appendix p. 2) [observation]. PKG already enforces the single knee.

What this study adds is how the clinch ends:

- In the lawful case, the man who took the hold releases it himself immediately after his one knee. No command is needed [inference from 5.07.A.11].
- If either man holds on, the referee uses "Break". The athletes "must take a step backwards. The Referee steps in between both Athletes. As soon as he steps back the Athletes resume the fight without the instruction 'fight'" (GLORY 5.05.A.2, p. 12) [observation]. A technique thrown after "Break" is a foul (GLORY 5.07.B.2, p. 17) [observation].
- A hold that continues, or holding to rest, is a foul. It draws a caution, or a warning with the stop-and-neutral-corner procedure (GLORY 5.08.A) [inference].

GLORY's clinch is shorter than other bodies'. ABC-KB and Ontario allow a clinch with knees for up to five seconds while the knee attack stays "consistent and effective" (ABC-KB 11.T; ONT 10) [measurement]. WAKO K1 Style allows "Clinching (active, for 5 seconds only)" and one knee per hold (WAKO-RULES ch. 10 arts. 4.1, 4.3, pp. 151–152) [measurement]. Under GLORY the hold lasts only as long as one knee takes [observation]. The duration of a single knee belongs to the screenplay's technique study.

## Knockdown and count {#knockdown}

### Definition {#down-definition}

An athlete is "Down" for an eight count in two cases (GLORY 5.05.B.1, p. 12) [observation]:

- a. As the result of a damaging attack, "any part of an Athlete's body (other than their feet) touch the floor or would have touched the floor if not for the ropes or holding the opponent".
- b. As the result of a damaging attack, the athlete "appears to be in a weakened state and may be in danger of injury, but is still standing (known as 'Standing Eight Count')".

GLORY keeps the standing eight count. ABC-KB has "NO Standing Eight (8) Count" (ABC-KB 7), and Ontario forbids it (ONT 17) [observation]. The film uses only case a.

### Procedure {#down-procedure}

- The referee "will indicate this by the Down call and gesture" (GLORY 5.05.B.2, p. 12) [observation]. GLORY does not describe the gesture. Ontario's referee calls "down" and points to the ring floor (ONT 17, knockdown procedure) [observation].
- "The Referee starts the Down count after he has sent the opponent to the farthest removed neutral corner." The opponent waits there until the referee lets him leave. If he leaves early, "the counting stops and only starts again once the opponent has returned to his neutral corner" (GLORY 5.05.B.2, p. 12) [observation].
- "Referee will not begin the count until the opponent is in the neutral corner facing the center of the Ring" (GLORY App. 3 D, appendix p. 2) [observation].
- "During the Down count it is not permitted for any of the Cornermen to approach their Athletes in any way" (GLORY 5.05.B.2). While the referee counts, cornermen "may not throw in the towel" (GLORY 5.05.B.7, p. 13) [observation].

### The mandatory eight {#mandatory-eight}

"The Referee is obliged to continue counting until the eighth count and then assess whether the Athlete is capable of resuming the fight in a responsible manner. If that is the case, the Referee gives the command Fight" (GLORY 5.05.B.3, p. 12) [observation]. At eight he may check "the Athlete's eyes and balance". If he concludes the athlete cannot continue, "the Referee follows up with the nine and ten count", and the result is a technical knockout (GLORY 5.05.B.6, p. 13) [observation]. PKG `KnockdownCount.READINESS` is 8. This study adds nothing to it.

### Ten count and knockout {#ten-count}

- "In the case a Referee counts to ten without any interruption ... the Athlete will lose on the grounds of Knockout. In that case, the Timekeeper will sound the gong/bell indicating the end of the Match" (GLORY 5.05.B.5, p. 12) [observation].
- Knockout: "If an Athlete is struck in such a way that he or she is incapable of resuming the Match within the Referee's ten-count or if the Referee declares the knockout before the count to ten in order to bring in immediate medical assistance" (GLORY 5.06.E.2, p. 14) [observation].
- Cadence: GLORY gives none. Ontario's referee counts "one-second intervals, in a loud clear voice", showing the count "with a motion of their arm and upraised fingers" (ONT 17) [observation]. WAKO's referee counts "from 1 to 10 with a second interval between each number", showing each second with his fingers (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9, p. 135) [measurement: 1 s per number]. BIBA's knockdown timekeeper counts "strictly in accordance with his watch" (BIBA p. 2) [observation]. PKG `KnockdownCount.number` already places each number one second after the last, from the count's start. This study adds nothing.
- B never rises, so the ten is uninterrupted and the result is a knockout (5.05.B.5, 5.06.E.2), not the technical knockout of 5.05.B.6 [inference].

### Three knockdowns {#three-knockdowns}

In a single match or a tournament final, an athlete "knocked down three times in the same Round or a total of four times in the same Match" loses by technical knockout (GLORY 5.06.E.3.c, p. 15) [observation]. A title bout outside a tournament is a single match under this rule [inference]. The film has one knockdown, so the rule is not reached. ABC-KB applies its three-knockdown rule only to knockdowns from strikes or kicks to the head (ABC-KB 6) [observation].

### The bell cannot save him {#no-bell-save}

"If, during the course of the count ... the time of the Round ends, the Timekeeper may not sound the gong/bell before the end of the Round but must wait until the Referee has counted to the eighth count and, once the Referee has given the Fight command, the timekeeper must immediately sound the gong/bell signaling the end of the Round" (GLORY 5.05.B.4, p. 12) [observation]. The rule names no round, so it holds in round one [inference]. ABC-KB 8 ("CANNOT be saved by the bell in any round, including the final round"), ONT 17 and BIBA p. 3 agree [observation]. In the film the count ends at 167.6 s, before the scheduled end of the round, so the question does not arise [inference].

Tensions:

1. **The delay before the count (about 5–6 s).** Under GLORY it is required. The count begins only once the opponent stands in the far neutral corner facing the centre (5.05.B.2; App. 3 D). The time is set by the walk. The ring is 6.40 m or 7.00 m inside the ropes, so a neutral corner lies 4.5–5.0 m from the centre along the half-diagonal [measurement from GLORY 4.06.B, arithmetic]. If the fighters stand near the centre, A walks about 4–5 m; if they are off-centre towards the near neutral corner, about 6 m. Allowing about 1 s for the referee's call and gesture, A must cover that ground in roughly 4.5–5.5 s: an average of 0.8–1.3 m/s, an unhurried walk plus a turn [inference]. ABC tells the referee to send the standing man and "DO NOT RUSH!" (ABC-REF22 p. 6) [observation]. Other bodies would count differently:
    - Boxing commissions run a knockdown timekeeper who starts counting the moment the man is down. The referee "picks up" that running count after sending the opponent away (ONT 17; ABC-REF25 p. 5; ABC-REF22 p. 6; BIBA p. 2). Under that practice the referee's first spoken number would be about "Five" or "Six", and "Ten" would land about 10 s after the fall, near 161 s rather than 167 s [inference].
    - WAKO's referee "must immediately start counting out the seconds", suspending the count only if the opponent refuses the corner (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9, p. 135) [observation]. That too would end the count near 161 s [inference].

    The commission's "One" at 158 follows GLORY, not these. The film shows no knockdown timekeeper and no count from ringside [choice].
2. **An early stop for medical help.** GLORY lets the referee declare the knockout before ten "in order to bring in immediate medical assistance" (5.06.E.2). ABC tells referees that if a boxer "goes down hard" with "eyes ... closed" or "muscles twitching/spasms", they "may want to stop the count and immediately end the contest" (ABC-REF25 p. 7; ABC-REF22 p. 7) [observation]. A fallen man who reads as motionless and unconscious invites that early stop, against the commission's full real-time count [inference]. The full count is right when B is visibly conscious and trying, but failing, to rise: a turned head, a hand pressing the canvas, a knee drawn up that will not bear him. That staging also keeps within "no asserted injury mechanism" [choice].
3. **Readiness at eight.** PKG assesses at eight. B is still down at eight, so no "Fight" check is made and the count runs on to ten (5.05.B.3, 5.05.B.5) [inference]. If the brief has B half-rise, a man still touching the floor with anything but his feet is still down (5.05.B.1.a) [inference].
4. **The knockout gesture.** GLORY names none. ABC's referee "shall wave their arms above their head to signal the bout is over" (ABC-REF25 p. 6; ABC-REF22 p. 7). Ontario's waves both arms above the head (ONT 17). WAKO's KO/TKO signal, photographed, is the forearms crossed above the head (WAKO-REF App. 11.4, p. 48) [observation]. The referee study records the choice.

## After the knockout {#after-knockout}

### Who enters the ring and when {#ring-entry}

- **The ring doctor** must be present at the ring throughout matches (GLORY 5.01.E.1, p. 8). The referee may ask the doctor to examine an injury. "Only the Ring Doctor may enter the Ring during an examination" (GLORY 5.01.E.4, p. 8) [observation]. The referee's "immediate medical assistance" clause anticipates the doctor being called at the knockout (GLORY 5.06.E.2) [inference]. ABC's referee "shall rule the bout over and immediately signal for the doctor" (ABC-REF25 p. 6) [observation].
- **Who stays with an unconscious man**: "only the Central referee and the doctor in charge will be allowed to stay in the ring, unless the doctor needs extra help" (WAKO-RULES ch. 7 art. 9.1, pp. 135–136) [observation]. Ontario admits no one but the contestants, the referee and the ringside medical practitioner during a round (ONT 4) [observation].
- **The corner teams** may not enter the ring at any time during the match, even for a doctor's consultation (GLORY 5.03.B, p. 10). A cornerman who enters during the match, other than in an interval, causes his athlete's disqualification (GLORY 5.08.A.4, p. 18) [observation]. GLORY does not say when corners may enter after a knockout. ABC has the referee stay with a hurt boxer "until the doctor or his cornermen come into the ring" (ABC-REF22 p. 7) [observation]. The commission keeps both corners outside the ropes until 180 s. That fits GLORY 5.03.B and WAKO 9.1 while the doctor is working [inference; choice].
- **The winner** stays in the neutral corner until the referee permits him to leave (GLORY 5.05.B.2). After that, nothing in GLORY confines him, but the referee keeps him away from the fallen man (see the referee study, [care](../officiating/referee.md#care)) [inference].

### Result announcement {#announcement}

- The ring announcer "is responsible for announcing the Match, Warnings and the scores" (GLORY 5.01.H, p. 9). The judge supervisor authenticates the scores and informs the announcer of the result (GLORY 5.01.D, p. 8) [observation].
- ABC asks the referee to "Help the ring announcer declare the fight result accurately" (ABC-REF25 p. 15) [observation]. WAKO's referee announces the winner on the chief referee's command (WAKO-REF 3.4.3, p. 8) [observation].
- No source sets when the announcement follows a knockout. With the doctor attending B, a formal announcement with the winner's hand raised normally waits until the fallen man is cared for [inference]. The commission makes the referee's the only spoken words and admits no commentary. So the announcer is not heard before 180 s, and no hand-raising is shown [choice].

### The match-ending bell {#match-bell}

The timekeeper sounds the gong or bell when the knockout ends the match (GLORY 5.05.B.5, p. 12) [observation]. At 170.1 s, 2.5 s after the signal [measurement from the commission].

### After the bout {#post-bout-medical}

An athlete who loses by knockout must have a clinical assessment, and GLORY may require a CT scan of the brain (GLORY 2.03.D, pp. 3–4) [observation]. This happens after the film and is not shown.

## Rule-outcome table {#outcomes}

The outcome classes the brief uses, with the conditions that produce each under GLORY. Each row combines the attacker's action, the target and the defender's response.

| Outcome class | Conditions | Rule | What follows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal landed strike | A punch with the padded glove, a kick with the foot or lower leg, or a knee. It reaches a target not in PKG `FOUL_REGIONS` (a push kick only the body), "with full power, from the correct distance and body position", and the defender's guard, check or evasion does not intercept it | GLORY 5.06.A, p. 13; PKG | Play continues with no stop. It counts towards damage and clean strikes (5.06.C.2–3). Before 150 s it costs breath, guard and balance but no knockdown (commission) |
| Blocked strike | A legal strike that meets the defender's glove, forearm, shoulder or raised shin instead of the target, or is slipped or parried | GLORY 5.06.A requires landing on a legal target. WAKO: a technique "partially deviated or blocked, or that simply touches, brushes or pushes ... will not be scored" (WAKO-RULES ch. 10 art. 4, p. 151) | Play continues. Not scored [inference] |
| Foul | Any prohibited technique, target or conduct (GLORY 5.07; PKG), whether intended or accidental | GLORY 5.07, 5.08, pp. 16–18 | A caution without stopping, or "Stop", the opponent to the farthest neutral corner, and a warning or penalty gesture ([consequences](#foul-consequences)) |
| Clinch with one knee, then break | One man grabs the head or neck to throw one immediate knee, then releases | GLORY 5.07.A.11; PKG | If he releases at once, play continues with no command. If either holds on, "Break": both step back, the referee steps between, and they resume as he steps back (5.05.A.2) |
| Knockdown | A legal, damaging strike lands and any part of the man's body other than his feet touches the canvas, or would have but for the ropes | GLORY 5.05.B.1.a, p. 12 | "Down" call and gesture. The opponent goes to the farthest neutral corner and faces the centre. The count runs from then, with a mandatory eight (5.05.B.2–3) |
| Knockout | A knockdown after which the man cannot rise and resume before the uninterrupted ten, or the referee stops the count early for medical help | GLORY 5.05.B.5; 5.06.E.2, pp. 12–14 | Knockout signal. The match-ending bell. The doctor is called; the corners stay out ([after the knockout](#after-knockout)) |

A strike landing on an opponent who is already down is a foul, not a knockout (GLORY 5.07.B.1; PKG) [observation]. The commission's "no downed attack" makes this row unused.
