# The knockout and its aftermath

This study records how a fighter knocked out by a blow to the head falls, what a downed fighter does during a ten-count, what the rules allow in the ring after a knockout, and how a winner may conduct himself. The installed `knockdown` action performs one fall, a buckle to a sitting landing that rolls back to lie supine; the commission needs a fall onto the right side, the downed fighter's attempt to rise and the aftermath, which the vocabulary does not carry. The film asserts no injury mechanism, so this study records only what can be seen and heard. Each observation carries its kind as the [index](../index.md#observation-kinds) defines it; source ids are in the [index](../index.md#sources).

## The fall {#fall}

- [reported] In 153 knockouts filmed in boxing and UFC compilations, 134 (87.6%) were free falls: "an active person instantaneously transformed into an inanimate object, followed by a free fall in gravity accompanied by flaccidity"; in 133 of them "no lower limb movement was evident after the hit and during the free fall" (`HUANG-2023`, abstract, p. 4, Table 2).
- [reported] "The top of the head of the stricken fighter sank significantly … within a time span of 100 msec"; the fighters "were on the ground, typically within one second or less"; free-fall times work out at "~450 msec for the buttocks (at 1 meter) and ~640 msec for the head (at two meters)" (`HUANG-2023`, pp. 2–4).
- [reported] "The falls took all forms (front, back, or sideways)"; the authors did not count them by direction (`HUANG-2023`, p. 4). No source located measures whether a fighter falls toward or away from the blow.
- [reported] Fighters braced the fall with one or both arms in 26 of 134 free falls (19.4%), and in 2 of 26 boxing falls (7.7%) (`HUANG-2023`, pp. 4, 6). "The upper and lower limbs of the fallen fighter may assume 'unnatural' positions" (p. 7).
- [reported] In 23 of 35 knockout videos (66%) one arm extended and the other flexed at impact and held "for several seconds as flaccidity gradually returns", for 2 to 16 s (mean 6.3 ± 4.0 s), "regardless of the side of impact" (`HOSSEINI-2009`, Results, Table 2, Fig. 2); a later sample found it in 47% (`BEITCHMAN-2022`).
- [reported] A fighter dropped by a body shot falls differently: "the falls typically took 2-3 seconds and were never free falls … the stricken fighter almost always made steps" (`HUANG-2023`, pp. 4–5).

Law [inference, with the side as the commission's choice]: a fighter knocked out by a head kick does not step or catch himself. His head drops first, his knees and hips fold under him and his body falls in under a second; he lands on the canvas on one side, here his right, shoulder and hip first, the head arriving last, and his limbs come to rest where they fall rather than in a chosen pose. His arms may hold a stiff, uneven position for a few seconds. The fall is one continuous collapse, shown from the hit to rest without a cut, as the commission requires.

## On the canvas during the count {#downed-fighter}

- [reported] "While still on the ground … the stricken fighter typically appears to be dazed, unfocused mentally, and weak or unable to move physically" (`HUANG-2023`, p. 3).
- [reported] Downed fighters were seen with "left arm extension during attempt to sit up" (`HOSSEINI-2009`, Table 1); "righting movement" occurred in 40 of 102 televised concussions and "gait unsteadiness" in 42 (`MCCRORY-2000`, abstract).
- [reported] WAKO's ready test at eight: "the kickboxer is on his feet, in fighting stand with his arms raised in fighting position. Kickboxer must be stable and balanced, with clear vision"; otherwise the referee "will continue to count until '10', the fight will be finished and a KO declared" (`WAKO-RULES`, Ch. 7 Art. 9, p. 135). Ontario: a knockout if the fighter "fails to rise to a standing and ready position before the referee reaches the count of 10" (`ONTARIO-KB`, s. 19).

Law [inference]: the downed fighter lies where he fell, breathing, slow to move. Partway through the count he tries to right himself, pushing up on an arm, and the arm does not hold him; he does not reach his feet, so he is not ready at eight and the count runs to ten.

## After the knockout {#aftermath}

- [reported] "If a Kickboxer remains unconscious, only the Central referee and the doctor in charge will be allowed to stay in the ring"; the referee, "after calling the doctor, will keep the kickboxer lying on the floor and will not allow him to get up until the arrival of the official doctor", and "will not otherwise intervene (remove the tooth guard or rotate the unconscious kickboxer to the lateral position)" (`WAKO-RULES`, Ch. 7 Art. 9.1, pp. 135–136).
- [reported] GLORY: "Only the Ring Doctor may enter the Ring during an examination" (`GLORY`, Ring Doctor). "Only the Referee and the Ringside Doctor can remain in the ring" when a boxer is unconscious (`IBA-TCR-2026`, 30.7.9, p. 62).
- [reported] The referee stays with the hurt fighter until the physician arrives (`ABC-2025` p. 7; see the [referee study](../officiating/referee.md#after-knockout)).
- [reported] No rule located says where the winner waits after the knockout is declared; the result is announced later by the ring announcer (`GLORY` §5.01.H, `WAKO-REFEREE` Art. 3.4.4), and the winner's hand is raised only after the announcement (`IBA-TCR-2026`, 39.8.8, p. 68).

Law [choice, from the rules above]: at the wave-off the referee kneels at the fallen man's side and keeps him lying still with a hand on his shoulder, and the ring doctor climbs through the ropes at the nearest neutral corner and kneels at the fallen man's head. Nobody else enters while the film runs, and the result is not announced before it ends.

## The winner's conduct {#celebration}

- [reported] Unsportsmanlike conduct under GLORY includes "making rude sounds or obscene gestures", "insulting the … opponent or his team" and "undisciplined conduct of any kind" (`GLORY` §5.07.D, p. 17); WAKO lists "unappropriated gesticulations towards opponent" (`WAKO-RULES`, Ch. 1 Art. 3.7.2, p. 53). No rule names celebration as such.
- [reported] After a head-kick knockout in ONE kickboxing, the winner "performed a few victory laps around the Circle, cheering wildly", and the loser's camp complained that the referee had to tell him "to stop cheering" while their fighter lay unconscious (`SCMP-2021`, one camp's account, reported second-hand).

Law [choice]: the winner's joy is released but turned away from the fallen man: gloves up, turning to the crowd, quick steps that never come toward the fallen man or past the referee, and no gesture at him.

## Measurable targets {#targets}

Each target is measured on the composed film [inference, from the sources above]:

- From the kick's contact to the fallen man's hip on the canvas takes at most one second, and his feet take no step in between.
- From the fall to the wave-off his feet never carry his weight.
- His attempt to rise lifts his trunk no higher than his supporting arm can push it, and ends with him back on the canvas before "Eight".
