## Checkpoint 1: what the research found {#after-research}

Each finding changes what may honestly be written, and each now has an owner.

1. A GLORY title match is five rounds, so a scheduled three-round match whose first round is three minutes is a non-title match ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#round)). Owner: [bout rules](../../../body/brief/world/bout.md#the-match). Failure: a screenplay calling the bout a title fight contradicts the round count.
2. GLORY counts only after the standing man is in the farthest neutral corner facing the centre, and a count paused at eight to check the fallen man yields a technical knockout, not the knockout the commission names ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown)). Owner: [bout rules](../../../body/brief/world/bout.md#count). Failure: A still walking at 157, or a pause at "Eight".
3. A real referee waves off a count when the fallen man presents as needing immediate medical help, so a full ten in real time needs a B who reads as conscious but unable to rise ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#knockdown)). Owner: [Roux](../../../body/brief/cast/roux.md#down). Failure: B lying limp while the count runs.
4. The finish lands legally only on the side of the jaw and neck; the rear of the neck is the back-of-head zone in the stricter regulations ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#legal-targets)). Owner: [bout rules](../../../body/brief/world/bout.md#finish-target). Failure: a contact written on the nape, or a kick that reads as arriving from behind Roux.
5. GLORY sounds no ten-second warning inside a round, and the round never reaches its last ten seconds before the match ends ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#round)). Owner: [bell](../../../body/brief/props/bell.md#no-warning). Failure: a clapper or warning heard near 170.
6. The referee does not remove a knocked-out man's mouthguard, roll him or lift him; that is the doctor's work ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#knockdown-procedure)). Owner: [referee](../../../body/brief/cast/referee.md#care). Failure: the referee pulling out Roux's mouthguard, rolling him or sitting him up.
7. GLORY's corners are white and black and the gloves match them ([rules study](../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md#ring-and-equipment)). With no name ever spoken, colour is how the audience tells the men apart. Owners: [gloves](../../../body/brief/props/gloves.md#corner-colours), [work record](../../../body/brief/world/work.md#audience). Failure: a beat that tells the men apart by name, or colours that change or repeat on both men.
8. A defensive habit forms only when the real strike is respected, and a trap keeps the start the opponent has learned while changing the end ([exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#tell)). Owner: [Roux](../../../body/brief/cast/roux.md#the-tell). Failure: the drop shown before Vos's body kicks have reached Roux's left side at least three times, or a finish whose start differs from the body kick's.
9. The motion vocabulary's knockdown ends supine, while the commission's fall ends on B's right side ([Brazilian kick study](../../../research/kickboxing/brazilian-kick.md#fall)). Owner: [Roux](../../../body/brief/cast/roux.md#the-fall) holds the end state, and the [Brazilian kick study](../../../research/kickboxing/brazilian-kick.md#fall) its law. Failure: a fall written onto Roux's back, or one that cuts away before he is at rest. Composing it is a later checkpoint's matter and outside the current scope.
10. The commission requires the kick and fall uncut and the count in real time, and leaves the rest of the coverage open. The coverage study proposes one real-time take from one ringside camera that keeps the referee beyond the fighters ([coverage study](../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md#laws)), and the brief adopts that proposal as its choice, because one continuous view keeps the continuous scene in one place and lets the audience watch the tell build without an edit choosing for them. Owner: [camera](../../../body/brief/cinematography/camera.md). Failure: a screenplay that cuts, or a view that loses a head, a foot or the target in an exchange.
11. Corner teams call out words during real rounds, and a crowd's shouts carry words; both would break the commission's single speaker ([sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#crowd)). Owners: [ringside](../../../body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#corner-teams), [crowd](../../../body/brief/crowds/arena-crowd.md#voice). Failure: an intelligible shout from a corner or the crowd.
12. No GLORY source has the referee raise the winner's hand, and the announcer, who declares results, would be a second speaker ([referee study](../../../research/officiating/referee.md#winner)). Owner: [bout rules](../../../body/brief/world/bout.md#result). Failure: the announcer heard, or the referee raising Vos's hand, before 180.

Not found by the research and left unavailable in the studies: footage of any kind, a measured study of the Brazilian kick, the bell's strike count, measured contact sounds and crowd levels. Their values stay choices of the studies or of the screenplay.

## Checkpoint 2: what the brief needed {#after-brief}

Writing the brief backward from the planned film exposed these decisions, each now settled at its owner, with the failure a screenplay would otherwise commit:

1. Where each man starts the touch and which ring landmark each corner is, so the screenplay can address places: [ring](../../../body/brief/sets/ring.md#corners). Failure: a beat that places a man "on the ropes" without saying which side, so the camera's side cannot follow.
2. Which neutral corner A goes to depends on where B falls, so the ring names both neutral corners and the screenplay picks: [ring](../../../body/brief/sets/ring.md#neutral-corners). Failure: Vos sent to the neutral corner nearer Roux.
3. The doctor enters only at the referee's call, which needs a doctor with a place and conduct: [ring doctor](../../../body/brief/cast/ring-doctor.md). Failure: a doctor appearing from nowhere, or entering during the count.
4. The judges, timekeeper and announcer may be in the background and need behaviour: [ringside](../../../body/brief/crowds/ringside.md). Failure: an official in frame with nothing to do, or a corner team inside the ropes before 180.
5. Faceless men read through posture, head and trunk turns and breath, so each man's readable state needs a vocabulary of body signs: [work record](../../../body/brief/world/work.md#faceless-polygon-men) and each fighter's state facet. Failure: a beat that needs a facial expression, a look of the eyes or blood.
6. The text's English needs one spelling and number convention: [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#language). Failure: mixed spelling, or a count written as numerals in speech.
7. Roux studied Vos's footage, so what Vos has shown on record had to be settled before Roux's knowledge could hold: the Brazilian kick is drilled but unrecorded. Owners: [Vos](../../../body/brief/cast/vos.md#repertoire), [Roux](../../../body/brief/cast/roux.md#knowledge). Failure: a Roux who has seen the kick and still drops his arm for it.
8. Roux's trademark long combinations had to agree with the level's short chains: they are a few bursts among two- and three-strike chains. Owner: [Roux](../../../body/brief/cast/roux.md#repertoire). Failure: a Roux who throws only long combinations, beyond the exchanges study's targets.
9. The body kicks that build the tell and the proof's blow to the head each need a contact sound of their own: shin on body and glove on head. Owners: [ring sound](../../../body/brief/sound/ring-sound.md#contacts), [sound study](../../../research/sound/ring-sound.md#contacts). Failure: the tell's key contacts heard as some other class.
10. The proof over the dropped left arm can only land with a weapon from Vos's right side, because both men are orthodox and face each other: his right hand. Owner: [Roux](../../../body/brief/cast/roux.md#the-tell), from the [exchanges study](../../../research/kickboxing/exchanges.md#tell). Failure: a left hook written as the proof, which would land on Roux's covered right side.
11. The commission names clinch breaks, so the round needs a clinch whose grip outlasts its knee, which GLORY counts as a foul; the brief decides that the referee, who alone imposes consequences, answers it with the break alone. The exchanges study's clinch choice and pause target, and the referee study's break target, were brought into line with it. Owner: [bout rules](../../../body/brief/world/bout.md#one-knee-clinch). Failure: a round with no break, or a break followed by a caution or a stopped clock.
12. The commission's "supported victory steps" needed a reading at the instruction's owner. Owner: [commission](../../../instructions/commission.md#ending). Failure: Vos lifted or held up by someone in the ring before 180.
