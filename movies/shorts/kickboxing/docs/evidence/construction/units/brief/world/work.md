# docs/body/brief/world/work.md#audience-and-experience

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet promises who the film is for and what it must leave them with, and both are this film's. The audience is "adult viewers of fight films, including viewers who do not follow kickboxing", limited to what the picture, the ring's sounds and the referee's words give them, and asked to "track two fighters, one referee and one habit, and no more" (`#audience`). The central experience is the round's own payoff: the body kick lands, "Visser's left arm begins to drop each time Benlloch's right knee rises", "A feint proves the drop", and the knockout "arrives as a payoff the audience has watched being made, not as a surprise" (`#central-experience`). How and when each piece of information reaches the audience is named as left to the screenplay, not as an unfinished decision here.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

The record settles the audience's means and limits, "They tell the two equal men apart by colour alone", and the one rule the film may not lean on: "It never relies on a rule the picture does not show being kept, such as scoring" (`#audience`). It states the experience's failure conditions, which a reviewer can hold a screenplay to: "if the audience never sees the habit form, if the finish reads as luck, or if Visser reads as a victim" (`#central-experience`).

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#direction

Its subsection `#central-experience` realizes the chosen direction, following both men with the audience let in on the plan before Visser, and cites the commission's direction as its source, of which it says "the brief's other records serve it".

# docs/body/brief/world/work.md#the-film

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet promises the film, its running time and its frame, and holds all three: the one-line film `kickboxing` with its premise and end (`#film-kickboxing`), "180.000 seconds as one continuous scene, from the round's opening bell to the end of its aftermath" (`#running-time`), and "landscape 16:9 at 1280 × 720" (`#the-frame`). Its own choice is where the clock starts: "The opening bell sounds at 0.0 seconds, so film time and round time are the same clock."

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

The running time and frame agree with the project's delivery profile, which each subsection cites. The record also settles what the frame demands of the camera: two men at kicking distance "fit it head to toe only when the camera looks across the line between them", which is why the camera's side view is owned in `cinematography/camera.md#side-view`.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#one-round

Its subsection `#running-time` holds the film to the one round: "with nothing outside that round", and `#film-kickboxing` has the knockout at 150 seconds and the aftermath "until 180".

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#phases

Its subsection `#running-time` carries the twelve fifteen-second phases as anchors of the running time: "Its twelve scenes each cover fifteen of them, so each scene's start is an anchor too". The scene format itself stays with the screenplay.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#frame

Its subsection `#the-frame` records the user's frame, "landscape 16:9 at 1280 × 720, the user's choice", and the reason the camera keeps the side view in it.

# docs/body/brief/world/work.md#representation

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet promises the adopted representation and what it cannot show. `#faceless-polygon-men` defines it, "a body of simple shaded forms with no face, eyes, mouth, hair or skin detail", and gives this film's translation of inner states into bodily signs, from attention ("the turn of the head and trunk toward its object") to care ("kneeling, a hand laid on another body, a lowered head"). `#what-it-cannot-show` applies the limits to this film's decisive moments: the finish "reads from where the shin and instep meet the side of the head and neck and from how the head moves, not from anatomy".

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

The record settles the limit and the rule that follows from it: "A screenplay line that needs a facial expression, a look of the eyes, a tear, sweat, blood or the shape of a mouth has nothing to show it with. It must be rewritten as one of the signs above." It also names what does read, "a glove and its colour, trunks and their colour, the shin and the bare foot", and that no story point rests on a mouthguard, wraps, tape or text.

# docs/body/brief/world/work.md#scope

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet promises what the commission leaves out, and `#left-out` lists it in one place: nothing outside the round, and within it no commentary, music, replay, slow motion, caption, clock, word from a fighter, corner or crowd, scoring, announcement or medical explanation, ending "No one bleeds."

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

Each exclusion carries the reason or source that bounds it: no scoring "because the knockout ends the bout before round one is scored", and no announcement or raised hand "before 180", because "These would come later, once the fallen man has been cared for", citing the rules study's `#announcement`.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#no-injury-mechanism

Its subsection `#left-out` keeps the ruling: "The film shows where the kick lands and that Visser cannot rise by ten, and never says why in medical terms".

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#sound

Its subsection `#left-out` excludes what the sound ruling forbids: "no commentary, music score, announcer's voice, replay, slow motion or other time compression".

# docs/body/brief/world/work.md#tone

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet promises the tone, and `#sober-and-close` turns the user's "sober and close" into this film's terms for violence, camera, sound and the knockout: "The knockout is decisive but not cruel. The film stays with the fallen man and the referee's care as much as with the winner's joy."

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

The record states how the tone fails, so a screenplay can be held to it: "It fails as highlight style: slow or repeated impacts, triumphant music, a camera that dives at the fallen man, or a winner who taunts." Its camera and sound consequences point to their owners, `cinematography/camera.md#look` and `sound/ring-sound.md#palette`.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#tone

Its subsection `#sober-and-close` cites and realizes the user's tone: "two professionals doing hard, skilled work in front of a loud room, with respect at the touch and care at the end".
