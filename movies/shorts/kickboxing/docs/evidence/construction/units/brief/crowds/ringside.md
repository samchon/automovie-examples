# docs/body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#audience

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet settles the hall's audience: full, unseen in a blacked-out hall, heard as one indistinct voice whose rises are tied to the round's moments.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The crowd's behaviour follows this round: it "roars at the knockdown and holds that roar through the count beneath the referee's voice", and it never carries a word, keeping the referee's the only speech.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

It settles whether the audience is seen, what it sounds like and when it rises, so no beat must invent a crowd reaction.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#record-and-currency

The audience's behaviour is owned here; the blackout cites the fight-coverage study. That the audience is never seen is also stated in `world/work.md#coarse-forms`, which owns the representation, in agreement with this record. The mix is owned by the sound record.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#sound

# docs/body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#officials

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet settles every official and corner the film places outside the ropes: the timekeeper and bell, the ring doctor, the judges and announcer, and both corner teams, with what each does while present. The rules study's ring inspectors, judge supervisor and scorekeeper are neither placed nor excluded: they are never seen or heard, and the film uses none of them.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

Their conduct follows the referee study's rules for this contest: the doctor "enters the ring only when the referee calls him" and then "climbs through the ropes at once", the corners do not approach during the count, and Kessler's corner stays out while Vermeer is down, while the winner, already in the ring, keeps away from him.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

It settles who is present and where, whether each is seen or heard, how the bell is struck at the start and the end, the doctor's dress, that he reaches Vermeer within seconds of the call and before the film ends, what he does there and that he works in silence, and why the announcer is silent, so every presence has behaviour.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#record-and-currency

The bell's second is linked to the anchors and the corner names to the ring record; the doctor's entry is settled here and its exact beat is the screenplay's.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#sound

Adopts the start and match-ending bells.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#count

Adopts the match-ending bell after the knockout.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#ending

Keeps everyone but the referee and the doctor away from B, so the wide ending holds the winner, the fallen man and the officials attending him.
