# docs/body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#officials

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet promises the officials who sit at ringside, and settles each with a place and a behaviour: the table "at the centre of one side of the ring, at the foot of the apron" with the timekeeper, judge supervisor, scorekeeper and announcer (`#officials-table`); the timekeeper's two bell strikes (`#timekeeper`); three judges who "watch and write, and do nothing else" (`#judges`); and the doctor, "a man in his forties in a dark blue medical tunic and trousers", and the paramedic at the steps, with what the doctor visibly does at Visser (`#doctor`). When the doctor reaches Visser is left to the screenplay.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

The record settles the limits a screenplay keeps: the announcer "only watches, because his work falls after the film"; the timekeeper "does not count the knockdown, and nobody at ringside does"; the paramedic comes in only if called, "which does not happen before 180"; and the doctor "enters only when the referee calls him", by the steps, and "Only he may enter during his examination." What he does there stays within the representation and the commission: "He does nothing the picture would read as a diagnosis or a treatment." The officials' roles cite the rules study's `#officials` and `#scoring`.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#venue

The timekeeper subsection realizes "a timekeeper rings the bell from ringside", striking it "once at 0.0" and "from 170.1".

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#count

The timekeeper subsection holds the match-ending bell at the count ruling's 170.1, after the knockout signal.

# docs/body/brief/crowds/ringside.md#corner-teams

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

The facet promises the corner teams, and settles them: three cornermen each "in matching team sportswear: white for Benlloch, black for Visser", a ring inspector with each team, coaching "by gesture" during the round (`#corners-seated`), and what each corner does after the knockout (`#corners-after`).

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

The record settles the limits: "They say no words at any point in the film", "Neither team enters the ring before 180", and "Nobody holds Benlloch up or steps in to join him." The inspector's role cites the rules study's `#officials`, and the corners' voices the coverage study's `#corner-voices`.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#venue

The corner subsections realize "the corner teams stay outside the ropes until the film ends": "Neither team enters the ring before 180".

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#ending

The after-the-knockout subsection keeps the reading of "supported victory steps" as A's own: "Nobody holds Benlloch up or steps in to join him".

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#language

The seated-corners subsection keeps the referee's words the only speech: "no voice of theirs is heard".
