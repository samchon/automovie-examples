# docs/body/brief/world/contest.md#knockdown

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

It delivers the three decisions the finish rests on: that nobody goes down before it, how the count runs, and what the knockout and its aftermath allow.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

The count is this contest's: started only when the standing man faces the centre in the farther neutral corner, "One" a second later, straight on to ten without a check, the crossed-arm wave-off and the bell.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

It settles the baseline condition the commission fixes (no early knockdown), with its consequence for head kicks (blocked or glancing, never clean, as the outcome table's consequences give), and the procedure the anchors at 157, 158 to 167 and 167.6 hang on, leaving those seconds to the screenplay.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#record-and-currency

It owns the procedure once; the referee record owns how he performs it, and the knockout study its sources.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#contacts-before-finish

"Every blow before the Brazilian kick, landed or glancing, costs breath, guard or balance and never puts a man on the canvas."

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#count

"Begins the count only when that fighter stands there facing the centre … one second after it begins he calls 'One', then one number a second through 'Ten'"; "an uninterrupted ten is a knockout … the timekeeper's bell then ends the match."
