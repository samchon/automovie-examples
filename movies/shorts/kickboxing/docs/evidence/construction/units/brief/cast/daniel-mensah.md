## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

[Referee](../../../../../body/brief/cast/daniel-mensah.md#referee) identifies “a fictional 46-year-old man from Ghana”, distinguishes him through a charcoal shirt and black trousers, gloves and shoes, and limits his speech to “concise English commands”. [Working official](../../../../../body/brief/cast/daniel-mensah.md#working-official) gives his authority and duties without pretending to script the bout: “His exact route, head direction, gestures and words are decided beat by beat in the screenplay.” This fills the delegated identity and the [adult active referee requirement](../../../../../instructions/commission.md#referee) without adding an accreditation claim.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

[The facet](../../../../../body/brief/cast/daniel-mensah.md#identity-and-authority) holds one official's identity, visible distinction, speech role and baseline conduct. “He seeks a side view of contact, gives space to legal work, intervenes when separation needs him and attends the downed fighter after the decision” bounds that conduct. The [current rules-and-procedure study](../../../../../research/kickboxing/round-practice.md#rules-and-procedure) supplies the cited external practice basis. No route, exact command timing, facial performance or measurement is invented in the brief. The actual commands and care remain decisions of the scene.
