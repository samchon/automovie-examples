# docs/body/brief/cinematography/coverage.md#cameras

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

It gives the film's three cameras and what each is for.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

A ringside hard camera at the middle of one side for orientation, an apron handheld on the far apron for the exchanges, and a fixed fighter-height camera in the neutral corner on the hard camera's side, the broadcast roles the study records, with the corner camera's stated departure from the broadcast far corner.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

It settles the cameras every beat record will choose from, citing the fight coverage study's law.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#record-and-currency

The apron and neutral corners it uses are owned by the ring record and cited, and the corner camera's mount on the post above the top rope stays clear of the steps and the ring doctor's way in; the operator's working stretch is owned by his record and cited, so the two fixed cameras and the far apron agree.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#camera

The apron camera's operator moves "to the point nearest to side-on to the line between the fighters as that line turns", the camera reacting to the action; its operator is seen whenever another camera's view takes in his stretch of apron, which the camera operator record owns.

# docs/body/brief/cinematography/coverage.md#framing

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

It states the framing rules for exchanges and for the referee.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

Both men's feet, the struck place and both heads in frame, with the fighters' line close to square to the camera; a closer size only when the other man is out of the action.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

It settles the framing grammar the commission asks for, as a rule each beat's camera keeps, and which camera takes an exchange the apron camera cannot see near side-on: the fixed camera that sees the line nearest square, usually the corner camera.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#record-and-currency

One owner of the framing grammar.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#camera

"Whenever an exchange is live the frame holds both men's feet, the place being struck and both heads."

# docs/body/brief/cinematography/coverage.md#editing

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#scope-preservation

It states how the film cuts, and the commission's three fixed conditions on the finish, the count and the ending.

## @evidence shared/principles/common.md#specificity

Cuts like a live director's that never skip time; the finish uncut from a side where no body hides the leg or the fall; the count whole with both fighters in view; a wide last frame.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#needed-decisions

It settles the cutting style and the commission's conditions. It says that the camera holding the finish is the screenplay's decision; for the count and the ending it states only what the frame must hold, naming no camera, which leaves the screenplay to choose within the fight coverage study's choices.

## @evidence shared/principles/brief.md#record-and-currency

The conditions cite their commission sections rather than restating them as new decisions.

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#fall

"From before Arjan's right knee rises for the last time until Bastien lies still on the canvas, the film does not cut."

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#count

"The count is seen whole and in real time, and the framing keeps Bastien down and Arjan in his neutral corner in view."

## @evidence instructions/commission.md#ending

"The film ends on a wide frame holding Arjan's celebration, Bastien on the canvas and those kneeling with him."
