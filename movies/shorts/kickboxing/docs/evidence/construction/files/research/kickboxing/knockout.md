# docs/research/kickboxing/knockout.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The study gives the law for what the installed `knockdown` action does not perform: a free fall without a step onto one side within a second, the head first and the limbs where they fall, from Huang's 153 measured cases and the posturing studies; the downed fighter's failed righting attempt during the count; who may enter the ring afterwards and what the referee may and may not do; and the winner's conduct under the unsportsmanlike-conduct rules (statements 1, 2 and 6). Its targets (statements 9 and 10) are the fall time, the absence of a step and the height of the failed attempt, drawn from the measured values. The side of the fall is the commission's choice where the source records all forms, and the study states it (statement 11 does not apply otherwise). Statements 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 belong to the exchanges, ring craft and coverage studies. The study asserts no injury mechanism, as the commission requires.

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#traceable-sources

Every observation is marked by kind and names its retained source by id, which the index resolves to its retained copy; most citations add the page, table, rule or part they rest on, and `BEITCHMAN-2022` and `SCMP-2021` are cited by id alone, pointing to the retained copy as a whole (`HUANG-2023` pp. 2–7, Table 2; `HOSSEINI-2009` Results, Tables 1–2; `MCCRORY-2000` abstract; `WAKO-RULES` Ch. 7 Art. 9 and 9.1, pp. 135–136; `IBA-TCR-2026` 30.7.9, 39.8.8; `SCMP-2021`, flagged as one camp's second-hand account). Ranges keep their dispersion (6.3 ± 4.0 s, 7.7 to 19.4%), abstract-only sources say so, and the missing tally of falls by side is recorded as unavailable.
