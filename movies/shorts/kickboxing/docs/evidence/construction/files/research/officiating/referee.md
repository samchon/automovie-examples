# docs/research/officiating/referee.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The study captures a championship referee's practice at the level the brief's referee record and ringside records use, so the record invents none of it. It records his qualification, language and attire (`#attire`), his conduct before the first exchange (`#before-the-round`), his positioning, side view, window, distance and movement (`#positioning`), the clinch break step by step under GLORY with ABC's mechanics (`#clinch-break`), his spoken commands and hand signals with each one's source and kind (`#commands`, `#signals`), the knockdown procedure placed against the commission's anchors (`#knockdown-procedure`), the care of a knocked-out man and what he must not do (`#care`), the markers of an experienced referee against a novice (`#level`), and measurable targets with ranges (`#targets`). Where a source gives no value, such as his distance in metres, the value is marked [choice] with its bounds: "2.0–3.0 m from the nearer fighter, outside a round kick's reach and inside the ropes", bounded by the ring's half-width.

Practice from other bodies states its translation: the ABC manuals "transfer to kickboxing because the ring, the neutral corners, the count and the referee's place inside the ropes are the same", with the stated difference that his distance "must also keep him clear of round kicks"; WAKO's commands and signals carry over but its immediate count does not; Ontario's gestures carry over but its knockdown timekeeper does not.

Its `#scope` names the statements it leaves out, with their reasons:

- Technique laws (1): the referee performs no fighting technique, and his movements are composed from the motion vocabulary.
- Legal actions (2) are in the rules study, and his outcome responses (3) in its outcome table. The referee has no exchange outcomes of his own.
- Exchange chains (4) and his path through each phase of the round (5) belong to the screenplay's own study.
- The crowd's conduct (6): the referee's practice has none to give, and the other officials are in the rules study.
- How the genre is shot (8): the referee's practice has none.
- Rates such as his movements per minute (9, 10) belong to the screenplay's own study.
- Invented action (11): none, because the referee follows real practice.
- The package's `KnockdownCount` and `KickboxingRing` (14, 15) are not restudied.

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#traceable-sources

Every observation names its source by key with a locator, and states its kind in brackets as the reading section defines: "[observation] for what the original's text or photograph states; [measurement] for a value with its unit that the original reports; [inference] for this study's reasoning from cited facts; [choice] for a decision this production makes where the sources leave room". Examples: "(ABC-REF25 p. 4) [observation]", "(WAKO-REF App. 11.4, pp. 47–48) [observation of photographs]", and in the command and signal tables a Source and a Kind column for every row.

Each key names its original in the key table. Every original is listed in `docs/research/index.md#originals-rules-referee` with its creator, URL, retrieval date, saved path under `references/private/`, rights and the locators used.

Uncertain values keep their range, such as the fall-to-count delay of "about 5–6 s" and the walk of "about 4–6 m in about 4.5–5.5 s". Values no source gives are marked: "No source gives a level in decibels: unavailable". Unavailable originals are recorded under `#scope`. The AIBA manual is recorded because "the server refused the secure connection on 2026-11-13". The Masters Boxing Australia manual is recorded because "the server returned a web page in place of the PDF". The study also records that no GLORY referee manual was found.
