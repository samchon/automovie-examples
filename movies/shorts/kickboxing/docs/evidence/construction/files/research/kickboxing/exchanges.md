# docs/research/kickboxing/exchanges.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

This is the screenplay's own study. It holds what the screenplay needs to write each exchange beat at championship level without inventing it, and nothing the other four studies already hold.

- **Each action the screenplay names, and how it is performed (`#vocabulary`).** It maps each fighting, officiating and aftermath action to the installed action and parameters that perform it: punches and punch feints, kicks and kick feints, the redirect, the knee and the plum clinch, every defence with what it answers, footwork and the guard, flinch and stagger, the glove touch, the referee's actions, Benlloch's walk and celebration, and the downed man. `#vocab-lacks` lists the ten actions the vocabulary does not perform, each with where its law is given or that the code composes it, including the cornermen's seated gestures.
- **Technique laws (statement 1), for the three actions with no installed law.** The Brazilian kick has its key poses in Benlloch's body frame through seven phases (`#bq-phases`), its contact surfaces, "the instep and the lower shin", its support and weight (`#bq-support`), its timing against the round kick (`#bq-timing`) and its travel from kicking range (`#bq-travel`). The fall to the right side has five timed phases and the order of contacts (`#fall-phases`). The failed rise has its five signs placed against the count, the rule against stillness, and the conduct after "Stay down." (`#rise-phases`, `#rise-after`). Each records its departure from the nearest installed action.
- **An outcome table (statement 3), `#outcomes`.** Its 18 rows give the intents and timings that yield each outcome, its defence and rule classes, and its visible consequence tied to a brief state record. Every outcome the screenplay cites has a row: the clean and glancing punches, the covered, parried and slipped punches, the counter, the low kick landed and checked, the body kick landed, blocked and checked, the push kick, the feint with and without a reaction, the feint's follow-up, the clinch knee, the tie-up, and the finish through to the knockout. Notes explain why the early kicks land and the late cover fails.
- **Chains and transitions (4)**, with how long each takes in the vocabulary, in `#chains`, `#visser-chains`, `#benlloch-chains`, `#resets` and `#handover`.
- **Spatial strategy and tempo (5, 9, 10)** in `#tempo`. It covers strikes per minute and landed shares from GLORY's CompuStrike counts, exchange length and idle time from the WAKO time-motion study, the high-to-low ratio, how round one differs, distance by range as a stated working choice where no measurement exists, and exchanges per fifteen-second phase. Each target is a range with its method, as in "Strikes thrown, both men: 4–8 per 15 s".
- **Conduct (6)**: the winner's conduct after the knockout (`#celebration`) and the downed man's. The referee, officials, corners and crowd are left to the referee, rules and coverage studies, as `#scope` item 6 says.

Its `#scope` names each statement it leaves to another study, and why: legal actions (2) to the rules study; level markers (7) to the styles study; the genre's shooting (8) to the coverage study. It records that no action is invented (11) and names the finish's closest real analogue. Values from other disciplines state their translation where they are used (12), as in "Translation: WAKO full-contact is amateur, with 2-minute rounds and no low kicks". Package movements and tables are cited by name and not restudied (14), "The vocabulary's laws and tables are cited by name".

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#traceable-sources

Every observation carries one of five kinds in brackets, "[observation]", "[measurement]", "[inference]", "[choice]" or "[commission]", defined in `#reading`. Computed values name their arithmetic, as in "[inference: arithmetic on PKG values]". Sources are cited by key with a locator: web articles by heading or opening words, as `(GLORY-BTN14, para. beginning "'CroCop' landed 36")`, and papers by section, table or page, as "(OUERGUI, Table 2, p. 3539)". The installed vocabulary is cited as PKG with the export's name and is not an original.

The study's key table names 21 originals, and the research index's exchanges section (`docs/research/index.md#originals-exchanges`) lists each with its creator, URL, retrieval date, saved path, rights and locators used. Every saved path exists. Six are permitted copies under `references/`, and fifteen with unconfirmed rights are under the ignored `references/private/`. Each quotation in the study was found in a saved copy, in the installed package's source or in the body it cites.

Uncertain values keep their range, such as "2.2 ± 1.2 s", "about 0.40–0.55 s" and "3.1–6.4 s". Unavailable sources are recorded as unavailable with their reason, in the study's `#scope` and the index:

- Belošević et al., "the server returned HTTP 403";
- the 2017 activity-profile paper known only from a table of contents;
- the Business Insider report;
- the sideways-fall papers known only from search summaries;
- the two papers opened and discarded, with why;
- footage, for which "no video decoder is available on this machine".
