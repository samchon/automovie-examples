# docs/research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The study records how the genre is shot (statement 8): the broadcast camera plan with its tripod main cameras, apron handhelds, neutral-corner and canvas-level cameras, the fiction films' choices, and the shot-length ranges; from them it gives the film's camera law and its knockdown coverage as marked choices. Its targets (statements 9 and 10) are the shot-length band, translated from Bordwell's fiction ranges to live fight cutting with the reason stated (statement 12), feet and heads in frame, the fighters' line near square to the camera, no camera inside the ropes and the uncut finish. Statements 1 to 7 and 11 do not apply: the study describes the camera, not a performed practice, so it has no technique laws, legal actions, outcome table, sequences, spatial strategy of the performers, officials' conduct or level markers. Statements 12 and 13 hold: the 2.7-second action-film figure the earlier production used is not repeated, because no open original supports it.
