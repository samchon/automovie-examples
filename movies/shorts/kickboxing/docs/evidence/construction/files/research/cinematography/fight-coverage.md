# docs/research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The study captures how a ring bout is shot, lit and heard, at the level the brief's camera, sound, crowd, arena and bell records use, so those records keep to real practice without inventing it. It records:

- broadcast camera plans, main-camera height and distance, apron, low and overhead cameras, how both fighters' feet stay in frame, how the referee's occlusion is handled, cutting, screen direction and shot length (`#broadcast`), with the rope geometry worked out for a lens outside GLORY's ropes (`#main-cameras`);
- how Creed, Southpaw and Raging Bull shoot the ring and when fiction cuts (`#fiction`);
- the contrast between sober, close coverage and highlight style, in lenses, framing, motion, slow motion, light, colour and sound (`#sober-coverage`, `#highlight-contrast`);
- arena and ring light, rig height, the dark audience and the grade (`#lighting`);
- ring microphones and what they pick up, the crowd-against-ring mix, the absence of commentary and the voices at ringside (`#ring-sound`);
- the fight crowd's arrangement, sound and knockout reaction, and how dark it reads in picture (`#crowd`);
- the timekeeper's bell, its position, strikes and sound (`#bell`).

Boxing sources stand in for kickboxing with a stated translation, "Kicks change the coverage in four ways" (`#translation`). Its measured targets keep their ranges and sources (`#targets`), such as ring illuminance "1,500–1,900 lx (140–175 fc)" and overhead microphones "2.4–3.2 m above the canvas". The values no source gives are marked unavailable: broadcast shot-change cadence and the ring and crowd sound levels.

Its `#scope` names the statements it leaves out, with their reasons:

- Technique laws (1), legal and illegal actions (2) and an outcome table (3): a camera, lighting or sound style has none. The bout's rules and outcomes are in the rules study.
- The chain of shots across the round (4), the fighters' measured rates and the path of each exchange (5), and distance by phase, action rate and idle time (9, 10) belong to the screenplay's own study.
- The fighters' ring use and tempo at the level of style (5) and their markers of level (7) are in the styles study, which `#scope` links.
- Package exports (14, 15): no installed package exports a camera, lighting, crowd or sound style.

Covered:

- Chains (4) and tempo (5), for coverage only: cutting at resets and long takes.
- Conduct (6) of camera operators, the crowd, the corners as heard and the timekeeper.
- The level marker (7) for a director: over-cutting.
- How the genre is shot (8).
- The invented action's analogue (11): the borrowing from boxing coverage, stated in `#translation`.

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#traceable-sources

Every observation names its source by key with a locator and states its kind in brackets: commission, measurement, observation, inference or choice, each defined in `#reading`. Articles are located by section and paragraph, the podcast by transcript timestamp and the rules by section, as in "(SKY-AJ "Camera positions through the ropes" para. 1) [observation]" and "(TONE, Aaron Glasscock 44:07) [observation]". Imperial values are converted with "the original value kept in brackets".

Each key names its original in the key table. The study points to the tracked index for the retained copy: every original "is listed, with its creator, URL, saved path, rights and the locators used, in the research index's fight-coverage section". That section of `docs/research/index.md` lists the originals behind the keys, with RULES excepted as the production's own rules study, and every saved path in it exists under `references/` or `references/private/`.

Uncertain values keep their range, such as ring illuminance "1,500–1,900 lx (140–175 fc)" and the lighting truss at "10–12 m above the floor". What no source gives is tagged [unavailable] where it is used, such as shot length, sound levels and the crowd's knockout reaction. The sources that could not be retrieved are listed under `#unavailable`, from the theasc.com live pages to the Trilux page and the undecodable footage.
