# docs/research/sound/fight-sound.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The study records how the genre is heard, the sound half of statement 8: who carries a microphone and is heard (the referee, the corners), how contact, footwork and a body on the canvas are picked up and mixed, how the bell reaches the broadcast, and what the crowd does in the mix; from these it gives the film's sound law as a marked choice. Statement 6 is met for the crowd and corners as far as they are heard. Where no source describes contact sounds by surface, the study records the production's choice to distinguish them, which the brief's palette realizes (statement 14). Its targets (statement 9) are the referee's intelligibility over the crowd, a sound for every shown contact and none without one, distinct sounds per contact kind and nothing the arena could not produce, each marked as a choice, because no source measures crowd level or the audibility of the count (statement 10 records that absence). Statements 1 to 5, 7 and 11 do not apply: the study describes the sound of a broadcast, not a performed practice.
