# docs/research/kickboxing/styles.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The study captures the two fighters' practice at championship level as far as the brief's cast records use it, so the records name a style, a habit and a finish without inventing them. It records:

- the Dutch pressure fighter's origin, repertoire, ring-cutting, tempo, costs and weaknesses, each from a coaching text or fight report (`#pressure-fighter`);
- the patient kicker's repertoire, check and counter, angles and feints, answer to pressure, ring use and weaknesses (`#counter-kicker`);
- the markers that separate championship from club level, such as guard discipline, return to stance, economy, defence while attacking, feints, reading the head and trunk, and breathing, with the measured sample of elite K-1 kickboxers (`#level-markers`, `#level-numbers`);
- how a guard tell forms, how it is read in real GLORY and K-1 fights, how a feint tests it, and why an expert defender's anticipation at the rising knee can be betrayed (`#tell`);
- the Brazilian kick's origin, why it beats a guard drawn low, what its chamber shows, its knockouts, and where the coaching sources' landing differs from the commission's (`#brazilian-kick`);
- the visible cost of body kicks, low kicks and round one's work, with measured heart rate and lactate ranges (`#kick-costs`);
- championship fighters' conduct at the touch, in silence, in their exhale sounds, toward their corners and toward the referee (`#conduct`).

Each production choice is marked [choice] and offered to the brief, which takes it up: B's tell as "the disciplined elbow block degraded under pressure", and the commission's "feint that lands" read as a feint whose follow-up lands.

Its `#scope` names the statements it leaves out, with their reasons:

- Technique laws (1), including the Brazilian kick's chamber, turnover and contact law, the outcome table (3), exchange-by-exchange chains (4), measured activity and distances (5) and the remaining targets (9, 10) belong to the screenplay's own study, because no brief record uses them. The brief defers the same items, for example "Its law belongs to the screenplay's own study".
- Legal actions (2) belong to the rules study, and the officials' and crowd's conduct (6) to the rules, referee and coverage studies.
- How the genre is shot (8): the subject has none.

The invented action (11) is B's habit forming on screen within one round. The study names its closest real analogue, Gerges's hands lowering to the middle kick and Brestovac's head kick, and states the departure. Values from Muay Thai, MMA, amateur WAKO and amateur K-1 state their translation where they are used, as the reading section requires ("Each value or practice taken from Muay Thai, MMA, amateur WAKO or amateur K-1 states its translation where it is used"). Single movements are left to the package's `StrikingSkill` (14), and the level and patterns of the practice are studied here (15).

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#traceable-sources

Every observation names its source by key with a locator and states its kind in brackets: observation, measurement, inference or choice, each defined in `#reading`. Web articles are located "by their section heading, or by the opening words of the paragraph where the article has no headings", papers "by page, table or section", and GLORY rules by number and printed page. Examples are "(ATLAS, section "How To Cut Off The Ring", paras. 5–6) [observation]" and "(RYDZIK, Tables 2 and 3, pp. 3–4, and "Physiological Measurements", p. 4) [measurement]". The Croatian report is marked as translated by the study ("translations here are this study's").

Each key names its original in the key table. The study points to the tracked index for the retained copy: "The research index lists every original, with its creator, URL, retrieval date, saved path, rights and the locators used, in its styles section". That section of `docs/research/index.md` lists the originals behind the keys, with PKG excepted as the installed package, and every saved path in it exists under `references/` or `references/private/`.

Measured values keep their spread and range, such as "178.2 ± 5.5 bpm (172–190 bpm)", with their method and group. Unavailable sources are recorded: the LowKick MMA Brazilian-kick page and the CBS results "unavailable", and the four primary anticipation papers "not retrieved, cited only through MQB19". The study also records a disagreement between two GLORY pages on the Brestovac event.
