# docs/research/kickboxing/bout-patterns.md

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#depicted-practice

The study gives the screenplay the round's measurable shape: the measured duration of exchanges and of the footwork between them, the interval between exchanges, pauses and the number of exchanges in a first round, with their standard deviations and method (Ouergui et al. 2014), translated into an expected range for the 150 seconds before the finish with the translation stated. It records how first rounds differ from later ones, the Dutch style's punch-to-low-kick pattern and its defensive variants, spatial strategy against pressure and counter-fighters and on the ropes, how a hurt defender comes to answer a technique early, and why the rising knee is answered before the kick's height can be read. Fighters' conduct, silent and respectful, is drawn from rule and practice.

Technique laws, legal actions and the outcome table are owned by the [technique](../../../../../research/kickboxing/techniques.md) and [rules](../../../../../research/kickboxing/rules.md) studies; officials' conduct by the [referee study](../../../../../research/officiating/referee.md); shooting practice by the [fight-coverage study](../../../../../research/cinematography/fight-coverage.md). No action is invented here.

## @evidence shared/principles/research.md#traceable-sources

Every statement names its source and locator, tables 2 to 4 and the discussion of Ouergui et al., named passages and examples of the Bloody Elbow and Evolve texts, AIBA sections and GLORY and WAKO articles, and is marked *measured*, *coaching*, *rule*, *cross-check* or *inference*. The amateur-to-professional translation keeps its range and is labelled an approximation; missing professional measurements and footage are recorded as unavailable.
