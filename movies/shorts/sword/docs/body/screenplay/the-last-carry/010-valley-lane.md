# The last carry

## The lane to the burial ground {#lane}

- Standing state. A morning funeral in a lane on the Aosta side of the [pass](../../brief/world/pass-road.md#two-approaches). A [woman in a cream kerchief](../../brief/cast/gate-woman.md#appearance) stands by the open burial gate a short walk ahead. Facing the gate, the [ochre-hooded front bearer](../../brief/cast/front-bearer-left.md#appearance) has the left pole and the [slate-capped front bearer](../../brief/cast/front-bearer-right.md#appearance) the right. At the rear, [Denis](../../brief/cast/denis.md#appearance) carries the left pole on his right shoulder and [Colin](../../brief/cast/colin.md#appearance) the right pole on his left. The cousins are hired swords from different branches of the dead man's family. Their [shrouded uncle](../../brief/cast/shrouded-uncle.md#appearance) lies under off-white linen, his feet towards the rear. [His widow](../../brief/cast/widow.md#appearance), their aunt, walks just behind Denis. A [mourner in a russet hood](../../brief/cast/relief-bearer.md#appearance) walks outside Colin. The file keeps pace ahead of them.
- Opening view and sound. From the rear of the moving bier its cloth brushes the widow's skirt. The woman beside the gate gives a quiet “Lord, have mercy.” The moving file takes it up.
- Performance time. One continuous minute in authored seconds. [The nephews' promise](../../../instructions/commission.md#fourth) · [Bearers and bier](../../../research/history/road-and-households.md#bearers)

EXT. VALLEY LANE - MORNING

### The hem {#hem}

With each step the hem creeps a little farther back from the shape of a shoe. The widow pulls it down again. Denis shortens his step to give her time.

DENIS  
We'll carry Uncle all the way, Aunt.

Colin meets her eye across the cloth.

COLIN  
Both of us.

The shoe disappears. The widow lets her hand rest there until the cloth lifts under her palm.

- Time: 0–13 s.
- Intent and change: Denis makes room for his aunt's hand and gives her his word. Colin adds his. She walks with her hand over her husband's foot.
- Camera: Travel with the hem and the widow. Open the view to take in both men as Colin speaks.

### What the dead man owed {#pay}

Denis fishes a loose corner of the cloth out of his sword belt. A purse hangs there. Stitched into the flap is a little hare with one crooked ear. Colin touches the ear.

COLIN  
My father's purse. Uncle owed him a winter's pay.

DENIS  
Uncle paid me. Ask him yourself.

He hears what he has said. His eyes go to the covered shoe. Beside Colin the man in russet puts his left shoulder under the free length. The wood settles. Colin steps clear. At the sound of Colin's sword coming free, Denis starts to duck out. His corner dips. Both front bearers stop. The ochre hood turns back as the widow gets both palms under Denis's pole.

WIDOW  
Wait till I've got him.

Denis stays bent under the wood. She gets her right shoulder against it behind his. It lifts off his coat. He turns and sees her there before he steps away.

- Time: 13–32 s.
- Intent and change: Colin asks for his father's due. Denis sees the shoe too late to call back his answer. The mourner has Colin's corner first. Denis feels the wood lift before he leaves his aunt under his own corner.
- Camera: Let the belt pass close. See the shoulder behind each cousin before he leaves the frame of the bier.
- Sound: Colin's steel comes out underneath the petition. The praying falters when the bier dips.

### Those who go on {#go}

Denis's sword is out too. Across the tail of the procession the two bare lengths of steel come together with a sound far too big for the lane. A mourner squeezes past them to catch up.

The widow takes one level step. The ochre hood and the slate cap bob together. All four are walking again. The hem rides up. The widow starts to lift one hand to it. The pole rolls on her shoulder and her hand goes straight back to the wood. Behind them Denis loses his footing in a rut. Colin catches a fistful of his coat. For an instant they're holding each other up. The little purse swings against Colin's hand. He lets go as if it burned him. The blades flash between them again.

WOMAN AT THE GATE (O.S.)  
Lord, have mercy.

The file answers her as it goes in. Denis can still hear it between blows. Then all that reaches him is the last syllable of mercy. Over Colin's shoulder the ochre hood passes through the gate beside the taller man's slate cap. The widow goes after them with both hands on the wood.

- Time: 32–48 s.
- Intent and change: The widow gets the procession moving. The cousins keep up their quarrel through their uncle's last passage into the burial ground.
- Camera: Cut to a full view from the lane. Hold the cousins near and the gate beyond. The procession gets smaller within the fixed frame.
- Sound: The funeral response recedes through two bursts of steel. The men still have breath to spend on each other.

### The mark {#mark}

Denis waits for the next answer from up the lane. All he can hear is Colin breathing. His sword sinks. Colin looks behind him at last.

The last mourner slips inside. The woman in the cream kerchief draws the gate to. Through the bars, close enough to see the worn leather, the uncle's brown toe shows under the hem. The widow carries it past the gap.

Denis lifts his hand to the hollow in his shoulder where the pole rode. There's a splinter sticking out of his coat. Colin plucks it away. They stand with it between them.

- Time: 48–60 s.
- Intent and change: From the lane the men see that their aunt has carried their uncle inside. The audience gets one last look at the leather toe as she bears it away. Colin picks a sliver off his cousin.
- Camera: Leave the cousins in the lane for the close view between the gate bars at bier height. Return to Denis's shoulder, close enough for the green weave to show as Colin lifts the pale sliver clear.
- Sound: The gate settles. Neither man speaks.
