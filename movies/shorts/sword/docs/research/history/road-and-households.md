# The road and its households

## A delivery by more than one pair of hands {#delivery}

The heading on folio 19v identifies rye bread taken from Entremont to the house on the pass in 1419. In entry 1304 Colet Tyot’s son receives bread from François de Versonay at Orsières and carries it up. In 1305 another supplier at Orsières hands bread to the same son. Entry 1306 names two people who carry a consignment, the son and the servant Johannetus Lombar. A delivery could depend on somebody being there to take the weight from somebody else. The records give quantities in measures. The screenplay invents the number of loaves a person can rescue in the scree. [Accounts, printed p. 73](https://bib.rero.ch/api/records/rew18-ydn26/files/I-N-268_1973_04_00.pdf/content)

The congregation places the hospice in Savoy until 1475. The house above the carriers therefore falls under Savoyard jurisdiction at the anthology's date. The two bends above Orsières and the gates in the lower valley are scene geography. [Congregation archive](https://gsbernard.ch/60/630.php) · [Commission](../../instructions/commission.md#period)

## Walking in armour {#armoured-travel}

Askew, Formenti and Minetti measured the energy cost of walking and running on a treadmill in four men accustomed to wearing replica armour. At comparable speeds both gaits cost the men more energy in armour. On the road Perrin gains a few strides, stops on firm dirt and is still breathing hard when he walks on. The slope, length of his pause and his response to a blow are particular to this scene. [Authors’ study, methods §2 and results §3](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2011.0816)

## Things a rider brings into a yard {#riders}

Fiore dei Liberi's illustrated fencing manuscript dates to around 1410. The Getty identifies Niccolò III d'Este as its patron and describes pen drawings of armoured knights and horses. The Italian court commission supplies an earlier visual comparison for the armoured rider at the muster and Perrin on the road. The stories take place in Savoy about fifty years later. The hospice accounts establish the local transport of bread, and Perrin's encounter on the climb is fictional. [Getty catalogue, Ms. Ludwig XV 13](https://www.getty.edu/cona/CONAFullSubject.aspx?subid=700002326)

The Milanese helmet at the Met dates to about 1460. Its steel sides come down beside the face around a T-shaped opening. The catalogue describes the original bright polish. In the wager the opening keeps the visitor's mouth visible when everyone waits to hear what he will say. A German or Tyrolean saddle of about 1450 includes both wood and leather. That gives the two invented saddles a body that knocks on a bench and a seat that can split. The museum identifies its own example as a decorated parade saddle. Its carvings and particular shape belong to that object. [Helmet 42.50.15](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/27960) · [Saddle 04.3.250](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/21991)

The Louvre identifies Pisanello's drawing of a harnessed horse's head and neck as a study from life and draws attention in its title to the hanging bridle. The slack curve of a rein can register visibly against a horse's neck. The mounted film uses that small change when the running stops. The call, the turn and the moment the foal stands still are individual events written for these animals. [Drawing INV 2359 recto](https://collections.louvre.fr/ark:/53355/cl020003146)

## Arms at the muster {#muster}

In Rogier van der Weyden's panel of about 1432–35, the lance stretches almost the height of the picture beside the armoured rider. The mounted film uses that difference in scale in the two long shadows running along the paddock wall. The Met dates mace 14.25.1320 to the late fifteenth century and tentatively identifies it as French. The catalogue places its style in Western Europe from about mid-century. Openings in the short steel head let bits of sky show through. That feature makes the pair identifiable from their first appearance at the saddles to their different resting places. [National Gallery of Art, 1966.1.1](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/50662-saint-george-and-dragon) · [Metropolitan Museum of Art, 14.25.1320](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/33836)

The clerk issuing household arms, the painted stripes, saddle loops and promise about the mare are staging choices for this muster. The miss, the glance off Luc's armour and the horses' reactions are events written for this encounter. [The stolen mare](../../instructions/commission.md#mounted)

## A household carries its dead {#bearers}

The Morgan's catalogue of a Paris Book of Hours made in 1430–35 identifies five men carrying a draped bier in the margin of folio 120r. Clergy walk ahead of them. That separation in the image gives the film room to let the front of a procession keep moving while something goes wrong at the back. The widow taking a pole, the nephews' paid service, their family debt and the words heard in the lane belong to this funeral. The miniature supplies a visual comparison from France. [MS M.359, fol. 120r](https://ica.themorgan.org/manuscript/page/236/76807)
