# The Better Seat

The finished picture establishes two saddles, a marked ring and each saddle's final owner. It spends much of the minute holding poses or repositioning figures. Two brief blade crossings give way to a shared shuffle and a kneel. The deciding step is shown, but the movement and reactions give a first-time viewer little reason to connect it to the result. The yard also contains visible and measurable body intersections. The commission asked for an engrossing one-minute wager, an earnest encounter, a reversal Carlo owns and a generous ending the household witnesses. ## Earlier approval and evidence limits

The director initially withheld picture acceptance when the independent reviewer read Martin's kneel as a loss. The final retake redrew 309 of 1,440 source frames to bring forward the boot, open palm and fingers. Its fresh reviewer could locate those objects and movements and trace ownership, yet still could not tell what the crossing decided, what caused the kneel or why the fingers mattered. The signed verdict accepted the picture by treating placement and order as sufficient and leaning on unheard dialogue or later playback for the rule and payoff. It also acknowledged ambiguity in the coat catch and handshake. That decision left the long visually idle spans, the weak action and reaction, and contact defects in the delivered picture. The encoded technical pass establishes file integrity and measured alignment. The director's record explicitly left listening and uninterrupted audiovisual acceptance open.
